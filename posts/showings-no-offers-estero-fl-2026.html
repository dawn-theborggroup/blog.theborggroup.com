Lots of showings, no offers in Estero FL? What buyers are telling you 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 27, 2026·8 min read

Why are buyers touring my Estero home but not making offers?

Because something between the photos and the purchase isn’t holding up. Steady showings tell you the price and the listing are good enough to get buyers in the door. No offers tell you that what buyers see in person, learn afterward, or compare it against is changing their mind. In Estero right now, it usually comes down to condition, an insurance or roof question that comes up after the showing, or a similar home that looked like a better deal. Finding out which one it is for your home takes information that only a listing agent can see.

“We’ve had all these showings — why hasn’t anyone made an offer?” is one of the most frustrating places a seller can be. It’s also one of the most common seller questions we see online right now, from Reddit threads to national surveys of agents.

The good news is that a home with showings and no offers is closer to selling than a home with no showings at all. Buyers are already interested. Something specific is stopping them, and it can be found.

What the Estero market looks like right now

Here are the newest numbers we could open today.

  • Estero single-family: our Southwest Florida MLS market ticker (updated November 26, 2026) shows 292 active listings, 86 pending, and 87 sales in the last 45 days. The median sale price is $725,000, and homes are selling at 96.7% of list price. By our arithmetic, that’s about 5 months of supply.
  • Estero condos: 273 active, 52 pending, 59 sold in 45 days, a $338,900 median, and 96.6% of list — closer to 7 months of supply by the same math.
  • Lee County overall: Realtor.com’s October 2026 inventory data (published through the St. Louis Fed’s FRED database) counted 9,635 active listings in Lee County, and 2,684 of them had taken a price cut. That’s more than one in four.
  • Time on market: the same data shows a median of 80 days on market in Lee County in October, down from 91 a year earlier.
  • Closed sales: Florida Realtors reported 1,115 single-family sales in Lee County in October 2026, at a $389,900 median, flat from a year ago.
  • Rates: Freddie Mac’s 30-year fixed average was 7.21% on November 25, 2026, down from 7.28% the week before.

Two things matter here. Estero buyers are active — 86 single-family homes are under contract heading into the holidays. And buyers have choices. With roughly five months of supply and more than a quarter of Lee County listings already reduced, a buyer who tours your home has probably toured three or four others that week too.

What sellers can expect: where buyers drop off

Every showing is a test your home has already half passed — the buyer liked it enough to book the appointment. From there, buyers usually drop off at one of three points.

1. During the showing

The home in person doesn’t match the price or the photos. In a national HomeLight survey of 715 top agents (fielded August–September 2026), 47% said price was the most likely reason a home gets showings but no offers, and 19% pointed to condition. Those two are usually linked: buyers aren’t saying the home is bad, they’re saying it’s not worth this price in this condition.

The hard part is that buyers almost never tell the seller what bothered them. You’re too close to your own home to see it the way a stranger does, and the people who could tell you — the buyers and their agents — are talking to their agent, not to you.

Strong photos are still essential, and we covered why in why professional photos and video matter when selling. But when showings are steady and offers aren’t, the photos are doing their job. The gap is in the room.

2. After the showing

This is the Southwest Florida step that sellers from other states often don’t see coming. A buyer can love your home on Saturday, call an insurance agent on Monday, and quietly move on. Roof age, older systems, the flood zone, and HOA or CDD costs can all turn a good showing into a no — and the seller never hears why.

Which of those is in play depends on your specific home, and so does the fix. We go deeper on the insurance side in can your buyer actually insure your home?

3. In comparison

Sometimes nothing is wrong with your home. Another one simply looked like more for the money. In Estero, that competition often includes new construction, where builders may be offering rate buydowns or closing-cost help that a resale seller isn’t.

The clearest answer to “why didn’t they buy mine?” is what those same buyers bought instead. That trail runs through the MLS and through agent-to-agent conversations, which is why it’s so hard to see from the outside.

Why the showing feedback alone won’t tell you

Showing feedback helps, but it’s filtered. Buyers’ agents work for their buyers, and the National Association of Realtors notes that many keep feedback brief and general to protect their client’s negotiating position — especially if the buyer might still make an offer.

So “they loved it, just not the right fit” is often polite code. The real reason usually comes out in a direct conversation between agents, not on a feedback form.

Timing considerations heading into season

Timing works in your favor right now if you act on what buyers are telling you. Many seasonal buyers arrive after the holidays, and January through April is when the most buyers are touring in person. A change made in early December reaches the serious buyers already here. The same change made in mid-January lands alongside a wave of fresh listings.

If you’re thinking about a short break instead, we walked through that decision in should you take your home off the market for the holidays?

Common seller mistakes when showings don’t turn into offers

  • Waiting for “the right buyer.” Fifteen showings with no offer usually means fifteen right buyers said no to something specific.
  • Cutting the price in small steps. Several small reductions can signal pressure without ever reaching the buyers you lost. Our post on days on market versus price reductions explains why.
  • Fixing the wrong thing. New paint won’t solve an insurance problem, and a price cut won’t fix a smell. Guessing is expensive.
  • Pricing off the active listings. Active listings show what sellers hope for. Pending and closed sales show what buyers actually paid — see our 2026 pricing strategy guide.

How The Borg Group markets listings that need a second look

We’ve been selling homes in Southwest Florida since 2005, and we treat “lots of showings, no offers” as one of the most useful problems a listing can have. Buyers have already told you they’re interested. Our job is finding out what changed their mind.

What would we look at first if you called us about an Estero home with showings but no offers?

We’d start with the parts of the picture a seller can’t see from the outside: the full showing history, who toured, and what those buyers did next. When buyers who walked through your home went under contract somewhere else, that tells us exactly what the market chose over yours.

We’d also talk to the agents who brought those buyers. Agent to agent, the conversation is usually far more candid than the feedback form, and it often surfaces the one thing nobody wanted to put in writing.

Then we’d look at your home the way an Estero buyer’s insurance agent and inspector will, before a buyer ever has to ask. In this market, that’s often where a good showing turns into a quiet no.

The fix that comes out of that is usually specific rather than dramatic. When it’s handled well, the next round of showings feels different, because buyers aren’t leaving with unanswered questions. That’s the part we take care of for you.

If your home is listed with another brokerage right now, your listing agent is the right first call — these are good questions to talk through with them. If your listing has expired, or you’re planning to list in the new year, we’re happy to talk.

The bottom line

Lots of showings and no offers isn’t bad luck. It’s buyers telling you something specific — just not telling you directly. Finding out what it is, and fixing the right thing, is where the right agent makes the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I have showings but no offers on my house?

Usually because something buyers see in person, learn after the showing, or compare your home against is changing their minds. In a national HomeLight survey of 715 top agents in 2026, 47% named price as the most likely cause and 19% named condition. In Southwest Florida, insurance and roof questions after the showing are a common third reason.

How many showings should it take to get an offer?

There is no fixed number, but if a steady stream of showings has produced no offers, the pattern itself is the signal. What the buyers who toured your home bought instead usually points to the real issue faster than waiting for more showings, and a listing agent can trace that through the MLS.

Is buyer feedback after a showing reliable?

It helps, but it is often filtered. Buyers’ agents work for their buyers and may keep feedback brief to protect their client’s negotiating position. The real reason usually comes out in a direct conversation between agents rather than on a feedback form.

Can insurance keep my Florida home from selling?

It can slow it down. Buyers often get an insurance quote after a showing, and roof age, the four-point inspection, wind mitigation features, and the flood zone all affect that quote. A listing agent who knows what Southwest Florida insurers and inspectors look at can get ahead of those questions before buyers walk away.

Should I lower my price if I’m getting showings but no offers?

Not automatically. If buyers are walking away over a smell, a repair, or an insurance concern, a price cut may not fix it. It pays to find out why buyers are passing before deciding whether price, condition, or something else is the right change.
