Spring Run at The Brooks Estero FL: what happens when the golf club is also the HOA? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 26, 2026·11 min read

Golf membership at Spring Run at The Brooks is not optional, because the golf club and the master association are the same organization rather than two separate bodies. Every one of the 847 homes carries the same annual club fee — $9,820 for 2026-2027, effective November 1, per the club's own Membership at a Glance document. There is no social-only tier to downgrade to, no waitlist to skip, and no equity stake to sell back when you leave. What there is instead is a $12,000 resale fee at the closing table that appears on no listing sheet.

Most buyers who tour Spring Run come in asking how long the wait is to join. That's the right question at Shadow Wood, at Bonita Bay, at most private clubs in Southwest Florida. It's the wrong question here, and the reason is structural.

At Spring Run, the organization that runs the golf club is the organization that runs the community. The club's own paperwork shows it: the body that publishes the annual membership fee also lists the neighborhood management companies, collects the master estoppel fee when you sell, and processes the paperwork when you lease your home out. One organization, one assessment, one set of books.

That single fact drives almost everything a buyer needs to know here — including the parts that surprise people at the closing table.

What Spring Run actually is

Spring Run sits inside The Brooks, the master development east of US 41 in the Village of Estero, even though the homes carry a 34135 Bonita Springs mailing address. That mismatch matters: your mailing address is not your government, and the municipality sets your millage rate, approves your renovation permit, and determines your flood discount. Our guide to checking whether a home is inside the Village of Estero walks through the verification.

Pulte built Spring Run for master developer Bonita Bay Group between 1998 and 2002, and nothing has been added since. The community is fully built out at 847 residences across ten neighborhoods, so every home is a resale — no sales center, no builder incentive to time, no model row.

The golf course is an 18-hole Gordon G. Lewis design, later renovated by Sanford Golf Design with Wadsworth Construction, a project reported in 2014 that reworked tees, greens, bunkers, irrigation, and drainage without changing the routing. Also on site: a clubhouse with dining, a heated resort pool and spa, lighted Har-Tru tennis and bocce, and a fitness room.

Homes and pricing in 2026

Spring Run is the most accessible address in The Brooks. Its ten neighborhoods break down into four product types:

  • Garden condominiums — Streamside, Sunset Stream, and Winding Stream. Two-bedroom, two-bath plans, typically with carport parking. The condo buildings are two stories with no elevators.
  • Carriage and coach homes — Autumn Lake and Hidden Lakes, with attached garages. Also two stories, also without elevators.
  • Attached villas — Sabal Cove and Coral Cove.
  • Detached villas and single-family homes — Whisper Creek, Silver Creek, and Willow Creek, most with two-car garages and many with private pools.

As of late October 2026 there were eight active listings, ranging from $309,900 to $499,000. That's thin inventory, and worth saying plainly: with this few homes on the market, any median you read — ours included — is a small sample, not a trend.

MLS figures reported for the trailing twelve months through July 2026 put closed sales around 34, a median sold price near $386,500, a median sale-to-list ratio around 94.9%, and median days on market around 63. Treat those as directional and verify before you write an offer. What the sold data does show consistently is a gap between asking prices and closing prices — homes here are not trading at list.

What it costs to own, and which parts you choose

Here's where the one-organization structure shows up on your bank statement.

The club fee is mandatory and it is an assessment. The 2026-2027 annual fee is $9,820, effective November 1, covering capital, a new project fund, reserve, and a $725 prepaid food minimum already baked into the number. Two years earlier the same document series showed $9,308 for 2024-2025 — a rise of about 5.5% over two years.

For context, trade coverage of bundled golf communities generally puts annual dues in the $4,000 to $8,000 range nationally. Spring Run sits above that band, because an 18-hole course and a full amenity package are carried by 847 homes rather than a few thousand.

Golf is included; the cart is not. Members pay no green fee, but cart fees run $31.00 for 18 holes and $21.00 for 9 holes, year round.

On top of the club fee:

  • Blue Stream Fiber — $215.55, subject to change
  • The Brooks CDD — operations and maintenance is a flat $1,017.25 per residential unit for FY2027, identical for a condo and a single-family home. Some neighborhoods carry additional debt service. If CDD assessments are new to you, our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero explains how they appear on your tax bill.
  • Your neighborhood association fee — and this is the number that is not published anywhere

That last line deserves emphasis. Spring Run's ten neighborhoods are administered by three different management companies, and the club's own document names them: Gulf Breeze at (239) 498-3311 for Willow Creek and Sunset Stream; Advanced Property Management at (239) 513-9433 for Silver Creek, Whisper Creek, Steamside, Sabal Cove, Autumn Lake, and Coral Cove; and Sandcastle Community Association at (239) 596-7200 for Winding Stream and Hidden Lakes. The association fee for the home you're considering lives with one of those three offices, not on the listing.

The Commons Club is a separate, optional decision. It's the shared amenity organization across The Brooks communities, and it is not part of your Spring Run assessment. It sells in three tiers, each with a non-refundable equity contribution plus annual dues: Bronze at $1,000 equity and roughly $490 a year, Silver at $6,000 and roughly $1,831 plus a $125.98 renovation fee running through 2030, and Gold at $12,000 and roughly $2,385 plus the same renovation fee. Gold is the only tier that includes the private Beach Club. If the Gulf beach is why you're looking at The Brooks at all, that's roughly $12,000 up front and about $2,500 a year on top of everything above.

The fee nobody sees coming

There is a resale fee of $12,000 when an outside buyer purchases in Spring Run, reduced to $3,500 when an existing Spring Run member moves within the community. It funds the replacement reserve.

This is not a club initiation deposit. You are not buying equity, and there is nothing to redeem when you sell — the membership is a licence to use the facilities, not an ownership share. The $12,000 is association capital, and it goes in at the closing table.

A few more line items from the same document:

  • Master estoppel fee — $250. Expect a second estoppel from your neighborhood association as well.
  • Delegation of use — $550 plus 6.5% tax, which includes the rental admin fee
  • Rental admin fee — $150 plus 6.5% sales tax

That delegation-of-use line is the one investors and snowbirds should read twice. Spring Run lets an owner transfer club privileges to a tenant — genuinely valuable if you plan to lease — but the transfer suspends your own access for the term of the lease. You cannot hand your tenant the golf and keep it too.

Buying here without a surprise

Spring Run is resale-only, which changes the shape of the transaction. There's no builder representative across the table, no incentive window to time, no upgrade list to negotiate. What there is instead is a stack of governing documents from two separate associations, a mandatory membership, and a five-figure fee at closing that appears on no listing sheet.

The practical consequence: by the time you're reviewing documents you need to already know which neighborhood you're buying in, which company holds its budget, and what its reserves look like. The master fee is the same for everyone. The neighborhood fee is not, and neither is the condition of the building it maintains. For the condominium neighborhoods, Florida's structural reserve rules apply — our guide to buying a condo in Southwest Florida covers what to request and when.

What would we look at first if you called us about Spring Run?

Honestly? Not the house.

The first thing we'd pull is which of the three management companies runs the neighborhood you're interested in, and what that association's budget and reserves actually say — because the club fee is identical across all 847 homes, which means the only variable cost in this community is the one nobody publishes. Two listings fifty yards apart can carry meaningfully different carrying costs, and the difference lives in a document you have to ask for.

Next, we'd want to understand what you actually want from the place. If you golf, this structure is a genuine value — you get a membership with no waitlist and no equity buy-in, which is rare in this market. If you don't golf, you need to see the number with clear eyes: $9,820 a year, every year, whether you play or not, and it is an association assessment rather than a club bill you could walk away from. That's not a reason to avoid Spring Run. It's a reason to decide deliberately instead of discovering it in week three.

We'd also flag the $12,000 resale fee early, because buyers who learn about it late tend to feel ambushed, and buyers who know about it from the start tend to simply fold it into the offer.

None of this is complicated. It's just easy to miss, and expensive to miss. That's the part we take care of for you.

How Spring Run compares

Against Copperleaf at The Brooks. Same master development, same Commons Club, same CDD structure — opposite access models. Copperleaf sells memberships to non-residents, so its tee sheet isn't limited to its homeowners, and its all-in annual cost runs meaningfully higher. Spring Run's course serves its 847 homes and nobody else. We covered the density math in our Copperleaf at The Brooks buyer's guide.

Against Shadow Wood at The Brooks. The mirror image. Golf there is optional and non-equity across 54 holes, with a waitlist — you can buy a home and skip golf entirely, or buy in and wait. Spring Run offers neither choice: you're in from day one, at a fixed price, with no queue. Our Shadow Wood at The Brooks buyer's guide covers that structure. Which model is better depends entirely on whether you play.

The short version: Spring Run is the lowest entry price in The Brooks and the least flexible cost structure in The Brooks. Those two facts are the same fact.

Flood, taxes, and the rest

Spring Run sits inside the Village of Estero, which holds a Community Rating System Class 6 rating — a 20% flood insurance discount for properties in a special flood hazard area. Flood zone is parcel-specific, so don't assume a zone from an address. Our guide to checking a home's flood risk in Estero covers how to verify a parcel, and the Village floodplain office confirms it at (239) 221-5036.

On taxes, Florida resets assessed value at sale, so the prior owner's bill tells you little about yours. The Village of Estero levies 0.7300 mills and Estero Fire Rescue 2.2880 mills, on top of county, school, and district levies. If this will be your primary residence, file for the homestead exemption — and note the additional exemption applies only to non-school taxes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy in Spring Run and not join the golf club?

No. Golf membership is automatic with ownership and there is no social-only alternative. The annual fee of $9,820 for 2026-2027 applies to every home regardless of whether you play, because the club and the master association are the same body and the fee is an association assessment.

Is there a waitlist or an equity buy-in for golf at Spring Run?

Neither. There is no waitlist and no equity purchase — membership is a licence that comes with the home. What there is instead is a $12,000 resale fee paid by outside buyers at closing, reduced to $3,500 for existing Spring Run members relocating within the community. It funds the replacement reserve and is not refundable or redeemable when you sell.

What are the HOA fees at Spring Run at The Brooks?

There isn't one number. You'll pay the master club fee of $9,820, a Brooks CDD assessment of $1,017.25 per unit for FY2027, Blue Stream Fiber at $215.55, and a neighborhood association fee that varies by which of the ten neighborhoods you buy in. Those neighborhood fees are not published — they're held by three different management companies, and you'll need the budget for your specific association.

Can I rent out a home in Spring Run and give my tenant golf access?

Yes. Spring Run allows an owner to delegate club privileges to a tenant for $550 plus 6.5% tax, which includes the rental admin fee. The trade-off is that delegating suspends your own access for the lease term — the membership transfers rather than duplicates. Each neighborhood also has its own lease application and approval process.

Is Spring Run in Estero or Bonita Springs?

Spring Run is in the Village of Estero, in Lee County, but it carries a 34135 Bonita Springs mailing ZIP code. The municipality — not the mailing address — determines your millage rate, your permitting authority, and your flood insurance rating class.

Deciding whether Spring Run fits

Spring Run is straightforward once you understand its structure: one organization, one mandatory membership, one fixed annual number, and a neighborhood fee you have to go find. For a golfer who wants to play without a waitlist or an equity buy-in, it's one of the better values in Estero. For a buyer who doesn't play, it's a question worth asking before you fall for a floor plan.
