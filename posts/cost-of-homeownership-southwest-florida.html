What does it really cost to own a home in Southwest Florida?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·January 11, 2026·3 min read

Buyers searching for homes in Southwest Florida quickly realize that the purchase price is only part of the equation. Today's buyers want clarity around total monthly costs — and rightly so.

Owning a home in Southwest Florida typically includes:

  • Mortgage payment
  • Property taxes
  • Homeowner's insurance
  • Flood insurance (when applicable)
  • HOA or condo fees
  • Maintenance and utilities

Insurance and HOA costs vary dramatically by location, building age, elevation, and construction type. A waterfront condo in Naples will have very different expenses than a single-family home in Estero or Fort Myers.

At The Borg Group, we help buyers compare true cost of ownership before making an offer. This includes reviewing HOA budgets, insurance history, flood zone maps, and future assessments — details that can dramatically impact affordability.

If you're searching for homes for sale in Southwest Florida, understanding these numbers upfront protects your budget and your peace of mind. A few of the numbers worth knowing before you shop:

Property taxes reset when you buy

Florida's Save Our Homes cap limits annual increases in assessed value for homesteaded properties — which means a long-time owner's tax bill often looks nothing like what a new buyer will actually pay, since the property reassesses to market value at purchase. If the home will be your primary residence, filing for the homestead exemption reduces your taxable value going forward. See our guide to Florida's homestead exemption and Save Our Homes cap for how and when to file.

Insurance is often two policies, not one

Homeowner's insurance here commonly runs several thousand dollars a year, driven by roof age, wind mitigation features, and proximity to the coast — our guide to 4-point and wind mitigation inspections covers how that inspection can lower your premium. If the home sits in a flood zone requiring coverage, flood insurance is a separate policy on top; see flood insurance costs in Southwest Florida for what drives that number.

HOA and CDD fees vary block by block

Some communities carry no HOA or CDD at all; many master-planned communities carry both, and combined costs can range from a couple hundred dollars a month to well over $1,000. Get the exact current figures for the specific home you're considering — see our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero for how these assessments work.
