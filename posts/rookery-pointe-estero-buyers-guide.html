Rookery Pointe Estero FL: what does the HOA fee actually cover? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 13, 2026·10 min read

What does the Rookery Pointe HOA fee actually cover?

For a detached home in Rookery Pointe, recent 2026 sale listings show one association fee of $1,545 a quarter — $6,180 a year — and it covers more than most buyers expect: internet and cable TV, lawn and grounds care, irrigation water, the gate and security, management, legal and accounting, the clubhouse and recreation facilities, and reserves. Rookery Pointe has no community development district on Lee County’s official list and no golf or club membership, so for most homes that one fee is close to the whole recurring community bill. Heatherstone, the townhome section, is billed differently: a neighborhood fee plus a master fee, about $5,650 to $5,800 a year on two 2026 sales.

That’s the question we hear most about Rookery Pointe, usually phrased as “Why is the HOA so much higher than the place down the road?” The honest answer is that you’re often not comparing the same thing. Some Estero communities split your costs across an HOA, a CDD on your tax bill, and a club. Rookery Pointe puts most of it in one line — and some of what’s in that line is money you’d otherwise be paying a cable company. Here’s the full picture: the community, the homes, the fee line by line, the flood map, and how to buy here well.

What Rookery Pointe is

Rookery Pointe is a gated, resale-only community in Estero’s 33928 ZIP code. Local market sites describe it as about 111 acres with five lakes and two preserves, built roughly between 2001 and 2006, with around 250 detached one- and two-story homes plus the Heatherstone townhome section. There’s no builder sales center and no new phase coming, so every purchase here is a standard Florida resale.

The U.S. Census Bureau’s boundary file places the community inside the Village of Estero, which matters for permits and for the Village’s flood insurance discount. If municipal status matters to your plans, confirm it for the exact parcel — our guide to checking Estero’s village limits shows how.

Access runs through the Envera gate system, according to one 2026 listing, so plan on your agent getting you added before a showing rather than pulling up to a guardhouse.

Homes and pricing in 2026

Rookery Pointe sits in the middle of Estero’s gated market: above the entry-level communities, well below the golf-club tier. Here’s what actually closed this year:

  • 20261 Rookery Dr — 2 bedrooms plus den, 1,834 square feet, built 2002, lakefront with a pool and a 2021 tile roof: sold for $520,000 in April after 57 days on the market.
  • 20141 Rookery Dr — 3 bedrooms, 2,612 square feet, built 2005, lake and fountain views: sold for $599,000 in July after 210 days.
  • 20441 Rookery Dr — 3 bedrooms plus den, 2,646 square feet, built 2002, 2021 tile roof: listed at $640,000 and sold for $595,000 in October.
  • Heatherstone townhomes — 3 bedrooms and about 1,700 to 1,770 square feet: one sold for $310,000 in June, another for $289,000 in early November.

In November 2026, one market site showed nine homes for sale at an average asking price of about $598,000, with detached homes ranging from roughly 1,660 to more than 4,300 square feet. Two things stand out in that data. First, sale prices are landing below list on the larger detached homes. Second, days on market vary widely — 57 days for a well-priced lakefront pool home, 210 for another. Pricing and condition decide how long a Rookery Pointe home sits, and that gives a prepared buyer room to negotiate.

The fee, line by line

Every detached-home listing we found from 2026 showed the same $1,545 quarterly fee. Taken together, those listings describe it as including:

  • Internet and cable TV — a bulk service agreement, which means you may be able to skip a separate internet and TV bill.
  • Lawn and grounds maintenance, plus irrigation water.
  • Security — the gate system.
  • Recreation facilities — the clubhouse, community pool and spa, fitness center, tennis and pickleball courts, basketball, playground, library, and the outdoor skating rink.
  • Management, legal and accounting, and reserves — the money set aside for future repairs and replacements.

One caution: an older 2025 listing showed the fee as $5,804 with no frequency stated, which is a good reminder that fee fields on listings aren’t always complete. Always confirm the current amount and what it includes with the association before you rely on it.

What the fee doesn’t cover

For a detached home, the fee doesn’t touch the house itself. Your roof, exterior, pool, windows, and air conditioning are yours, and so is your homeowners insurance. You’ll also pay property taxes, and here’s the trap: the seller’s tax bill isn’t your tax bill. One 2026 listing showed 2025 taxes of $3,348 on a home that sold for $599,000. That number likely reflects years of Save Our Homes caps. When you buy, the home is reassessed at market value, so budget from the purchase price, not the seller’s bill — and file for your own homestead exemption if it’s your primary residence.

How Heatherstone is billed

Heatherstone townhomes carry two lines instead of one. A June 2026 sale showed a $684 quarterly neighborhood fee plus a $763 quarterly master fee, with total annual recurring fees of $5,788. A November sale showed $730 (frequency not stated) plus a $684 quarterly master fee, totaling $5,656 a year. The two listings don’t agree on the master amount, so ask for the current budget for both associations rather than trusting either one.

The same June listing showed one-time fees of $1,650 at closing, including a $1,500 transfer fee, and leasing rules of a 30-day minimum and up to three leases a year. A detached-home listing also showed a 30-day minimum lease. If you’re buying with any plan to rent, confirm the current rules in the governing documents before you write an offer.

No CDD, and why that matters for comparisons

No community development district under the name Rookery Pointe, Rookery, or Heatherstone appears on Lee County’s official special districts list of 49 districts. That’s the reason Rookery Pointe’s fee looks high next to some neighbors: in a CDD community, part of what you pay for roads, drainage, and amenities shows up as a separate assessment on your property tax bill. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero explains how that works. Still, confirm on the tax bill for the specific parcel — a list check isn’t a title search.

The flood map: mostly minimal, with one exception

We checked six Rookery Pointe addresses, including two in Heatherstone, against FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer on November 13, 2026. Five mapped to unshaded Zone X, FEMA’s minimal-hazard category. One point, on Rookery Drive, mapped to Zone AE in a regulatory floodway. All six fall on panel 12071C0583G, effective November 17, 2022.

Two caveats. The address points come from the Census geocoder, which estimates a location along the street, so a single AE reading tells you a mapped flood area runs close to that part of the community, not that a particular house sits in it. And a listing’s line like “no flood insurance required” is the seller’s description, not a determination. Pull the flood zone for the exact parcel — our guide to checking flood risk in Estero walks through it.

Even in Zone X, the flood decision isn’t over. The Village of Estero participates in FEMA’s Community Rating System at Class 6, which gives National Flood Insurance Program policies a 20% discount, and Citizens Property Insurance requires flood coverage on most policies with wind coverage starting January 1, 2027.

Amenities, and who Rookery Pointe fits

The amenity set is built around a 4,500-square-foot clubhouse, according to local market sites, with a Junior Olympic-size pool and spa, fitness center, tennis and pickleball, basketball, a playground, a library, and lakeside walking. There’s no golf course, which is part of why there’s no club bill.

Rookery Pointe tends to fit buyers who want a gated Estero address with real amenities and no golf dues, people who like one predictable number instead of three separate bills, full-time residents who’ll actually use the included internet and TV, and seasonal owners who want the lawn and grounds handled while they’re away.

How to buy in Rookery Pointe

Because it’s resale only, there’s no builder contract or builder rep. The process is a standard Florida purchase: offer, inspection period, insurance quotes, association approval, the HOA estoppel letter, and closing through a title company, with the seller paying doc stamps at $0.70 per $100 of the price. What changes here is what you need to see before the inspection period ends — the association’s budget, reserves, and rules, and, in Heatherstone, both associations’ documents. Our guide on how to evaluate a Florida HOA before buying covers what those documents tell you.

What would we look at first if you called us about Rookery Pointe?

The fee, but not the way most people look at it. We’d start with what that $6,180 a year is replacing in your budget. If it covers internet and TV you’d otherwise pay for, the real gap between Rookery Pointe and a lower-fee neighbor with a CDD may be much smaller than it looks — or it may not, and we’d show you which.

Then we’d look behind the fee at the association’s budget and reserves, because in a community built 20 to 25 years ago, how well the reserves are funded tells you whether that number holds steady or comes with a special assessment later. In Heatherstone, we’d confirm both associations and why listings disagree on the master fee.

On the house itself, we’d check the roof’s age and the wind mitigation report — many homes here have had tile roofs replaced in the last few years, and that moves your insurance premium more than the square footage does. And we’d pull the flood picture for that parcel, given the one AE reading on Rookery Drive. With days on market running from two months to seven, we’d use all of it to shape your offer.

The outcome you want is a clear, all-in monthly number before you commit, with no surprise in year two. That’s the part we take care of for you.

How Rookery Pointe compares

Copper Oaks is a useful contrast. Its detached homes showed a lower $864 quarterly HOA fee in our research, but Copper Oaks also has a CDD, adding about $1,353 a year for a single-family home in fiscal 2027, and all three addresses we checked there mapped to Zone AE. Our Copper Oaks Estero guide has the details.

Fountain Lakes is the low-fee option: a single-family listing showed $425 a quarter, with no CDD and a 9-hole par-3 course included. Homes there are older, built mostly between 1988 and 2002, and generally sell for less, and most of the community maps to shaded Zone X. See our Fountain Lakes Estero guide.

The takeaway: compare communities on the total of HOA, CDD, club dues, and the bills each fee replaces — not on the HOA line alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the HOA fees in Rookery Pointe, Estero?

Detached-home listings from 2026 show $1,545 a quarter, or $6,180 a year. Heatherstone townhomes pay a neighborhood fee plus a master fee, about $5,650 to $5,800 a year in total on two 2026 sales. Confirm the current fee with the association for the specific home.

What does the Rookery Pointe HOA fee include?

Listings describe it as covering internet and cable TV, lawn and grounds care, irrigation water, the gate and security, management, legal and accounting, recreation facilities, and reserves. It doesn’t cover your home’s roof, exterior, insurance, or property taxes.

Does Rookery Pointe have a CDD?

No community development district under the name Rookery Pointe or Heatherstone appears on Lee County’s official list of special districts. Confirm on the property tax bill for the parcel you’re considering.

Is Rookery Pointe in a flood zone?

Five of six addresses we checked against FEMA’s map on November 13, 2026, mapped to unshaded Zone X, the minimal-hazard category. One point on Rookery Drive mapped to Zone AE in a floodway, so check the exact parcel before relying on any listing’s description.

How much do homes cost in Rookery Pointe?

Detached homes sold for $520,000 to $599,000 in 2026 in the sales we reviewed, and Heatherstone townhomes sold for $289,000 to $310,000. In November 2026, active listings averaged about $598,000.
