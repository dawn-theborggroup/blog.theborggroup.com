Closing in December? Who pays the 2026 property tax bill? Southwest Florida 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 19, 2026·8 min read

Who pays the 2026 property tax bill when you sell in November or December?

You pay your share of 2026 — but on a November or December closing, it’s a real number, not an estimate. Lee County mailed 2026 tax bills on October 31, so a late-year closing usually works from the actual bill instead of last year’s. In most cases the bill is paid in full at closing at the early-payment discount (4% in November, 3% in December), and your buyer credits you back for the days they’ll own the home. If your closing slips to January, you own all of 2026 and the whole bill is yours.

“Who pays the property taxes if we close in December?” is one of the questions we hear most from Southwest Florida sellers this time of year. It’s a fair question, because the answer changes once the bill comes out — and most of what you’ll read online describes the mid-year version.

Here’s how it actually works on a late-year closing, what the market looks like right now, and where sellers lose money without noticing.

What the Southwest Florida market looks like heading into season

The newest closed-sale numbers from Florida Realtors, released this week for October 2026, show a market that’s steady rather than sliding — at least for single-family homes.

  • Lee County single-family: 1,115 closed sales in October, down just 0.7% from a year ago, while statewide single-family sales fell 10.1%. The median sale price was $389,900, flat year over year. That’s the seventh straight month at or above the prior year.
  • Lee County condos and townhomes: 373 sales, up 12.7%, but the median fell 3.9% to $269,000. Buyers are active; they’re just paying less.
  • Collier County single-family: a $780,000 median, up 4.9%, on 362 sales (down 4.7%). Collier condos went the other way — the median fell 5.5% to $415,750 and sales dropped 25.9%.
  • Inventory: Florida Gulf Coast University’s research institute counted 9,635 active listings in Lee County in October, down 12.7% from a year earlier but up 5.0% from September. Collier had 5,020, up 11.0% month over month.
  • Days on market: the Naples Area Board of Realtors reported an average of 99 days on market in September, with 5.5 months of inventory — down from 7.8 months a year earlier.

In plain terms: fewer homes are for sale than a year ago, single-family prices are holding, condos are still adjusting, and listings are starting to build as season approaches. We covered the price side in detail in our look at whether prices have hit bottom in Estero. Today’s question is narrower — and it’s about money that shows up on your closing statement.

How the 2026 tax bill is handled on a late-year closing

Florida property taxes are paid in arrears. The 2026 bill covers January 1 through December 31, 2026, and it isn’t due until March 31, 2027. Under Florida law, paying early earns a discount: 4% in November, 3% in December, 2% in January, and 1% in February.

For most of the year, a closing has to estimate your share using last year’s bill — that’s the version in our seller closing costs breakdown for Lee and Collier counties. Once the bill is out, the math gets cleaner:

  • The actual 2026 bill is used, not last year’s. No guessing, and usually no re-proration later.
  • The bill is typically paid in full at closing, out of your proceeds, at whatever discount applies that month.
  • Your buyer credits you back for the days from closing through December 31 — based on the discounted amount, not the full bill.

What does that look like in real dollars?

Say your 2026 bill is $6,000 before any discount. Here’s roughly how three closing dates compare:

  • Closing November 20: the bill is paid at the 4% discount, $5,760. You owned the home for 323 days of 2026, so your share is about $5,097, and your buyer credits you about $663.
  • Closing December 15: paid at 3%, $5,820. Your 348 days come to about $5,549; your buyer credits you about $271.
  • Closing January 8, 2027: you owned all of 2026, so the full bill is yours — $5,880 at the 2% January discount — plus about $113 for the first week of 2027, estimated from the 2026 bill.

Same house, same buyer, roughly a $440 swing between mid-December and early January. That isn’t a reason to rush a closing you’re not ready for. It is a reason to know the number before you agree to a date.

These figures are illustrations. Your title company calculates the exact amounts, and the day-count conventions in your contract decide who owns the closing day.

What else is on that bill?

Your tax bill isn’t only ad valorem tax. In much of Estero, Bonita Springs, and the newer parts of Fort Myers, it also carries non-ad valorem assessments — CDD debt and maintenance, solid waste, and stormwater. One Southwest Florida title attorney notes those are generally treated as billed in advance, while ad valorem taxes are billed in arrears, which is why one line can show as a credit and another as a debit on the same statement. If you’re in a CDD community, our guide to reading a CDD budget explains what those lines are.

Why a December closing can matter to your buyer

A buyer who plans to live in the home generally needs to own it and occupy it as a permanent residence on January 1 to apply for that year’s homestead exemption. Close on December 15, and an owner-occupant buyer can apply for 2027. Close on January 8, and their first homestead year is 2028.

That’s a real reason some buyers push for December this year — and a bargaining point you should know you’re holding. It sits on top of the Amendment 3 changes, which raise the non-school homestead exemption starting January 1, 2027. Lee County’s tax collector notes Amendment 3 doesn’t change 2026 bills, so it won’t touch your proration. Our Amendment 3 overview covers what it does change.

Common seller mistakes with year-end closings

  • Estimating your net from last year’s bill. If your 2026 bill is out, use it. Non-ad valorem charges aren’t on your TRIM notice, so the final bill can be higher than you expected.
  • Assuming your lender’s escrow “handles it.” If your servicer already paid the 2026 bill in November, the closing statement should show your buyer crediting you — and any leftover escrow balance comes back to you from the servicer after payoff, not at the closing table.
  • Agreeing to a January date without running the numbers. A few weeks can move the full 2026 bill onto your side of the ledger and shrink the discount.
  • Overlooking the CDD line. In a CDD community, the assessment rides on the same bill and gets handled at the same time.
  • Pricing off the headline. Lee condo prices are still falling while single-family is flat. Your number comes from your community’s closed sales, not the county median.

What would we look at first if your closing is landing in December?

We’d start with where your 2026 bill stands — whether it’s already been paid, by you or by your lender’s escrow, and what non-ad valorem charges ride on it. That one answer changes what your closing statement should look like, and it’s the piece sellers most often get surprised by.

Next, we’d look at what the date is worth on both sides. For you, it’s the proration and discount. For an owner-occupant buyer, it may be a full year of homestead. Knowing both lets us treat the closing date as part of the negotiation instead of an afterthought.

Then we’d make sure your net sheet reflects the real bill, so the number you plan around is the number you see at closing — and we’d coordinate with the title company early so the math is settled well before the closing table.

When that’s handled well, you choose a closing date with eyes open, you don’t leave a credit on the table, and nothing on the statement catches you off guard. That’s the part we take care of for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays property taxes when you sell a house in December in Florida?

Each side pays for the days it owns the home. On a December closing, the 2026 bill is usually already out, so it is typically paid in full at closing from the seller's proceeds at the 3% December discount, and the buyer credits the seller for the days from closing through December 31. Your title company calculates the exact figures.

Are Florida property taxes paid in arrears?

Yes. The 2026 bill covers January 1 through December 31, 2026, was mailed in Lee County on October 31, 2026, and is due by March 31, 2027. Paying early earns a 4% discount in November, 3% in December, 2% in January, and 1% in February.

Is it better to close in December or January when selling in Southwest Florida?

It depends on your situation. Closing in January means you owned all of 2026, so the full 2026 bill is yours and the discount drops to 2%. A December closing also lets an owner-occupant buyer apply for a 2027 homestead exemption, which can make the date valuable in negotiation.

What if my mortgage escrow already paid my 2026 property taxes?

Then the closing statement should show your buyer crediting you for their share of 2026. Any remaining escrow balance is refunded to you by your loan servicer after the loan is paid off, not at the closing table.

Does Amendment 3 change my 2026 property tax proration?

No. The Lee County Tax Collector notes Amendment 3 takes effect January 1, 2027 and does not affect 2026 tax bills. It raises the non-school homestead exemption and lowers the non-homestead assessment cap starting in 2027.

Florida Realtors data for October 2026 shows Lee County's single-family median at $389,900, flat year over year, on 1,115 sales, while statewide sales fell 10.1%. Lee condo prices fell 3.9% to $269,000. Collier's single-family median rose 4.9% to $780,000.
