The Place at Corkscrew vs Corkscrew Shores, Estero FL: which is right for you? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 24, 2026·10 min read

Is The Place at Corkscrew or Corkscrew Shores the better fit in Estero?

It comes down to how you want to pay and how you want to live. The Place at Corkscrew is a Lennar and Pulte community of about 1,325 homes built around a resort pool campus, and it carries a CDD bond assessment that runs roughly $1,000 to $1,500 a year depending on your lot width, with bonds scheduled to pay off in 2046 or 2050 depending on the phase. Corkscrew Shores is a sold-out, 648-home Pulte community built around a 240-acre lake, with no CDD at all but a larger HOA of roughly $7,600 a year that never pays off. If you want the lower annual number today and resort-style amenities, The Place usually wins. If you want lakefront living, a single fee, and nothing on your tax bill but taxes, Corkscrew Shores usually wins.

These two sit a few minutes apart on Corkscrew Road, both carry Estero mailing addresses, and Pulte built homes in both — so buyers compare them constantly. Most comparisons stop at “one has a CDD and one doesn’t.” The more useful question is what the CDD actually is at The Place, how long it lasts, and what it buys you compared with a bigger HOA that has no end date. Here’s the side-by-side.

Quick snapshot: both communities at a glance

The Place at Corkscrew

  • About 1,325 single-family homes planned, built by Lennar and Pulte since 2016
  • Homes roughly 1,674 to 4,242 square feet, one- and two-story
  • Amenity campus with a resort pool and waterslide, the Barefoot Bar & Grill, a two-story fitness center, a day spa, tennis, pickleball, and a dog park
  • Corkscrew Farms Community Development District, plus The Place Master Association
  • Mostly resale today — when we checked on November 24, 2026, the builder’s own site showed no homes listed as available, so confirm any remaining new construction directly

Corkscrew Shores

  • About 648 single-family homes on roughly 722 acres, built by Pulte between 2014 and about 2020
  • A 240-acre former limerock lake for kayaking, paddleboarding, catch-and-release fishing, and electric boats up to 16 feet — no gas engines
  • The Captain’s Club, about 13,000 square feet, with a resort pool, a lakeside restaurant and bar, five Har-Tru clay tennis courts, pickleball, bocce, and a 24-hour fitness center
  • No CDD — it doesn’t appear on Lee County’s roster of special districts
  • Every home is a resale

Price and value: what each one really costs you every year

On purchase price, the two overlap more than you’d expect. Both have homes starting in the low-to-mid $400s. Corkscrew Shores reaches higher — past $1.5 million for larger lakefront homes — and its median sale has run well above The Place’s, largely because of the lake. At The Place, resales have mostly run from the mid-$400s to about $1.25 million.

The bigger difference is the yearly carrying cost, and it isn’t the one most people assume.

The Place at Corkscrew: a smaller HOA plus a CDD that ends

Listing data shows HOA dues at The Place running roughly $300 to $450 a month, which covers the amenity campus and common-area upkeep (not food and drink at the Barefoot Bar & Grill). Confirm the current figure for the specific home on the estoppel letter.

On top of that comes the Corkscrew Farms CDD, created by Lee County Ordinance 15-16 in December 2015. Here’s what the district’s own audited financial statements show:

  • Two bond series. $20 million in Series 2016 bonds, scheduled to be fully repaid by November 1, 2046, and $28 million in Series 2017 bonds for the second assessment area, scheduled to be fully repaid by November 1, 2050. As of September 30, 2025, $22,730,000 was still outstanding.
  • Your lot width sets your share. The debt assessment is tiered by lot size — the district’s audits list 52-foot, 62-foot, and 75-foot lots, ranging from about $1,000 a year for the narrowest lots to $1,500 for the widest.
  • The operating piece is small. The district’s operations and maintenance assessment was $70.35 per lot in the fiscal year ending September 2025. Day-to-day maintenance runs through The Place Master Association under an agreement with the district.

The line on your Lee County tax bill typically runs a little higher than these base rates because of collection costs and the early-payment discount built into the billing, so look at the actual bill for the address you’re considering.

Put together, a home at The Place often carries somewhere around $4,700 to $7,000 a year in HOA plus CDD, depending on the dues and the lot. And the CDD bond portion — the bulk of it — is debt with a payoff date, not a forever fee.

Corkscrew Shores: one bigger fee, no end date

Corkscrew Shores runs approximately $1,782 per quarter ($7,128 a year) for the master HOA, plus a $480 annual Captain’s Club food-and-beverage minimum, for a total of about $7,608 a year. There’s no CDD and no separate dining membership.

Budget for one-time costs at closing too. Recent listing disclosures have shown a transfer fee of about $2,393, a capital contribution of about $2,392, and an irrigation setup charge of about $1,500.

That’s the trade-off in plain terms. At The Place, the biggest piece of your extra cost is a loan that shrinks every year until it’s gone. At Corkscrew Shores, your cost is an HOA that pays for a lake, a club, and a restaurant forever — and HOA budgets usually rise over time. Neither is better on its own. It depends on how long you plan to stay and what you want the money to buy. If you want to read the district’s numbers yourself, our guide on how to read a CDD budget before you buy in Estero walks through it.

Amenities head-to-head

The Place at Corkscrew is built for activity on land. The pool deck has a 100-foot waterslide, beach entry, cabanas, and a splash area next to the Barefoot Bar & Grill, a café, and a bourbon bar. The fitness center spans two floors with a studio and a day spa, and you’ll find tennis, pickleball, bocce, basketball, beach volleyball, fire pits, and an enclosed dog park.

Corkscrew Shores is built for life on the water. The 240-acre lake is deep and clear because it started as a limerock excavation, and most of the community’s identity comes from it — paddling, fishing, electric boating, and lake views from the Captain’s Club. The club adds the pool, the restaurant and bar, clay courts, pickleball, bocce, fitness, walking trails, and an activities director.

Both are gated, neither is age-restricted, and neither has golf. If golf is on your list, look elsewhere in Estero. If a gas-powered boat is a must, our WildBlue vs The Place at Corkscrew comparison covers the community up the road that allows it.

Who each community is best for

The Place at Corkscrew tends to fit you if:

  • You want the lowest realistic annual cost in this part of Corkscrew Road without giving up a full amenity campus
  • You love a busy pool deck, on-site dining, and lots of court sports
  • You want a newer home, or you’re hoping to find one of the last builder homes
  • You’re comfortable with a CDD line on your tax bill that ends on a set date

Corkscrew Shores tends to fit you if:

  • Lake views and quiet, non-gas water recreation are high on your list
  • You want a single association fee and no CDD on your tax bill at all
  • You prefer a finished community with mature landscaping and no construction nearby
  • You have room in your budget for a higher purchase price and a higher HOA in exchange for the lake

On flood risk, our earlier guides found most homes in both communities in FEMA Zone X, but zones are set parcel by parcel. Check the exact address before you offer — our guide to checking flood risk in Estero shows how.

Buying process differences

At Corkscrew Shores, every purchase is a resale on a standard Florida contract. The key documents are the HOA estoppel letter, the Captain’s Club minimum, and the one-time closing charges. Because the homes are now roughly 6 to 12 years old, roof age, a four-point inspection, and a wind mitigation report matter for your insurance quote.

At The Place at Corkscrew, most purchases are now resales too. If you find a remaining builder home, you’re on the builder’s contract instead, and you’ll want an agent registered with you before your first sales center visit — our guide to new construction contracts in Bonita Springs and Estero explains why. On a resale, you’re taking over the seller’s share of the CDD bonds along with the house, so the lot width, the phase, and whether that share was ever prepaid all matter.

What would we look at first if you called us about The Place versus Corkscrew Shores?

We’d start with the specific homes you like, not the communities in general. At The Place, the first thing we’d pin down is which bond series and lot width the home falls under, because that tells you how much CDD you’re taking on and how many years it has left. We’d also check whether the CDD share on that lot has already been paid down, because the district has collected prepayments from owners, and a home with no bond balance left is a different value than the one next door.

At Corkscrew Shores, we’d look at the total cost of the HOA and the one-time charges, what the association’s budget and reserves suggest about future increases, and how the home’s lake position and age affect both price and insurance.

Then we’d put the two homes side by side the way you’ll actually live with them: what you pay at closing, what you pay every year, what changes in 2046 or 2050, and what you get for it. That’s the part we take care of for you, so you can choose with the whole picture in front of you.

Our recommendation

If your priority is value and amenities, start with The Place at Corkscrew. Even with the CDD, its yearly cost is usually lower, and the bond portion has an end date. Just make sure you know the exact assessment for the lot you’re buying.

If your priority is the water and simplicity, start with Corkscrew Shores. You’ll pay more each year, but it’s one predictable fee, there’s nothing extra on your tax bill, and you get a 240-acre lake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does The Place at Corkscrew have a CDD?

Yes. It’s in the Corkscrew Farms Community Development District, created by Lee County in 2015. The district’s audits show a debt assessment of roughly $1,000 to $1,500 a year depending on lot width, plus a small operating assessment, billed on your Lee County property tax bill.

When does the CDD at The Place at Corkscrew end?

The Series 2016 bonds are scheduled to be fully repaid by November 1, 2046, and the Series 2017 bonds by November 1, 2050. Which date applies depends on which part of the community the home is in. After the bonds are repaid, only the small operating assessment would remain.

Does Corkscrew Shores have a CDD?

No. Corkscrew Shores doesn’t appear on Lee County’s roster of community development districts. Your community costs come through the HOA, which runs about $7,128 a year plus a $480 Captain’s Club food-and-beverage minimum.

Which is cheaper to own, The Place at Corkscrew or Corkscrew Shores?

Usually The Place, on a yearly basis. Its HOA plus CDD typically lands around $4,700 to $7,000 a year, compared with about $7,600 at Corkscrew Shores. Corkscrew Shores avoids the CDD entirely, and purchase prices overlap, so compare the specific homes.

Can you still buy new construction at The Place at Corkscrew?

Availability has thinned to the point that most sales are resales. When we checked on November 24, 2026, the builder’s site showed no homes listed as available. Ask us to confirm with Lennar and Pulte before you plan around a new build.
