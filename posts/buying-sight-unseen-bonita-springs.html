Buying a home sight unseen in Bonita Springs: what remote buyers need to know

By Mark & Dawn Borg·May 25, 2026·9 min read

Yes — and more buyers are doing it than you might think. Remote purchases in Bonita Springs work when you pair a knowledgeable local buyer's agent with a thorough live video walkthrough, comprehensive inspections (including Florida's required 4-point and wind mitigation inspections), a flood zone check before you make an offer, and insurance quotes before you close. Keep all your contingencies intact, and you have real protection even if you never set foot in the home until moving day.

You found a home that checks every box, but you're in Ohio, New York, or Michigan and can't get down before it goes under contract. That's not rare anymore, especially in a relocation market like Southwest Florida. This market's pace works in your favor too — homes have been averaging around 120 days on market in 2026, well above the frenzied pandemic-era pace, which means you have time and don't need to waive inspections or rush due diligence to compete.

Start with the right local agent — your eyes on the ground

Every sight-unseen purchase lives or dies by the quality of your local buyer's agent — the person who knows whether the street floods after an afternoon rain and whether the listing photos are hiding something. Ask directly: how many out-of-state or sight-unseen buyers have you helped? Will you do a live video walkthrough and stay on as long as needed? Will you follow up with a detailed written summary? Hesitant or vague answers mean keep looking.

The live video walkthrough: what to ask your agent to cover

A static 3D tour or listing photos aren't enough — you need a live call (FaceTime, Zoom, WhatsApp) with your agent walking the home in real time while you ask questions. Give your agent a specific checklist beforehand: the roof from multiple angles, under every sink, the electrical panel type and age, the AC unit inside and out, the water heater, all windows and sliding doors (impact-rated or not), any patched flooring or drywall, the garage floor and corners for moisture, the lanai and pool deck, the view from every window (not just the listing photos), the street in both directions, and any standing water after recent rain.

Professional photographers have tricks to make rooms look larger and brighter than they are. Your agent's job is to give you the unvarnished version — smells, sounds, and anything that feels off.

One more step: pull the property's prior listing history on Zillow — older listings often show photos of rooms the current one omits.

Florida inspections: what buyers from up north don't expect

Florida has inspection requirements that directly affect insurance costs and your ability to get coverage at all, and remote buyers often don't know they exist until deep in the contract. Budget for the most comprehensive standard inspection available, and ask for an inspector willing to do a virtual follow-up call.

The 4-point inspection covers roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. Most carriers require one for homes 20–40+ years old before writing a new policy. If the seller has one under 12 months old, check whether your insurer will accept it; otherwise budget $75–$150 standalone, often less bundled with your main inspection.

The wind mitigation inspection documents hurricane-resistant features and can generate meaningful discounts on the wind portion of your premium — costs roughly the same as a 4-point and is almost always worth having. See our full guide on Florida's 4-point and wind mitigation inspections for the complete breakdown.

Buying a condo adds a layer: Florida law requires associations with buildings three stories or taller to conduct milestone inspections and fund structural reserves. Ask for the building's structural integrity reserve study (SIRS) and any completed milestone inspection reports before you offer.

Check the flood zone before you make an offer

Non-negotiable. For properties along Bonita Beach, near the Imperial River, or adjacent to Estero Bay, the difference between an AE zone and Zone X can mean thousands of dollars a year in mandatory flood insurance. Ask your agent to pull the FEMA flood map designation before you submit an offer — see our flood zones guide for what each designation actually means. If an elevation certificate exists, request it: every foot above Base Flood Elevation roughly halves your annual flood premium; every foot below roughly doubles it.

Get insurance quotes before you close — not after

This is the single most common financial mistake remote buyers make. Once under contract and inside your inspection period, get quotes from multiple agents for homeowners and flood coverage immediately — ask specifically about Citizens Insurance eligibility and current private-market options for that property. See our homeowner's insurance guide for the full picture.

Keep every contingency — all of them

Florida contracts typically provide a 15-day inspection period during which you can cancel for any reason discovered, even in an as-is sale. Don't waive it. Keep your financing and appraisal contingencies too — a remote purchase is not the moment to cut corners on contract protections.

Researching the neighborhood from 1,200 miles away

Use Google Street View to walk the neighborhood virtually. Join community Facebook groups for the specific development — residents will tell you things a listing agent won't. Check the Lee County property appraiser's site for permit history; an addition with no corresponding permit is worth knowing about. And ask your agent the questions no database answers: what's the street like at 6 p.m. on a Tuesday, how's the HOA's reputation, any recent community management issues.
