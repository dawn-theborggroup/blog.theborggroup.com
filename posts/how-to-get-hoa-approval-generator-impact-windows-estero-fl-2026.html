How to get HOA approval for a generator or impact windows in Estero FL: 2026 buyer's guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 22, 2026·9 min read

How do you get HOA approval for a generator or impact windows in Estero, FL?

Ask the association for its adopted hurricane protection specifications, design your generator or impact windows to match them, and run the association's architectural review and the building permit side by side. Since 2024, section 720.3035(6) of the Florida Statutes has required every homeowners' association to adopt those specifications, and it bars the board or architectural committee from denying an application that conforms to them — the list expressly includes impact windows and doors, storm shutters, reinforced garage doors, exterior fixed generators, and fuel storage tanks. The association can still control color, style, and a consistent look. The second approval comes from the Village of Estero or Lee County, depending on where the address actually sits — and the Village's own permit list includes generators, windows, doors, and shutters.

If you're buying in Estero and a whole-home generator or impact glass is on your list, the question isn't really "will the HOA let me?" anymore. For a single-family home in a Florida HOA, the answer to that is now mostly yes. The better question is "what exactly will they approve, and what will it take to get it installed?" — and the best time to ask it is before you close, not after.

It's a question that comes up a lot with buyers moving down from the Northeast and Midwest, especially anyone who has lived through a long power outage. Here's how it actually works in Estero.

Why this matters before you buy, not after

Florida's hurricane-protection rule for HOAs came from CS/HB 293, which passed the House 108–0 and was signed on May 28, 2024 (chapter 2024-205, Laws of Florida). According to the House's final bill analysis, it applies to every HOA in the state, "regardless of when the community was created." That's real protection.

But there are three things the law doesn't do, and they're where buyers get surprised:

  • It doesn't pick your product. The board adopts the specifications. They can cover color, style, and "any other factor deemed relevant," as long as they comply with the building code. If the spec says bronze frames and you've priced white, your quote just changed.
  • It doesn't skip the permit. The Village of Estero's "Do I Need a Permit" brochure (updated February 21, 2025) lists generators, windows, exterior doors, garage doors, and shutters as permitted work.
  • It doesn't cover condos the same way. A condo association runs under a different statute with different wording — more on that below.

And here's the expensive part: a generator that's already on the house isn't automatically an approved, permitted generator. If a previous owner skipped either step, you inherit the question.

How to get a generator or impact windows approved in Estero, step by step

This is the "what to know and what to watch for" version. It's the order we'd think about it in if you were buying today.

  1. Confirm what kind of association it is — and which government holds the permit. A single-family home or villa in an HOA falls under chapter 720. A condominium unit falls under chapter 718. Separately, an Estero mailing address doesn't guarantee the home is inside the Village; some Estero ZIP codes sit in unincorporated Lee County. Our guide on how to check if a home is inside the Village of Estero walks through settling that.
  2. Get the hurricane protection specifications and architectural guidelines during your inspection period. Under section 720.303(4)(b), an HOA with 100 or more parcels has had to post its declaration of covenants and current rules on a website or app since January 1, 2025. Many Estero associations keep their architectural standards there or with the management company. If the association can't produce adopted hurricane specs, that's worth knowing now.
  3. Hold what's already installed up against the permit history. If the home already has impact windows, shutters, or a generator, check that the work was permitted and finaled. Our open-permit guide for Estero covers where those records live, including the 2016 handoff from Lee County to the Village.
  4. Know what the association can and can't say. It can require you to match the specs and an existing unified look. It can't deny a conforming hurricane-protection application. If it denies anything, section 720.3035(4)(a) requires written notice naming the specific rule and the specific part of your plan that doesn't conform.
  5. Plan for both approvals at the same time. Section 720.3035(1)(c) says an association can't require a building permit as a prerequisite to its review. You don't have to finish one line before you get in the other.
  6. Think about placement early. The Village's generator permit checklist (updated January 1, 2024) asks for a scaled site plan showing the generator and any LP tank in relation to the building, its openings, and the property lines. It also says all generators and electrical equipment must be installed above Base Flood Elevation — which matters on lower Estero lots. Our guide to checking a home's flood risk in Estero explains where to find that number.
  7. Match the windows to the insurance credit you want. On Florida's wind mitigation form (OIR-B1-1802), the top opening-protection rating requires all openings — doors, windows, skylights, and the garage door — to be large-missile impact rated. A partial upgrade can still help, but it may not land the credit you're picturing. Our post on four-point and wind mitigation inspections goes deeper.

Florida-specific nuances Estero buyers should know

Condos play by a different rule

Section 718.113(5) also requires condo boards to adopt hurricane protection specifications, and says that if the condo documents require approval, the board can't refuse a conforming installation. But the condo statute's wording isn't the HOA list — it doesn't name exterior generators — and condo windows and doors are often tied up with who maintains the building envelope. Becker & Poliakoff's Joseph Adams wrote in the News-Press in January 2025 that he expected most condo associations weren't yet in strict compliance with the specification requirement. If you're buying a condo in Estero, read the declaration and the board's adopted specs before you assume anything. Our Southwest Florida condo buying guide covers the rest of that due diligence.

The fuel matters as much as the generator

The HOA statute lists "fuel storage tanks" as hurricane protection right alongside generators, so a conforming tank gets the same protection from denial. The Village's checklist also asks for a fuel gas subcontractor form. Whether natural gas is available on a particular Estero street is an address-by-address question — confirm it with the provider before you design around it.

The estoppel only catches what's been noticed

Florida's HOA estoppel certificate asks whether there's "any open violation of rule or regulation noticed to the parcel owner." That's useful, but it only reports violations the association has actually sent. An unapproved generator nobody has flagged yet won't show up there.

Grants can help pay for it

If you're planning impact windows or doors after closing, the My Safe Florida Home program is worth checking for current eligibility and funding before you sign a contractor.

Red flags to watch for

  • "We don't allow generators." For a Florida HOA, that answer doesn't square with section 720.3035(6). Ask for the adopted specifications instead.
  • No adopted specs at all. The law has required them since 2024. If they don't exist, expect a slower, less predictable review.
  • A denial with no rule cited. A written denial has to name the specific rule and the nonconforming part.
  • Equipment with no paper trail. A generator, shutters, or impact glass with no matching permit or approval letter is a question for the seller during your inspection period.
  • Mismatched windows. One non-rated slider or an unrated garage door can keep a home from the top wind mitigation rating.

How a buyer's agent protects you

Most of this isn't hard once you know it exists. The problem is timing. Every item above is easy to resolve during your inspection period and much harder after closing, when the seller has moved on and you're the owner of record.

What would we look at first if you wanted a generator or impact windows in an Estero home?

We'd start with two questions most buyers don't think to ask: what kind of association is this, and which building department does this address answer to? Those two answers decide which statute protects you and where the permit history lives.

From there, we'd look at what's already on the house. If there's a generator or impact glass, we want to know it was approved and permitted — because if it wasn't, that's a conversation to have with the seller now, not a surprise with the association later. If you're planning to add one, we'd want the association's hurricane specifications in front of you early, so you can price the product they'll actually approve. We'd also flag the things that change the plan on a specific lot: the flood elevation, where a tank could go, and whether a window package would earn the insurance credit you're expecting.

What you get when it's handled well is simple: you close knowing what you can install, roughly what it'll take, and that nothing already on the house is going to come back on you. That's the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Florida HOA deny a whole-home generator?

Not if the application conforms to the association's adopted hurricane protection specifications. Section 720.3035(6) lists exterior fixed generators and fuel storage tanks as hurricane protection and bars an HOA board or architectural committee from denying a conforming application, though it can still require you to match an existing unified look.

Do I need a permit for a generator in Estero, FL?

Yes. The Village of Estero's "Do I Need a Permit" brochure lists new and replacement generators as permitted work, and its generator checklist requires a scaled site plan, load calculations, and installation above Base Flood Elevation. Homes in unincorporated Lee County permit through Lee County instead.

Can my HOA make me get a building permit before it reviews my plans?

No. Section 720.3035(1)(c) of the Florida Statutes says an association can't require a government-issued building permit as a prerequisite to its review, so the association's review and the permit can run at the same time.

Can an HOA control the color or style of my impact windows?

Yes. The specifications the board adopts may include color, style, and other relevant factors, as long as they comply with the building code. The protection is against denying a conforming application, not against design standards.

Does the same rule apply to Estero condos?

Not exactly. Condo boards must adopt hurricane protection specifications under section 718.113(5) and can't refuse a conforming installation where approval is required, but the condo statute's wording differs from the HOA list and doesn't name exterior generators. Read the declaration and the adopted specs for that building.

The bottom line

In Estero, a generator or impact windows need two yeses — the association's and the building department's. Florida law makes the first one much more predictable than it used to be, but only if you know what the specifications say and what's already on the house before you close.
