Miromar Lakes vs Grandezza, Estero FL: what does $630,000 buy you in each? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 10, 2026·10 min read

Miromar Lakes or Grandezza: what does about $630,000 buy you in each?

At roughly $630,000, both Estero-area clubs will sell you about 2,000 to 2,200 square feet of 2004–2007 construction — a coach home condo at Miromar Lakes or an attached villa at Grandezza. The price and the property tax bill look almost identical. The yearly carrying cost does not: Grandezza adds a mandatory social club membership and has no CDD on Lee County’s district list, while Miromar Lakes stacks a condo fee, a master association fee, and a CDD assessment that’s still paying off bonds through 2035. And despite what you may have read, neither community is a blanket “no flood zone” answer.

This is the comparison we end up drawing on a napkin more than almost any other in Estero. Buyers start at Miromar Lakes because of the beach, notice that Grandezza’s prices start lower, and then ask the right question: if I spend the same money in both places, what’s actually different? Here’s the honest answer, built from current listings, the club’s own fee schedule, the CDD’s audited financials, and FEMA’s live flood map.

Quick snapshot: two clubs a few minutes apart

Miromar Lakes Beach & Golf Club sits east of I-75 near Ben Hill Griffin Parkway and Florida Gulf Coast University, built around a private freshwater lake with a white-sand beach, an Arthur Hills golf course, and a mix of condos, coach homes, villas, and estate homes that runs well into eight figures. It’s still selling some new construction, which is why the developer markets the ability to join golf “from day one” with a new home purchase.

Grandezza sits off Corkscrew Road, a few minutes’ drive away, with roughly 1,000 homes across 12 neighborhoods built between the early and late 2000s. It’s entirely resale. Over the past year, about 56 Grandezza homes sold at an average of $656,266 — roughly 95% of asking — at about $243 a square foot, and homes took an average of 146 days to sell, according to a November 2026 market summary. There were 31 active listings, from $275,000 to about $2.5 million.

So the overlap zone is real but narrow. Grandezza’s average sale is Miromar’s entry point. That’s exactly why the matched-budget comparison is the useful one.

Price and value: the same $630,000, side by side

Here are two homes listed this fall that make the comparison concrete. We’re not recommending either listing — they’re here because they’re nearly the same size, age, and price.

  • Miromar Lakes (San Marino): a coach home condo on Marino Lake Circle — 3 bedrooms plus den, 3 baths, about 2,160 square feet, built in 2007, listed at $629,000. Condo fee $1,800 a quarter, plus a master association fee listed at $1,990 with no billing frequency shown. Estimated taxes about $6,051.
  • Grandezza (Saraceno): a single-level attached villa — 3 bedrooms, 2 baths, about 2,041 square feet, built in 2004, listed at $639,000. Association fee $1,300 (a neighboring Saraceno villa lists $1,250 a quarter). Estimated taxes about $6,031.

Two things jump out. First, the property taxes are within $20 of each other — and both figures reflect the current owner’s bill, so yours will reset after you buy no matter which you choose. Second, the line items underneath are built completely differently.

What the yearly bill is made of at Grandezza

  • Neighborhood association: about $5,000 to $5,200 a year for a Saraceno villa, based on the quarterly figures above.
  • Mandatory social membership: the club’s 2023 schedule set it at $4,092.82 a year and says it’s “mandatory for all Grandezza residents who do not have higher category of membership.” More recent third-party figures put it closer to $5,300, so ask for the current schedule.
  • CDD: no district under Grandezza’s name appears on Lee County’s official list of community development districts. Confirm it on the tax bill for your parcel.

That puts a Grandezza villa at roughly $9,100 to $10,500 a year in association and club costs before golf.

What the yearly bill is made of at Miromar Lakes

  • Condo association: $7,200 a year at $1,800 a quarter.
  • Master association: listed at $1,990. If that’s a quarterly figure, it adds about $7,960 a year — the estoppel letter will settle the frequency.
  • CDD: the Miromar Lakes Community Development District’s fiscal 2024 audit reports an operations and maintenance assessment of $739.42 per unit (up from $620.14 the year before) plus a debt service assessment of $563.45 to $2,372.40 depending on the unit.

Even before the master fee, that’s about $8,500 to $10,300 a year. With a quarterly master fee, the Miromar coach home carries roughly $16,500 to $18,300 a year. In exchange, listings note the beach club is included — the thing most buyers came for.

The CDD piece deserves one more sentence. The district had $13,335,000 in bonds outstanding at September 30, 2024: Series 2022 bonds maturing May 1, 2032, and Series 2015 bonds with a final maturity in May 2035. If you want to know what that means for a specific parcel, our guide on how to read a CDD budget before you buy in Estero shows where to find the numbers.

Same price, same tax bill, nearly the same square footage — and a yearly carrying cost that can differ by several thousand dollars depending on which lines the home sits under.

Amenities head-to-head: a beach versus a club

This is where the extra carrying cost at Miromar Lakes either makes sense to you or it doesn’t.

  • The water: Miromar Lakes is built around a private freshwater lake with a sand beach, boating, and a beach clubhouse. The CDD shares lake-management costs with neighboring Esplanade Lake Club under an interlocal agreement — 71.23% Miromar, 28.77% Esplanade for the fisheries plan. Grandezza has 23 lakes and 41 acres of preserve, but no beach or boating lake.
  • The clubhouse: Grandezza’s 53,000-square-foot clubhouse anchors dining, tennis, bocce, fitness, and a Junior Olympic-size pool, and the mandatory social membership is your ticket in. Several Grandezza neighborhoods also have their own private pools.
  • Golf: optional in both, but priced very differently. Grandezza’s 2023 schedule listed a $55,000 golf initiation and $11,227.83 in annual dues, with membership capped at 400. Miromar’s 2022 developer brochure listed an $80,000 initiation plus Florida sales tax and $13,000 a year; a 2026 third-party estimate puts it at about $140,000 and $16,500. Treat both as starting points and get the current schedule in writing.

Flood zones: the detail most comparisons get wrong

You’ll sometimes see Miromar Lakes described as Flood Zone D with no flood insurance requirement. That isn’t what FEMA’s current National Flood Hazard Layer shows. On November 10, 2026, we checked five street addresses in each community against the live map:

  • Grandezza: all five mapped to Zone X (minimal flood hazard), on a panel effective in 2008.
  • Miromar Lakes: four mapped to Zone X, and one — on Marino Lake Circle — mapped to Zone AE, a high-risk zone where lenders require flood insurance, on a panel revised in November 2022.

That’s a small sample from geocoded street points, not a survey, and it’s not a guarantee for any specific home. What it does show is that the answer is address by address, especially near the water. New FEMA maps for unincorporated Lee County take effect March 2, 2027, too. Our step-by-step guide to checking flood risk in Estero walks through how to look up a specific parcel.

Who each community fits best

Grandezza tends to fit you if you want the most house for the money, you’ll actually use a full-service club for dining and tennis, you’d rather not carry a CDD, and a 146-day average market time sounds like negotiating room rather than a warning sign. Our Grandezza buyer’s guide covers its 12 neighborhoods in detail.

Miromar Lakes tends to fit you if the lake and beach are the reason you’re moving to Southwest Florida, you’re comfortable trading square footage or a higher yearly bill for that lifestyle, and you might want a path into golf without waiting. Our Miromar Lakes guide covers its neighborhoods and price tiers.

Buying process differences

At Grandezza, every purchase is a resale on the Florida As-Is contract, and your due diligence runs on two tracks: the neighborhood association’s estoppel letter and a separate check on the club account through the club’s management.

At Miromar Lakes, you may be choosing between a resale and new construction from the developer. A resale condo means three layers to verify — condo association, master association, and the CDD line on the tax bill — plus the condo’s own building records. A new home means a developer contract, where your own agent matters even more.

Our recommendation

If you’re genuinely torn, don’t compare list prices. Compare the all-in yearly number for the two specific homes you’d buy, then ask yourself whether the beach is worth the difference to you. For a lot of buyers, it is. For others, Grandezza’s extra space and simpler bill win easily.

What would we look at first if you called us about these two?

We’d start by putting both homes on one page — not the advertised dues, but every recurring line that will hit you in year one, including the CDD figure for that exact parcel and the club charges. Then we’d run the flood zone for each address ourselves, because in a community with a lake, two homes a street apart can land on different sides of the line, and that changes your insurance and your resale story.

At Miromar Lakes, we’d flag which association bills which fee and whether any one-time club or capital charge applies at closing. At Grandezza, we’d confirm the current social membership amount and, if you golf, where the 400-member cap stands before you write an offer. With homes averaging nearly five months on market there, we’d also look hard at how long the specific villa has been listed.

Handled well, you walk into the offer knowing your real number in both places and choosing on lifestyle, not surprises. That’s the part we take care of for you — we’ve been doing it in Southwest Florida since 2005.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Miromar Lakes more expensive to live in than Grandezza?

At a matched price of about $630,000, usually yes on a yearly basis. A Miromar Lakes coach home condo carries a condo fee, a master association fee, and a CDD assessment, while a Grandezza villa carries a neighborhood fee and mandatory social club dues with no CDD on Lee County’s district list. Compare the exact homes, since fees vary by neighborhood.

Does Grandezza have a CDD fee?

No district under Grandezza’s name appears on Lee County’s official list of community development districts. Because CDD status is parcel-specific, confirm it on the tax bill for the address you’re considering.

Is Miromar Lakes in a flood zone?

Parts of it can be. When we checked five addresses against FEMA’s live map in November 2026, four were Zone X and one near the lake was Zone AE. It isn’t a blanket Zone D community, so check the specific parcel before you rely on a no-flood-insurance assumption.

Is club membership mandatory at Grandezza and Miromar Lakes?

At Grandezza, social membership is mandatory for residents who don’t hold a higher category, and golf is optional and capped at 400 members. At Miromar Lakes, golf is optional and listings note beach club access is included. Ask for each club’s current written fee schedule before you offer.

When do Miromar Lakes’ CDD bonds pay off?

According to the district’s fiscal 2024 audit, its Series 2022 bonds mature May 1, 2032, and its Series 2015 bonds have a final maturity in May 2035. The operations and maintenance portion of the assessment continues after the debt is retired.
