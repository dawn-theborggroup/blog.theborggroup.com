Moving to Bonita Springs, FL: the 2026 complete relocation guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 29, 2026·9 min read

Bonita Springs is a coastal city of approximately 55,000 residents in southern Lee County, positioned between Naples to the south and Estero to the north. The median home price is approximately $585,000 as of mid-2026, and the market is firmly in buyers' favor — homes are averaging 75+ days on market and 37% of active listings have carried price reductions. Out-of-state relocators should budget for Florida-specific carrying costs: homeowners insurance ($4,000-$12,000+/yr depending on age, construction type, and flood zone), potential flood insurance in FEMA AE or VE zones near the coast, property taxes of approximately 1.0-1.5% of assessed value on non-homesteaded properties, and HOA and CDD fees that vary widely by community — from $0 in San Carlos Estates to $12,000+/yr in Bonita Bay.

If you've been researching Southwest Florida, you've probably landed on Naples, Estero, and Bonita Springs as your top candidates. You've Googled the beaches, looked at Zillow, and tried to figure out why some HOA fees are $200 a month and others are $2,000. This guide gives you the straightforward picture.

Communities: where do people live in Bonita Springs?

Bonita Springs is not a single neighborhood; it's a city of about 55,000 with meaningful variety. Here's a practical orientation for relocators.

Pelican Landing

WCI Communities' flagship Bonita Springs masterplan, developed starting in 1990. About 3,300 homes across 2,258 acres on both sides of US-41. Single-family homes, condos, and villas from roughly $400,000 to $2M+. Amenities include a private 34-acre barrier island beach park with a community boat shuttle, 12 Har-Tru tennis courts, pickleball, and a kayak and canoe park. No CDD. Golf is available at adjacent Pelican's Nest (optional). One of the most established communities in the market — the variety of product types means you can find a villa at $500K or a large estate home at $1.8M within the same gates.

Bonita Bay

One of Southwest Florida's most complete private communities — 2,800 homes across 2,400+ acres with five 18-hole golf courses (90 holes total), a full-service private marina on Estero Bay, and a private beach pavilion on Bonita Beach. Social membership is mandatory for all owners (approximately $90,000 initiation, $12,930/yr dues). Golf membership is optional with a multi-year waitlist. If you want the full country club lifestyle at the Gulf Coast's highest level, this is the community. No CDD.

San Carlos Estates

Bonita Springs' anti-HOA enclave. AG-2 (Agricultural-2) zoning, approximately 472 homesites, lots ranging from one to five-plus acres. No HOA, no CDD. Boats, RVs, horses, and livestock are permitted on property. Prices run $700,000-$1,500,000+ for the right parcel. A specific type of buyer loves it — those who want land, privacy, and freedom from association governance. It's unlike anything else in this immediate market.

Vivid Shores

The newest development in Bonita Springs. STOCK Development and Pulte are building a lakefront new-construction community near Imperial Parkway with boatable lakes (motor boats permitted). If you want new construction in Bonita Springs proper rather than Estero, this is currently the primary option. Prices start in the $700,000s. As with all new construction, bring an independent buyer's agent — the on-site sales team represents the builder, and having your own representation costs you nothing while giving you a skilled negotiator on incentives and upgrades.

Valencia Bonita

GL Homes' 55+ active adult community on Imperial Parkway, with an exceptional amenity center and resort-style clubhouse.

If you're buying in a newer community with CDD fees, understand that those fees appear on your Lee County property tax bill, not your HOA statement. They represent your share of the community's infrastructure bond (roads, utilities, common areas) and typically run 20-30 years before the bond is paid off.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bonita Springs a good place to retire?

Yes — Bonita Springs consistently ranks among the best retirement destinations in Southwest Florida for buyers who want a balance of lifestyle, value, and access. It offers Gulf beaches, no state income tax, a coastal feel without the full price premium of Naples, and a wide range of communities from modest condos to multi-million-dollar estate enclaves. Retirees who want the most luxury tend toward Naples; those who want more value, less density, and more flexibility often prefer Bonita Springs.

How much does it cost to live in Bonita Springs, FL?

Housing averages around $585,000 for a single-family home as of mid-2026, with condos available under $400,000. Monthly carrying costs beyond your mortgage include homeowners insurance ($350-$800/mo), property taxes (roughly $600-$1,000/mo non-homesteaded on a $700K home), and HOA fees ($150-$600/mo depending on community). Plan for $1,500-$2,500/mo in non-mortgage costs in most gated communities, and budget more if you're looking at a property with a CDD or a community with club membership requirements.

Does Bonita Springs have good beaches?

Yes. Bonita Beach Park is a wide, accessible public Gulf beach with free parking, picnic facilities, and boat ramps. Lovers Key State Park — on a barrier island at the city's edge — offers kayaking, canoeing, and pristine beaches with almost no commercial development. Barefoot Beach Preserve County Park, just north of Bonita Beach, is consistently rated one of the best beaches in the United States. Several private communities like Pelican Landing and Bonita Bay also offer exclusive beach club access for residents.

What is the traffic like in Bonita Springs?

Traffic on US-41 and the I-75/Corkscrew Road corridor is heavy during the winter season (December-April), particularly in the morning and late afternoon. The stretch of US-41 through central Bonita Springs is the area's most commonly cited frustration among residents. Most gated communities are positioned off the main corridors, so daily life within a community tends to be fine; the challenge is access to and from US-41 during peak hours.

What is the hurricane risk in Bonita Springs?

Bonita Springs received a direct hit from Hurricane Ian (September 2022), which caused significant flooding in low-lying areas near Estero Bay and along Imperial River. Many gated communities east of US-41 and I-75 were largely unaffected; areas closer to the coastline and tidal waterways sustained the most damage. Properties rebuilt or remediated since Ian are generally in better structural condition than older unimproved inventory. Understanding a specific property's flood zone designation, insurance costs, and post-Ian history is essential before making an offer.
