Is the Naples housing market balanced right now? What the September 2026 numbers say

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 4, 2026·7 min read

Yes, the Naples housing market looks balanced right now, and the September 2026 numbers are why. Closed sales rose 2.5% from last September to 623, while the number of homes for sale fell 17.3% to 4,240. The median closed price was $575,000, up 4.5% from last year but exactly where it was in August. Naples now has 5.5 months of supply, down from 7.8 a year ago, and homes sold in an average of 99 days. In plain terms, last year's extra inventory has been absorbed, prices are holding, and buyers who need a mortgage are being more careful.

Why is inventory down 17% when new listings are up?

Both statements are true at once. Sellers listed 1,073 homes in September, up 4.8% from last September and up 35% from August's 792. Even so, total inventory is 17.3% lower than a year ago, because the extra supply built up in 2025 has been absorbed. From August to September, inventory rose only 3.6%, from 4,093 to 4,240.

Year to date, new listings are still down about 8%. The NABOR report suggests that may mean many sellers are choosing their moment carefully. Sales are up 15.1% for the year, and pending sales are up 19%, so there is real demand behind the lower inventory.

If inventory is down, why aren't prices going up?

That is the question the appraisers are asking too. Cindy Carroll of Carroll & Carroll Appraisers & Consultants said in the report that inventory is tight in many parts of Collier County, but she is not seeing values go up yet. She noted that properties doubled in value after the pandemic and suggested the market may have seen the top of this cycle, especially if new listings keep rising.

The averages hide a split. The single-family median price rose 3.1% to $747,500, while the condo median fell 2.4% to $400,000. Mike Hughes, Vice President and General Manager of Downing-Frye Realty, also pointed out that the luxury end has had a strong year, with an average September sales price of $1.2 million compared with roughly $500,000 before COVID.

Less inventory, steady prices and slower financed buyers is not a runaway market and it is not a stalled one. It is a market where pricing and timing decide the outcome.

How are mortgage rates changing who buys?

Hughes said rates were in the high 5s in February and are now over 7%. He added that the Naples market is seeing a lot of cash deals, but not because there are more cash buyers: there are fewer buyers financing a purchase. Jennifer Reedy of John R Wood Properties said that for a family moving up in home size, price is not the obstacle. The rate is, especially if it means giving up a current low rate.

That shows up in the pending sales number. September pending sales fell 6.8% from last September to 702, even though pending sales are still up 19% for the year.

Where is the market tightest, and where is it looser?

It depends on the price range and the type of home. Under $300,000, inventory actually grew 3.5% to 555 properties, and 470 of those are condominiums. Hughes noted that about 84% of the active listings in that range are condos, so entry-level buyers who want a single-family home have limited choices.

New construction is the other pressure point. Mike Bone of Christopher Alan Homes said many national builders are offering $30,000 to $50,000 in concessions, paying closing costs, or buying rates down as low as 5%, and that builders are cutting starts by roughly 50% in the area. For resale sellers, those incentives are competition, and it is worth knowing what a new-build neighbor is offering before you set your price.

What does this mean if you're buying in Naples?

You have more choices than a year ago in most price ranges, homes are taking a little less time to sell, and the market is not forcing bidding wars. The trade-off is your financing: with rates over 7%, the monthly payment matters more than the list price for many buyers. Hughes expects seasonal cash buyers to lift sales as winter approaches, so the calmer conditions may not last through the season.

What does this mean if you're selling in Naples?

Sherry Stein of Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Florida Realty said in the report that sellers who are positioned correctly, with homes priced right, are selling quickly. Pricing for the market that exists today, rather than the one from a year or two ago, is what separates the quick sales from the long ones.

How would we use these numbers if you called us?

We would start by taking the countywide headline off the table. A median price does not tell you what your home, or the one you want, will do. We would look at your price band and your home type, because condos and single-family homes are moving in opposite directions. We would check what new-construction builders near you are offering in concessions, since that sets the bar for a resale buyer. For a buyer, we would also look at how the payment changes at different rates, because that often matters more than a few thousand dollars on the price. For a seller, we would look at what is actually under contract in your neighborhood, not just what is listed. That is the work that happens before an offer or a listing goes out, and it is the part a monthly report cannot do for you.

What these numbers do not tell you

The NABOR report covers Naples and Collier County, excluding Marco Island. Bonita Springs and Estero sit mostly in Lee County and are reported separately, and individual communities and price bands can look very different from the county average. The comments quoted here are the opinions of the brokers and appraisers named in the report. This is general information, not financial or legal advice. Frequently asked questions

Is the Naples housing market a buyer's market or a seller's market right now?

On the September 2026 numbers, it sits in the middle. Naples has 5.5 months of supply, down from 7.8 a year ago, and homes sold in an average of 99 days. The Naples Area Board of REALTORS® described the market as finding its balance: sales are up 2.5%, inventory is down 17.3%, and the median price of $575,000 is up 4.5% from last September but unchanged from August.

Why is Naples inventory down if new listings are up?

Both are true. New listings rose 4.8% compared with September 2025 and jumped 35% from August, but inventory is still 17.3% lower than a year ago because last year's extra supply has been absorbed. New listings are still down about 8% for the year so far, which may mean many sellers are choosing their timing carefully.

Are mortgage rates slowing down Naples home sales?

Brokers quoted in the September report say yes for buyers who need a mortgage. Mike Hughes of Downing-Frye Realty said rates were in the high 5s in February and are now over 7%, and that many of the cash deals reflect fewer financed buyers rather than more cash buyers. Pending sales fell 6.8% from last September, though they are up 19% for the year so far.

Sources: Naples Area Board of REALTORS® (NABOR) September 2026 Market Report, released October 23, 2026; NaplesArea.com.
