WildBlue in Estero, FL: what buyers need to know in 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·July 2, 2026·9 min read

WildBlue is a master-planned gated community in Estero built around more than 800 acres of navigable freshwater lakes, where residents can powerboat, jet ski, wakeboard, and launch boats from private docks — a lifestyle no other community on the Corkscrew Road corridor can match. Spread across 3,100+ acres with roughly 1,000–1,100 single-family homes built by Lennar, Pulte, WCI, and Stock Development, WildBlue prices from the $700s up to $4 million-plus for luxury estate homes in Vista WildBlue. It also carries both HOA fees (roughly $500–$667/month) and CDD assessments ($227–$440/month) — total community fees that belong in your cost-of-ownership math from day one.

Every time a buyer asks us about Estero's Corkscrew corridor, WildBlue comes up first. Once they understand what makes it different from every other gated community in the area, it's easy to see why.

The lake life here is genuinely different

In most Southwest Florida communities, "lakefront" means a scenic view from your lanai. At WildBlue, it means backing a powerboat out of your own dock onto 800 acres of freshwater. Most inland gated communities on Corkscrew Road restrict their lakes to electric motors — Corkscrew Shores, for example, has a beautiful 240-acre lake but limits residents to kayaks, paddleboards, canoes, and small electric boats, no gas engines or jet skis. At WildBlue, two of the three interconnected lakes — WildBlue Lake and Vista Lake — allow jet skis and wakeboarding, and a private dock can be installed behind your home depending on your lot's lake frontage and the HOA's permitting process.

The amenity package matches the lifestyle: a resort-style pool and lap pool, a lakeside beach with cabanas, a full-service restaurant and bar overlooking the water, a spa with salon services, a fitness center, tennis and pickleball courts, bocce, basketball, boat launches, and a marina, all anchored by a 13,000-square-foot clubhouse.

Verdana Village has exceptional sports and dining amenities, but no boating. WildBlue has resort-level amenities plus lake access that lets you do things most Florida homeowners drive to the coast for.

What WildBlue actually costs — the full breakdown

This is where buyers sometimes get surprised, and where we spend real time with clients before they make an offer. WildBlue has both a CDD and an HOA — two separate fee structures, and you'll pay both.

CDD fees cover community-wide infrastructure: shoreline maintenance, seawall repair, aquatic management, conservation area upkeep, and capital debt repayment. The FY2026 CDD budget runs from about $2,723/year on a 52-foot homesite to $5,283/year on a 140-foot homesite — roughly $227 to $440/month, billed through your Lee County property tax bill.

HOA fees cover amenities, common area maintenance, and deed restriction enforcement, typically $6,000–$8,000/year (about $500–$667/month), billed quarterly.

Combined, that's $727 to over $1,100 per month in community fees alone — before your mortgage, insurance, or property taxes. A buyer who qualifies for a $1.5M purchase has often not accounted for an extra $1,000+/month sitting outside the mortgage payment entirely.

For comparison: Corkscrew Shores carries zero CDD fees, a potential $2,700–$5,300/year savings at today's assessment rates. Whether that outweighs WildBlue's boating access is a personal call — but it's one that needs real numbers behind it, not a guess.

What to know before you buy at WildBlue

Builders and price tiers

Four builders are active in WildBlue. Lennar and Pulte offer production-style single-family homes, generally pricing from the $700s into the low $1M range. WCI and Stock Development build semi-custom and custom estate homes at higher price points — Stock Custom Homes has also introduced new estate homes within WildBlue targeting $3M+ buyers.

Vista WildBlue

Vista WildBlue is a luxury sub-community within the larger development — an enclave of estate homes with elevated finishes, larger lots, and direct frontage on Vista Lake, carrying a meaningful price premium and its own HOA structure. Most homes priced $2M and above within WildBlue sit in Vista WildBlue specifically.

Dock access and permitting

If a private dock is non-negotiable, verify it before you go under contract, not after. Dock feasibility depends on your specific lot's lake frontage, phase, and HOA dock rules — some lake-view lots are set back in ways that affect it. Confirm dock eligibility as part of due diligence before making an offer.

New construction vs. resale

WildBlue is still actively building, so new inventory exists but fluctuates by builder, phase, and lot type. New construction often comes with builder incentives; resale offers a faster move-in and lets you see the finished product before buying. We track current availability across all four builders, including inventory not yet listed on the MLS.

Location

WildBlue sits east of I-75 on Corkscrew Road in Estero — about 20 minutes from RSW International Airport, 10 minutes from Miromar Outlets and Gulf Coast Town Center, and roughly 20–25 minutes from Bonita Beach and the Gulf. It's an inland community built around the lake lifestyle, not a beachfront address — if you want to walk to the beach, this isn't the right fit; if a resort-style lake setting with a short coastal drive works for you, it delivers extremely well.
