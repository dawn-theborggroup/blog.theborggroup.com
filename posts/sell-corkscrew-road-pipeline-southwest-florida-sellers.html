Seller Tips

Should you sell on Corkscrew Road before 14,500 more homes arrive? Southwest Florida 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 29, 2026·8 min read

Should you sell in the Corkscrew Road corridor before the approved homes get built?

If your plans already point to a sale inside the next three or four years, the case for going sooner is stronger than the market report alone suggests. Roughly 14,500 homes are already approved east of Estero's finished communities and mostly unbuilt — about 10,000 at Kingston in Lee County and up to 4,502 at Corkscrew Grove East Village in Collier, with a second Corkscrew Grove village of similar size planned on top of that. Meanwhile the road that carries all of it is on a slower clock than the houses are. Inventory in Estero is unusually tight right now. That combination does not last.

This is not a prediction about prices. It is a point about what you will be competing against, and when.

The market you're selling into today

Estero's resale market is the tightest it has been in a couple of years, and the figures depend on whose cut of the data you read — so here is the spread, with each source's vintage, rather than one confident number:

  • Estero, August 2026: 441 active listings, down 32.5% from 653 a year earlier; 3.5 months of supply against 6.3; median sold price $517,500, up 6.2%; median 63 days on market, down from 112; 90 closed sales (Worthington Realty, from Florida Gulf Coast MLS data pulled September 1, 2026).
  • Estero single-family, September 2026: median $650,000, 238 active single-family listings, 3.21 months of supply, 61 closed sales, 60 median days on market, 96.97% of list (The Ellis Team's Estero market hub, updated October 10, 2026).
  • Estero, July 2026: 469 homes for sale, down 29.7%; 5.0 months of supply; 95 closed sales (Bonita Estero REALTORS®).
  • Lee County, September 2026: single-family median $375,500, up 0.1%; 6,517 active listings, down 13.7%; 5.2 months of supply against 6.7; median 58 days to contract and 92 days to sale (Florida Realtors).

They disagree because they measure different things — all-residential versus single-family-only, city versus county, one month versus trailing twelve. Do not treat any of them as your price. What they agree on is the direction: Estero inventory is down roughly 30% year over year, and months of supply has fallen from about six to somewhere between three and five. That is a seller's position, and it is a seller's position right now.

What is actually approved east of you

The communities most people picture when they think "Corkscrew Road" are largely done. The Place at Corkscrew tops out at 1,325 homes. Verdana Village was announced in May 2021 as a 2,400-home Cameratta Companies community built by Lennar and Pulte, with roughly 100,000 square feet of Publix-anchored commercial space. Known quantities.

What is coming is a different order of magnitude:

  • Kingston — roughly 10,000 homes at build-out, five builders, ground broken November 2025, model homes opening in late 2026 and first residents expected in early 2027. A traffic signal at Corkscrew Road and Spine Road was scheduled to be operational by fall 2026.
  • Corkscrew Grove East Village — up to 4,502 homes approved. Collier County commissioners voted 5–0 on April 28, 2026, including 362 affordable homes, at least 10% multi-family, and senior housing capped at 300 units. Developer Alico Inc. has said construction could begin in 2028 or 2029, and nothing can start until South Florida Water Management District and Army Corps of Engineers permits are issued.
  • Corkscrew Grove West Village — a second village of similar size, planned. Together the two would total roughly 9,000 homes. The independent district that will build and finance the infrastructure was created by House Bill 4041, signed June 23.

Note what that timing means. These homes are not competing with your listing in 2026. They are competing with it in 2028 through the early 2030s — and when they do, they will arrive as brand-new product with builder rate buydowns and closing-cost incentives attached, which is a harder thing to price against than another resale down the street.

The road is on a different clock than the houses

Here is the part almost nobody checks before deciding when to sell.

Corkscrew Road is four lanes from US 41 east through the Estero commercial core, then narrows. Phase 1, Ben Hill Griffin Parkway to Bella Terra Boulevard, is complete. Phase 2 — which Lee County's own Roadwatch report for August 20–26, 2026 describes as constructing "four, 11-foot-wide travel lanes from Bella Terra Boulevard to just east of Alico Road" — put completion "at the end of 2026," having slipped from fall 2026 because of Lee County Utilities and FPL power-line relocation work.

Alico Road meets Corkscrew about seven miles east of US 41. East of that junction, Corkscrew Road stays two lanes — and that is the stretch Kingston, Corkscrew Grove, and the far end of Verdana Village feed onto.

The adopted Lee County MPO 2050 Long Range Transportation Plan, adopted December 12, 2025, carries the next two Corkscrew Road widenings on its roadway needs list:

  • US 41 to the Verdana Village entrance — widen four to six lanes, $273.70 million in 2025 present-day cost, needs-list priority rank 10.
  • Verdana Village entrance to the Collier County line — widen two to four lanes, $97.21 million, needs-list priority rank 34.

The same needs list carries Kingston Ranch Road as a new four-lane connection from Corkscrew Road north to SR 82 at $109.40 million, plus a $44.80 million I-75 interchange improvement at Corkscrew Road.

Two honest caveats. First, we could not read the plan's cost-feasible roadway tables in the published PDFs, so we are reporting what the needs list shows and not claiming either segment is formally unfunded — the plan's own cost-feasible tables, and Lee County MPO staff, settle that. Second, the "2031" start date that circulates for the Alico-to-Verdana segment traces to a county schedule published in a March 2023 magazine piece listing Phase 3 as starting 2031 and finishing 2035, with a 2026 local page repeating roughly the same date. Reported, dated, not primary.

What is primary is Lee County's own framing on its Corkscrew Road Improvement Study pages: four-laning Corkscrew Road sits "currently as a Tier Two priority," and a widening project "typically takes five to ten years, depending on permitting requirements." Phase 2 alone ran from early 2024 to the end of 2026 — local proof of that range.

What that does to your listing window

Put the two calendars side by side and the seller's question answers itself.

2026 into 2027: tight inventory, three to five months of supply, Phase 2 finishing. Your buyer's drive out to see you is about to get better, and the nearest big delivery of competing new homes is still a year or two out. This is the favorable half of the window.

2028 into the early 2030s: Kingston delivering at scale, Corkscrew Grove potentially breaking ground, and the two-lane stretch east of Alico Road still waiting on a widening the county itself describes as a five-to-ten-year undertaking.

If you plan to hold for fifteen years, none of this is your problem. If you are already thinking about a move in the next three or four years, you are looking at a window that narrows rather than widens, and season timing is the smaller of the two decisions in front of you.

Four mistakes we see sellers make in this corridor

  1. Pricing off the newest community's sticker. A brand-new home's price is not your comp if it comes with a rate buydown and a closing-cost credit. The effective price is lower than the list price, and buyers do that arithmetic. Our piece on competing with new construction as a resale seller covers how we handle that gap.
  2. Treating the road as a selling point without a date. "They're widening Corkscrew" is true of the segment finishing at the end of 2026 and not true of the segment past Alico Road. A buyer who checks and finds the difference stops trusting everything else in your listing.
  3. Leaving CDD out of the comparison. Your buyer compares total monthly cost, not sale price. Verdana Village sits inside the V-Dana Community Development District created by Lee County Ordinance 20-03, and Corkscrew Shores materials state there is no CDD. That difference is worth documenting; we break the numbers down in our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero.
  4. Waiting for the market report to tell you to move. Monthly stats describe the last thirty days. The thing that will shape this corridor over the next five years is already on paper in approval documents and a transportation plan, and none of it will show up in a months-of-supply figure until it has already happened.

How we market a listing in this corridor

Our approach is to put the corridor story in the listing file rather than leave it to the buyer's imagination. A buyer touring an east Estero resale has usually just come from a model home, carrying two unspoken questions: what is going to get built around here, and how bad is the road going to get. A listing that answers both with sourced, dated facts stops competing on price alone. One that goes quiet hands the buyer a reason to discount.

What would we look at first if you called us about your Corkscrew Road home?

Your position relative to Alico Road, before anything else — because that single fact decides which widening segment you sit on, which approved communities will be delivering between you and I-75, and whether the corridor story in your listing is a tailwind or something we need to get in front of. Then your community's own build-out status, since a community still being delivered by a builder and a finished community are two completely different selling situations even on the same road.

From there we're looking at what your real competition will be during your actual listing window, not today's active list. That means which builder phases are releasing nearby, what incentives they are carrying, and how your total monthly cost — HOA, CDD, insurance — reads next to a new home's. We'd also check your municipality, because Village of Estero and unincorporated Lee are not the same jurisdiction for taxes or flood-rating purposes; we wrote up how to check which side of the Village line a home sits on for exactly that reason. And if your buyer pool is families, the Estero school enrollment picture is part of the corridor conversation too.

What we are not doing is handing you a spreadsheet and a list of phone numbers. Reading the pipeline, deciding which parts of it belong in your marketing and which parts belong in a conversation with a buyer's agent, and sequencing a listing against a window that is genuinely time-sensitive — that is the part that goes wrong when it is improvised. That's the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

How many homes are approved along Corkscrew Road east of Estero?
Roughly 14,500, and mostly unbuilt. Kingston is planned for about 10,000 at build-out; Collier commissioners approved Corkscrew Grove East Village for up to 4,502 homes on April 28, 2026. A West Village of similar size is planned, which would take Corkscrew Grove to roughly 9,000 on its own.

Is Corkscrew Road being widened past Alico Road?
Not yet. Phase 2 runs from Bella Terra Boulevard to just east of Alico Road and Lee County put completion at the end of 2026. The next two segments appear on the adopted MPO 2050 plan's roadway needs list at $273.70 million and $97.21 million, with priority ranks 10 and 34.

Should I wait for the road to be widened before I sell?
That is a long wait to plan a sale around. Lee County describes four-laning Corkscrew Road as a Tier Two priority and notes that a widening project typically takes five to ten years depending on permitting. Phase 2 alone took from early 2024 to the end of 2026.

Will the new construction east of me hurt my resale price?
It changes what you compete against more than it sets your price. Today's Estero market is tight — 441 active listings in August 2026, down 32.5%, with 3.5 months of supply. The risk is a listing window that overlaps the 2028-onward delivery period, not this year.

Is the corridor still a good long-term hold?
For many owners, yes — we've written about why the Corkscrew Road corridor looks like a long-term growth play. The timing question in this post is about sellers with a three-to-four-year horizon, which is a different question from a fifteen-year one.

Where to start

Find out exactly where your home sits relative to Alico Road and what is approved between you and I-75. Those two facts, plus your own timeline, decide whether this is a 2027 listing or a 2032 one — and the answer differs for a home in Bella Terra versus one in Verdana Village, even though both have a Corkscrew Road address.
