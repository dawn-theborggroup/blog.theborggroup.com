Moving to Naples, FL: the 2026 relocation guide for Northeast and Midwest buyers

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 17, 2026·10 min read

If you are moving to Naples, FL from the Northeast or Midwest in 2026, you are arriving in a market that has loosened considerably. In August 2026, the median closed price across Collier County excluding Marco Island was $575,000, down 1.7% year over year — single-family at $717,500, condos at $422,500 — with active inventory down 20.7% to 4,164 listings and months of supply down to 5.4 from 8.1. But the figure that may matter most this year is not a price. It is a date: December 31, 2026. If Florida voters approve Amendment 3 on November 3, when you become a permanent Florida resident could change your property tax bill for four years.

We walk clients through this move constantly, and the questions are always the same three: what does the house cost, what does it cost to keep, and what will I get wrong because I have never bought in Florida before.

Why Southwest Florida, and why Naples specifically

The headline reasons are real. Florida has no state personal income tax — no tax on wages, pension distributions, IRA withdrawals, or capital gains. Sales tax is 6%, and winter days run in the seventies and low eighties. If you are coming from a state that taxes retirement income, the arithmetic is often decisive on its own.

Naples is where that trade comes with the most polish — Gulf beaches, Fifth Avenue South and Third Street South, Naples Botanical Garden, roughly ninety golf courses countywide, and a downtown that works year-round. It is also the higher end of the region: the same budget buys more square footage in Fort Myers or Cape Coral.

What is different in 2026 is the negotiating position. Collier County closed 8,249 sales in 2025 at an average 94.2% of list price. In August 2026, average days on market was 99 countywide — 92 for single-family, 108 for condos. A home that has sat three months is a home whose seller will talk. Meanwhile the 30-year fixed mortgage averaged 7.40% in Freddie Mac's October 15, 2026 survey. Rates are doing the opposite of helping, which is exactly why seller flexibility matters.

What your money buys: Naples neighborhoods and communities

Naples is not one market. It is a dozen, and the difference between them runs mostly west-to-east.

Coastal and in-town. Old Naples, the Moorings, Coquina Sands, Park Shore, and Pelican Bay put you within walking or short driving distance of the beach and downtown — top of the price range, tightest inventory. Many condo buildings here are mid-century, which brings Florida's milestone inspection and structural integrity reserve study requirements into play — ask for those documents early.

North Naples. The 34109, 34110, and 34119 corridors along Immokalee Road and Livingston Road: gated communities with resort amenities, newer construction, and quick access to I-75 at exits 111 and 116.

East and South Naples. Where the budget stretches. Our guides to Naples Reserve, Azure at Hacienda Lakes, and Compass Landing cover three versions of it — a lagoon resort community, a Toll Brothers enclave inside a large CDD, and a sold-out Mattamy neighborhood now trading on resale. Golden Gate Estates is the acreage option. Ave Maria, thirty minutes inland, is the county's lowest cost of entry for new construction; the trade is the drive.

For the full county picture before you narrow anything down, start with our Naples buyer guide and the community pages at communities.theborggroup.com/naples.

The mistake is not picking the wrong community. The mistake is picking a community before you have priced what it costs to own there — because in Collier County, two homes at the same purchase price can carry annual costs that differ by five figures.

The real cost of living: taxes, insurance, HOA and CDD

Naples runs about 5% above the national average on cost of living and about 6% above on household bills, per Salary.com and Doxo figures from June 2026; food is 14% below. Monthly utilities land near $103 for electric, $83 for water and sewer, and $91.50 for internet. Average rent was $1,873 a month in July 2026.

The costs that surprise people are the five below.

Property taxes. Collier County held its millage flat for fiscal 2026-27: 3.0107 mills for the county general fund, 0.0246 for Pollution Control, 0.2096 for Conservation Collier, plus 0.6844 for the unincorporated area general fund. Countywide taxable value rose from $164.5 billion to a gross $170.3 billion, up 3.5%. The mechanic that matters for a buyer is Save Our Homes: the 3% assessment cap that protected the seller resets to what you paid. Never budget from the current owner's bill — yours will almost always be higher.

The homestead exemption, and that December 31 date. In 2026 Florida's homestead exemption is up to $51,411 — $25,000 against all millages plus up to $26,411 against non-school millages. Amendment 3, on the November 3 ballot and needing 60% approval, would raise the non-school exemption to up to $150,000 from January 1, 2027 and up to $250,000 from January 1, 2028, leaving the school portion at $25,000. If it passes, the timing rule is what should move your calendar: permanent Florida residents as of December 31, 2026 would be eligible for the larger amount starting in 2027, and would keep that eligibility even if they buy later. Establish residency on or after January 1, 2027 and you would start at a $50,000 non-school exemption, reaching the full amount only on January 1 of the fifth year. Our Florida residency and domicile guide walks through the steps. Verify your situation with the Collier County Property Appraiser and a CPA — and note that nothing changes unless voters approve it.

Insurance. Collier County's average Citizens premium is about $3,328 a year, down 4.1% from $3,470 — seventh among Florida's 67 counties. Citizens rates for policies written or renewed on or after July 1, 2026 carry statewide average decreases of 8.8% for multiperil and 5.1% for wind-only. Private carriers vary far more than that average suggests. A wind mitigation inspection runs $100 to $150, and the credits commonly cut the wind portion of a premium by 15% to 40%. Pre-2000 homes generally need a four-point inspection too.

Flood, separately. Flood is never covered by a homeowners policy. Collier County's mapped zones include VE, AE, X500, and X on a Digital Flood Insurance Rate Map effective February 8, 2024, and the county's Community Rating System Class 5 rating earns a 25% discount on eligible NFIP premiums. An X-zone policy often runs a few hundred dollars a year; AE and VE cost materially more. Expect a 30-day waiting period before coverage begins, and know that in VE, AE, AH, and A zones the 50% rule can force a renovation into full compliance with current elevation requirements. The county's map viewer is at collier.gov/floodmap; our guide to flood zones worth avoiding versus buying goes deeper.

HOA and CDD. Collier County HOA dues run from a few hundred dollars a year in older non-gated neighborhoods to well over $10,000 in bundled-golf and full-amenity communities. On top of that, many newer master-planned communities carry a Community Development District assessment on the tax bill — a separate line item from the HOA that repays infrastructure bonds. Our CDD fee guide explains how to find that number before you write an offer, and our true cost of living breakdown is a useful regional companion.

Impact fees, on new construction. Collier County's residential impact fees effective May 1, 2026 total $23,763.97 per single-family detached unit under 4,000 square feet of living area and $26,535.95 at 4,000 and above, excluding fire district fees. Builders normally carry these in the base price — confirm it in writing.

Schools, healthcare, and getting back north

Collier County Public Schools earned an "A" district grade from the Florida Department of Education for the 2025-26 school year, announced July 1, 2026 — the district's ninth consecutive year at that grade, and one of only five Florida districts to hold it across all nine. Across the district, 51 of 52 traditional public schools were rated "A" or "B." Assignment is by attendance zone, and zones get redrawn as the county grows east — verify the current assignment for a specific address with the district's own school locator, not a listing description.

Healthcare is a genuine strength, and a common reason the move holds up long-term. NCH Healthcare System operates the main hospital campuses in Naples and has been recognized in national quality rankings; Physicians Regional Healthcare System runs additional campuses in the county. For specialty care, Fort Myers and Lee Health are forty minutes north.

Getting back north is easier than most people expect. Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW) in Fort Myers is roughly thirty to forty-five minutes away, with nonstop service to much of the Northeast corridor and the Midwest. Book January through March well ahead.

What to expect as an out-of-state buyer

Closings run through a title company, not an attorney. The title company handles escrow, title search, title insurance, and the closing. You can involve an attorney, and on complex purchases we sometimes recommend it, but it is not the default. Doc stamps on the deed run $0.70 per $100 of sale price and are customarily the seller's cost here.

You will see a Seller's Property Disclosure, not a Northeast-style disclosure package. Read the prior-flood-claim, roof-age, and drainage sections carefully, and treat a sparse disclosure as a reason to inspect harder.

HOA approval and estoppel letters are real steps on the timeline. Many Collier communities require association approval of the buyer, and the estoppel letter — the association's statement of what is owed on the unit — carries a statutory delivery window and a fee. Build both into your closing schedule.

Order insurance quotes before you waive contingencies. This is the most common sequencing error we see from out-of-state buyers. Up north it is a formality handled after inspection. Here it can change whether the deal works at all. Quote the actual address — homeowners and flood both — in week one of the contract.

Your home inspection should be Florida-specific. Roof age and remaining life, four-point items on older homes, wind mitigation features, pool equipment and cage, irrigation, and any sign of water intrusion.

Domicile is not the same as residency for income tax. If you keep a home in New York or New Jersey and spend more than 183 days there, that state can generally still tax you as a resident on worldwide income even after your domicile has changed to Florida. In an audit the burden of proof is yours, and undocumented days can be counted against you — auditors weigh the relative size and value of your two homes, continued business involvement, where your spouse and minor children live, and how you actually spent the year. Start keeping records the month you move, and have this conversation with a CPA licensed in your former state. We are real estate professionals, not tax advisers.

Five mistakes Northeast and Midwest buyers make in Naples

1. Budgeting from the seller's tax bill. Save Our Homes resets at sale. Run the number on your purchase price with the exemptions you will actually qualify for.

2. Buying in season and planning the year around it. Season is the best version of Naples. Visit in August too — the heat, the storms, and the quiet are part of what you are buying.

3. Treating all HOA dues as comparable. Two homes at the same price can differ by well over $10,000 a year once bundled golf, a CDD assessment, and a master-plus-sub association structure are counted.

4. Walking into a builder's sales center unrepresented. The on-site agent works for the builder. Have your own representation in place before the first visit. Our new construction versus resale comparison covers the trade-offs.

5. Letting the residency calendar slip. If Amendment 3 passes, December 31, 2026 is a hard line with a four-year consequence on the other side of it. If your move was already planned for early 2027, it is worth asking now whether moving it up pays for itself.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a buyer's or seller's market in Naples in 2026? Closer to balanced than it has been, and it varies by property type. Condos carry 5.7 months of supply and 108 average days on market, which gives buyers real leverage. Single-family sits tighter at 5.2 months and 92 days. Both leave more negotiating room than the markets most of our clients are leaving.

Should I rent for a season before buying in Naples? For many relocating buyers, yes — it lets you test the commute, the heat, and the community before committing. Average rent was $1,873 a month in July 2026, though January through March prices far above that. The counterargument this year is the December 31, 2026 residency date, which may favor homesteading sooner.

Which is the better value, Naples or Estero and Bonita Springs? It depends what you are optimizing for. Naples buys the downtown, the beaches, and amenity density. Estero and Bonita Springs generally buy more house for the money with a shorter drive to RSW. We have written the same Northeast and Midwest guide for Estero and Bonita Springs; the honest answer comes out of comparing all three side by side.

Naples in late 2026 gives a prepared buyer advantages that did not exist two years ago. The catch is that the cost of owning here is far less uniform than the cost of buying here, and that gap is where out-of-state buyers get hurt. Price the ownership, not just the purchase.
