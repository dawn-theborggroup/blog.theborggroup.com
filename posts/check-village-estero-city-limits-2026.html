How to check if a home is inside the Village of Estero, FL: 2026 buyer's guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 25, 2026·8 min read

Here is the fastest way to find out whether a home is inside the Village of Estero, FL: enter the address in Lee County's Find My Flood Zone map at leegis.leegov.com/FloodZones, click directly on the structure, and read the pop-up. Lee County's own instructions say it plainly — "The municipality should automatically appear as the first item." That one field is the answer. The mailing address is not, the ZIP code is not, and the name the community markets itself under is not.

This catches more Southwest Florida buyers than almost any other piece of local trivia, because the gap here is unusually wide. The Village of Estero only incorporated on December 31, 2014, covering about 25.4 square miles. Developers had been selling homes with Estero mailing addresses for decades before that, and the postal boundary never moved to match the new municipal one. So you get communities marketed as Estero that sit in unincorporated Lee County, and communities with a Bonita Springs mailing address and a 34135 ZIP that are legally inside the Village of Estero.

Three real things change at that line. None of them is enormous on its own. Together they are worth knowing before you write an offer.

What actually changes when the boundary moves

1. Your flood insurance discount. FEMA's Community Rating System scores each participating community separately and attaches a premium discount to the policy. The Village of Estero currently holds a Class 6 rating, worth a 20% discount inside the special flood hazard area and 10% on preferred risk policies outside it. Unincorporated Lee County is a Class 5 at 25% — the county's own flood page states that "property owners in unincorporated Lee County will automatically receive a 25% discount on their flood insurance," under Lee County ID# 125124, in the program since 1991. The City of Bonita Springs is also a Class 5 at 25%. So is the City of Naples (NFIP #125130) and unincorporated Collier County, which has held Class 5 since October 1992.

Read that again, because it runs the opposite way from what people assume: on the flood line, the communities sold as "Estero" that are not in the Village get the better discount. We walked through the parcel-level flood check itself in our guide to how to check a home's flood risk in Estero — this post is about the step before it, which is knowing whose rating your policy is even drawing on.

These ratings are not permanent, either. In March 2024, FEMA told Lee County and four of its municipalities — the Village of Estero among them — that discounts could be removed over compliance concerns, with FEMA citing repair work done without permits after Hurricane Ian. Unincorporated Lee County and Bonita Springs were both notified on November 21, 2024 that they would keep their Class 5 ratings and their 25%. The Town of Fort Myers Beach was placed on NFIP probation the same day. Your jurisdiction is not just a line on a map; it is a program your neighbors can affect.

2. Your tax bill, modestly. On Lee County's preliminary 2026 taxing district millage book, the Village of Estero levies 0.7300 mills. A parcel in unincorporated Lee County instead pays the Lee County Unincorporated MSTU at 0.8398 plus the Lee County All Hazards Protection District at 0.0693 — about 0.9091 combined. The City of Bonita Springs levies 0.8470. The honest headline: that spread is roughly 0.18 mills, or about $18 per $100,000 of taxable value per year. Everything else — county general revenue at 3.7623, the library, mosquito control, the school levies, the water management district — is identical either way. Anyone telling you the Village is a meaningfully cheaper or pricier tax address is overselling it. If you want the full anatomy of the bill, our guide to reading a Southwest Florida TRIM notice breaks down every line.

3. Which office has to say yes. This is the one that actually costs people time. The Village of Estero adopted its own Land Development Code and runs its own building and permitting operation — the Community Development Help Center at 239-221-5036, permitting@estero-fl.gov, Monday to Friday 8 to 5. An unincorporated parcel goes to Lee County instead. Different code, different counter, different review queue, different code compliance office looking at the lanai your seller enclosed. The Village's code compliance list includes "Building Without a Permit" in so many words.

The four-step check, in the order that settles it fastest

  1. Run the address through Lee County's Find My Flood Zone map. Search the address or zoom to it, click on the actual structure rather than near it, and read the first item in the information box. Give it a minute to load. This is the county's own documented method for answering exactly this question.
  2. Confirm it on the parcel's TRIM notice or tax record. The Lee County Property Appraiser's rate table separates Unincorporated Lee County from each city and town. A Village of Estero or City of Bonita Springs line means the parcel is inside that municipality. Lee County Unincorporated MSTU and Lee County All Hazards — Unincorporated lines mean it is not. The appraiser's own parcel data carries a taxing district field and a zoning-area field whose code ULC stands for unincorporated Lee County.
  3. If the community has a CDD, read the district's "About the District" page. A community development district publishes the municipality it sits in. That is how we settled the question for the Brooks communities: the Brooks district site states that both districts lie "in southern Lee County within the boundaries of the Village of Estero" — which puts Copperleaf, Spring Run, Shadow Wood, Shadow Wood Preserve, and Lighthouse Bay inside Estero despite their Bonita Springs mailing addresses. If you want the broader fee picture, our CDD fees guide covers how those assessments arrive.
  4. Call the jurisdiction the map named and ask it directly. Lee County publishes a floodplain contact for each one: Village of Estero 239-221-5036, City of Bonita Springs 239-444-6150, unincorporated Lee County 239-533-8948, City of Fort Myers 239-321-7925, Cape Coral 239-574-0553, Fort Myers Beach 239-765-0202, Sanibel 239-472-3700. In Collier, the flood information hotline is 239-252-2942. Two minutes on the phone beats an assumption carried to closing.

The Florida-specific wrinkles that catch out-of-state buyers

The municipal lien search is ordered from a jurisdiction. In Florida this is the search that surfaces open or expired building permits, code enforcement violations, unrecorded utility balances, special assessments, and unpaid stormwater or solid waste fees. These obligations often are not recorded in the land records, so a clean title search does not clear them, and they can follow the property after it changes hands. The searches are run jurisdiction by jurisdiction, and procedures differ between a county and a city. Point a search at the wrong government and you get a clean report on a property that is not clean. Given that FEMA's 2024 concern was specifically about unpermitted repair work, this is not a theoretical risk in this corridor.

Your fire district is a separate question. Estero Fire Rescue (2.2880 mills in 2026) and Bonita Springs Fire (2.0100) are independent special districts with their own boundaries. Being inside the Village does not tell you which one you are in, and vice versa.

Your homestead exemption does not care. Homestead eligibility, Save Our Homes, and portability are state and county matters. Doc stamps, at $0.70 per $100 of sale price, are the same statewide. None of this changes at a municipal line.

Published lists are helpful and incomplete. Engage Estero publishes a community-by-community table, current as of October 16, 2024, placing Bella Terra, Grandezza, Preserve at Corkscrew, River Creek, Stoneybrook, and Wildcat Run inside the Village, and Corkscrew Estates, Corkscrew Shores, FFD Farms, Kingston, The Place, WildBlue, and Verdana outside it in unincorporated Lee County. It is a good list. It also covers only the East Corkscrew corridor, which is why the parcel-level check above is the one that actually answers your question.

Red flags worth slowing down for

  • A listing, a builder brochure, or an MLS remark that names a city with no parcel-level backup. Marketing copy is not a jurisdictional finding.
  • A renovation, lanai enclosure, re-roof, or pool cage with no permit history — especially post-2022 work. Confirm which office should hold that permit before you ask whether it exists.
  • An insurance quote that assumes a discount nobody has verified. Ask the agent which community number and CRS class the quote was rated on.
  • A seller or listing agent who answers "which city is this in?" with the mailing address. That is not an answer, and it is the single most common way this goes wrong.
  • Anyone quoting a flat all-in millage for "Estero." The number depends on which side of the line the parcel sits on, and on the fire district.

How a buyer's agent protects you here

None of the four steps above is hard. The part that is hard is knowing that the question exists before you are three days from closing, and knowing what to do with the answer once you have it.

What would we look at first if you sent us an Estero address?

The parcel, not the listing. We start by pulling the governing municipality from the county's own map and matching it against the tax record, because those two sources have to agree before anything else we tell you is worth saying. Once we know whose jurisdiction it is, three things follow in order. We look at what the CRS class means for the flood quote you are actually going to be handed, so the number in your carrying-cost math is the real one and not a borrowed assumption. We look at the permit history on the right counter — the one that would hold it — because an open or missing permit found during the inspection period is a negotiation, and the same permit found after closing is your bill. And we make sure the municipal lien search goes to the correct government, which is the quiet failure point in a corridor where the mailing address and the jurisdiction disagree so often.

What that gets you is simple: by the time you decide, the insurance figure, the tax figure, and the permit picture all belong to the same property, and nobody is guessing. That's the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

Is an Estero mailing address the same as being in the Village of Estero?

No. The Village incorporated on December 31, 2014, long after homes in the area were given Estero mailing addresses, and the postal boundary was never redrawn to match. Communities marketed as Estero can sit in unincorporated Lee County, and some homes with Bonita Springs mailing addresses and 34135 ZIP codes are legally inside the Village.

Does being inside the Village of Estero cost more in property taxes?

Barely — and it is slightly cheaper on the municipal line. The Village levies 0.7300 mills on Lee County's preliminary 2026 millage book, while an unincorporated parcel pays the Unincorporated MSTU at 0.8398 plus All Hazards at 0.0693. That is about $18 per $100,000 of taxable value per year. Every other levy is identical.

Which gives the bigger flood insurance discount?

Unincorporated Lee County. The Village of Estero is a Community Rating System Class 6 at 20% inside the special flood hazard area and 10% on preferred risk policies. Unincorporated Lee County, Bonita Springs, the City of Naples, and unincorporated Collier County are all Class 5 at 25%. The discount follows the community the parcel sits in.

Why does the jurisdiction matter for a municipal lien search?

That search surfaces open or expired permits, code violations, unrecorded utility balances, and special assessments a title search does not reach — and those can follow the property after closing. The searches run jurisdiction by jurisdiction, so one pointed at the wrong government can come back clean on a property that is not.

Does my fire district match my city?

Not necessarily. Estero Fire Rescue and Bonita Springs Fire are independent special districts with their own boundaries and millage — 2.2880 and 2.0100 on the preliminary 2026 book. Confirm yours separately on the tax record.
