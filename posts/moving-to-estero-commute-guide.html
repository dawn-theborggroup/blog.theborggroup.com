Moving to Estero, FL: the 2026 complete commute guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 14, 2026·11 min read

What is it actually like to get around Estero, FL?

Estero is a driving town, and where you live inside it shapes your daily drive more than the mileage does. From the middle of the village, the airport terminal, FGCU, two major hospital campuses, and the Gulf beaches all sit within about 3 to 11 miles in a straight line. What changes your day is which side of I-75 you’re on, which road you depend on from November through April, and the road projects finishing in 2026 and 2027 — including the new Three Oaks Parkway link that opened November 2, 2026.

When people ask us about moving to Estero, the question usually comes out as “How bad is the traffic, really?” It’s a fair question. Most out-of-state buyers tour in one season and live in another, and the commute you test-drive in July isn’t the one you’ll drive in February. This guide maps Estero the way you’ll actually use it.

Why Estero works so well as a home base

The big reasons people choose Southwest Florida still apply here: Florida has no state personal income tax, the winters are mild, and you’re minutes from the Gulf. Estero’s particular advantage is position. It sits almost exactly between Fort Myers and Naples, with the airport just to the north.

We measured the straight-line distance from two central points in Estero to the places our relocating clients ask about most. These are “as the crow flies” numbers, so your drive will be longer:

  • Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW) terminal — 6.9 miles from Estero Village Hall, 8.7 miles from Coconut Point
  • Florida Gulf Coast University — 2.7 miles from Village Hall, 4.5 miles from Coconut Point
  • Hertz Arena — 1.5 miles from Village Hall, 3.1 miles from Coconut Point
  • Lovers Key State Park — 4.4 miles from Coconut Point, 5.2 miles from Village Hall
  • Bonita Beach Park — 5.6 miles from Coconut Point, 7.4 miles from Village Hall
  • Gulf Coast Medical Center (Fort Myers) — 8.5 miles from Village Hall, 10.0 miles from Coconut Point
  • NCH North Naples Hospital — 9.1 miles from Coconut Point, 10.8 miles from Village Hall
  • Downtown Fort Myers — about 15 to 17 miles
  • Downtown Naples — about 18 to 20 miles

That’s a short radius for an airport, a university, a pro hockey arena, two beaches, and two hospital systems. The catch is that almost everything north-south funnels onto a handful of roads.

The four roads that shape your Estero commute

If you remember nothing else, remember this: Estero runs north-south on US 41, Three Oaks Parkway, I-75, and Ben Hill Griffin Parkway, and east-west on Corkscrew Road, Estero Parkway, Coconut Road, and Alico Road at the north edge. Your I-75 on-ramps are Corkscrew Road (Exit 123) in the middle of Estero, Alico Road (Exit 128) to the north, and Bonita Beach Road (Exit 116) to the south.

Three Oaks Parkway now runs all the way to Naples

This is the biggest change for anyone moving here this season. On November 2, 2026, Lee County opened the Three Oaks Parkway extension, the “missing link” in a continuous route from Daniels Parkway in Lee County down to Radio Road in Collier County by way of Three Oaks Parkway, Imperial Parkway, and Livingston Road, according to WGCU. Commissioner Brian Hamman described it as a way to skip the interstate entirely: when there’s a crash on I-75, “you can hop off and drive this route all the way down to Naples.”

Construction isn’t completely finished. WGCU reported final completion scheduled for early 2027, and Lee County’s August 2026 Roadwatch report still listed Phase 2 work between Indian Paint Lane and Daniels Parkway with an estimated completion of mid-2028. Expect some lane shifts on the north end for a while. Even so, if you live between US 41 and I-75, Three Oaks just became your best alternate route north and south.

Corkscrew Road is the lifeline east of I-75

Most of Estero’s newer communities — Verdana Village, Corkscrew Shores, The Place at Corkscrew, Bella Terra, and WildBlue — sit east of I-75, and Corkscrew Road is the main way in and out. Lee County’s widening project covers 4.4 miles from Ben Hill Griffin Parkway to Alico Road. Phase 1, to Bella Terra Boulevard, is complete. Lee County’s August 2026 Roadwatch put Phase 2, Bella Terra Boulevard to Alico Road, at completion “at the end of 2026,” with the Village of Estero’s shared-use path along Corkscrew estimated for early 2027.

Corkscrew is also where growth is concentrated. The Village of Estero’s 2024 traffic study, prepared by Johnson Engineering, found Corkscrew Road and Three Oaks Parkway had the most crashes over five years, and it projected the Coconut Point–to–Three Oaks segment to fall to the worst service rating, F, by 2034. If you’re weighing a home out east, it’s worth understanding how many more homes are planned along Corkscrew Road.

US 41 and Ben Hill Griffin are the in-season pinch points

US 41 is your route to Coconut Point, Bonita Springs, and the beaches, and it’s the road that frustrates new residents most from November through April. Ben Hill Griffin Parkway carries FGCU, Miromar, and Hertz Arena traffic. Neither is a reason not to live here — they’re reasons to know your alternate route before you buy.

I-75 is getting wider, and that cuts both ways

FDOT’s project to widen about 21 miles of I-75 from Golden Gate Parkway to Alico Road started this fall, WGCU reported on September 20, 2026. It adds optional toll express lanes, with one running through the Corkscrew Road interchange, plus auxiliary lanes between interchanges. FDOT gave a three-year construction window, with the first congestion-relief lanes between Bonita Beach Road and Immokalee Road targeted for early 2027.

For buyers, the overlooked piece is noise. FDOT proposed 22-foot noise walls for 29 neighborhoods, but the plans showed no continuous wall along parts of The Brooks in Estero, and Estero Councilmember Jim Ward asked FDOT to meet with residents there. If you’re looking at a home that backs up to the interstate — in The Brooks or anywhere along the corridor — check the noise-wall plan for that stretch before you fall in love with the lanai.

Estero’s neighborhoods through a commute lens

You can think of Estero as four driving zones. Each one trades something.

  • West of US 41, near Coconut Point and the bay. Closest to the beaches and to Coconut Point shopping and dining. Your daily trips are short, but you’ll use US 41 constantly in season.
  • Between US 41 and I-75. The Brooks and many established communities. You get both US 41 and the new Three Oaks route, and the Corkscrew interchange is close. Homes nearest the interstate are where the noise question matters.
  • East of I-75 along Corkscrew Road. Newer construction, larger lots, and resort-style amenities, with one main corridor in and out. Commutes to the beach run longer here, and the Corkscrew widening matters most to you.
  • The north end near Alico Road and FGCU. The quickest airport runs and easy access to Ben Hill Griffin and the Alico interchange, which is useful if you travel often or split time between homes.

What getting around adds to your cost of living

Your housing cost, property taxes at your purchase price, homeowners and flood insurance, and any HOA or CDD fees are the big numbers — we break those down in our true cost of living guide for Bonita Springs and Estero. Getting around adds a few line items that people moving from a transit city often skip:

  • A car for every driver. Public transit is limited. LeeTran’s Route 600 ends at Coconut Point Mall and runs roughly hourly most days, and its on-demand ULTRA service operates in a Bonita Springs zone; check LeeTran’s service map for current boundaries.
  • Florida auto insurance and registration. Get a quote at your new Estero address before you move. It’s a real budget line, and it varies by driver.
  • Tolls, if you want them. The new I-75 express lanes are optional toll lanes. You’ll be able to pay to skip congestion, or not.
  • Golf carts aren’t a commute plan. Many communities allow them inside the gates, but Estero’s main roads are built for cars.

Schools, healthcare, and everyday amenities

Healthcare is a real strength of Estero’s location. Lee Health operates a campus at Coconut Point, Gulf Coast Medical Center is about 8.5 to 10 miles north, and NCH North Naples Hospital is about 9 to 11 miles south, measured in a straight line. For many relocating buyers, being between two hospital systems is a deciding factor.

Public school assignments in Lee County follow the district’s own process, so confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the School District of Lee County. Our guide to school enrollment when moving to Estero walks through how that works. FGCU sits right on Estero’s northern edge, and Coconut Point, Miromar Outlets, and Hertz Arena are all short drives for most residents.

What to expect as an out-of-state buyer

Plenty of our buyers start their search from hundreds of miles away. If you’re in that spot, three things will make the commute question easier:

  1. Drive your real routes at your real times. If you can visit between January and March, drive from the community to the places you’ll go most at 8 a.m. and 5 p.m.
  2. If you can’t visit in season, ask. Your agent should tell you which roads you’ll depend on and what changes in winter, not just show you the house.
  3. Plan for construction through 2027. Corkscrew Road, Three Oaks Parkway’s north end, I-75, and the Daniels Parkway interchange (FDOT’s completion estimate is summer 2028) will all have work zones for a while.

Our complete Estero relocation guide covers the rest of the move, and buyers coming from up north will find our guide to relocating to Estero from the Northeast and Midwest useful.

Common relocation mistakes we see with Estero commutes

  • Judging traffic in July. Summer roads are a different world. Seasonal residents return in fall, and the difference is noticeable on US 41 and Ben Hill Griffin.
  • Choosing a community on distance alone. Two homes the same distance from the beach can have very different drives, depending on whether you’re crossing I-75 and which road you’re stuck on.
  • Missing the noise question. A home that backs up to I-75 is about to sit next to a wider highway. Check the noise-wall plan for that exact stretch.
  • Assuming you won’t need a second car. Coming from a city with good transit, this one surprises people.

What would we look at first if you called us about moving to Estero?

We’d start with your week, not the listings. Where do you fly to, and how often? Who’s commuting, and where to? How often do you want to be at the beach, the doctor, or the grandkids’ house in Naples or Fort Myers? Those answers usually narrow Estero down to one or two of the four driving zones before we’ve looked at a single home.

Then we’d look at the specific roads each home depends on. If it’s east of I-75, we’d weigh where Corkscrew Road construction stands. If it’s between US 41 and I-75, we’d factor in the new Three Oaks route. If it backs up to the interstate, we’d check whether a noise wall is planned for that stretch.

We’ve been helping buyers settle into Southwest Florida since 2005, and the goal is simple: you move in knowing exactly what your daily drive will be like in February, not finding out after closing. That’s the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Estero from the Fort Myers airport (RSW)?

The RSW terminal is about 7 to 9 miles in a straight line from central Estero, measured from Estero Village Hall and Coconut Point. The north end of Estero near Alico Road is closest, and your actual drive depends on where in Estero you live.

Is traffic bad in Estero, FL?

Traffic is heaviest from November through April, when seasonal residents return, especially on US 41, Ben Hill Griffin Parkway, and Corkscrew Road. The Village of Estero’s 2024 traffic study rated most village roads B to C on an A-to-F scale in peak season, but it projected some segments to worsen as the area grows.

What is the Three Oaks Parkway extension?

It’s the new link that opened November 2, 2026, creating a continuous north-south route from Daniels Parkway in Lee County to Radio Road in Collier County via Three Oaks Parkway, Imperial Parkway, and Livingston Road. It gives Estero drivers an alternative to I-75 and US 41, with some construction continuing into 2027 and beyond.

Do you need a car to live in Estero?

Yes, for most households. LeeTran’s Route 600 ends at Coconut Point Mall and runs about hourly most days, but most daily errands, commutes, and beach trips in Estero require a car.

When will the Corkscrew Road widening be finished?

Lee County’s August 2026 Roadwatch report listed Phase 2 of the Corkscrew Road widening, from Bella Terra Boulevard to Alico Road, as expected to finish at the end of 2026. The Village of Estero’s shared-use path along Corkscrew was estimated for early 2027.
