How to use your 7-day condo cancellation right in Southwest Florida FL: 2026 buyer's guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 11, 2026·8 min read

Can you cancel a Florida condo contract after reading the milestone inspection or reserve study?

Yes. On a resale by a unit owner other than the developer, Florida Statute 718.503(2) gives you 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, to void the contract by delivering written notice after you receive the association's documents — including the inspector-prepared summary of the milestone inspection report and the association's most recent structural integrity reserve study. For contracts entered into after December 31, 2024, that same window attaches specifically to receipt of those reports. The right to void ends at closing, which means the clock is the whole game.

By Mark & Dawn Borg | October 11, 2026

Here's the version of this question we actually get, usually by text, usually on a weekend: the association just sent over a stack of PDFs, there's an engineering report in there, the numbers look bad, and we're supposed to close in three weeks. Are we stuck?

You're almost certainly not stuck. But the protection Florida gives you is narrow, it's written, and it expires — and most buyers don't learn it exists until the window has already closed.

Why this matters right now in Southwest Florida

Three things are converging at once, and together they explain why so many Southwest Florida condo deals are wobbling this quarter.

First, there's a hard deadline 81 days out. Under the condominium law changes enacted as Chapter 2025-175 (HB 913), buildings of three or more habitable stories reaching 30 years in 2026 generally must complete their milestone inspection by December 31, 2026 — and where that inspection is due on or before then, the structural integrity reserve study may be completed concurrently, with none permitted after December 31, 2026. A large volume of reports is landing right now, and the assessments that follow them are landing with them.

Second, Collier County pulls the trigger earlier than most buyers expect. Collier applies the 25-year rule for buildings within three miles of saltwater and the 30-year rule beyond that line, using a published milestone map and saltwater-boundary document to decide which side a building falls on. For a coastal building with a certificate of occupancy dated on or after January 1, 2000, the first inspection is due in the year it turns 25. Collier also raised its report submission fee from $50 to $500 effective January 1, 2026, and treats an ordinance violation as a misdemeanor — up to a $500 fine, up to 60 days in county jail, or both, with each day of continued noncompliance counted separately.

Third, reserves can no longer be waived. For budgets adopted on or after December 31, 2024, associations generally cannot waive or underfund required reserve components, and those that had previously waived or reduced reserves were expected to begin funding them January 1, 2026. The 2026 reserve-study threshold sits at $25,675, up from $25,000. In practical terms: the shortfall a reserve study identifies no longer sits quietly in a file. It becomes a line item, and somebody pays it.

You can see it in the numbers. In July 2026 the Naples condo and townhouse segment posted a median sale price of $407,000, down 5.3% year over year, at 91 median days on market, 6.9 months of supply, and 94.4% of list. Naples single-family homes the same month: $790,000, up 13.0%, 70 days on market, 5.0 months of supply. That spread isn't an accident — it's the market pricing in compliance risk.

A condo building that has finished its inspection, priced the repairs, and funded them is often a better buy than one that has done none of those things. What ruins a purchase isn't a known assessment. It's an unknown one that shows up 90 days after you close.

The step-by-step: how to use the window

1. Request the statutory documents in writing. At the seller's expense, you're entitled to current copies of the declaration, articles of incorporation, bylaws and rules, annual financial statement and budget, the inspector-prepared summary of the milestone inspection report if one applies, the most recent structural integrity reserve study or a statement that none has been completed, the turnover inspection report for any turnover inspection performed on or after July 1, 2023, and the "Frequently Asked Questions and Answers" document. Ask in writing, and ask early.

2. Add your Condominium Rider requests to the contract. The Condominium Rider revised July 1, 2025 added Paragraph 6(b), which lets you request additional association records from the prior 12 months by agreement with the seller — board and membership meeting agendas and minutes, insurance declaration pages, completed milestone, turnover, or reserve-study reports if received before closing, and up to three other relevant documents. Two cautions: these requests are negotiable, so a seller can strike them in a counteroffer, and they have to be written into the agreement to be binding. A request made by email and never papered into the contract carries no remedy.

3. Date-stamp everything on arrival. Your window runs from receipt. The delivery date is the only fact that fixes your deadline, so note the date and the method for every document, and don't rely on the listing agent's recollection of when things went out.

4. Read the milestone summary for one thing first: Phase 1 or Phase 2. Phase 1 finding no substantial structural deterioration generally pauses the process until the next cycle. Phase 2 means deterioration was found, and the consequences are concrete — in Collier County, a permit application within 150 days, repairs beginning within 365 days of the building official receiving the report, and an engineer's or architect's letter on continued occupancy, valid 180 days.

5. Put the reserve study next to the bank balance. The reserve study tells you what each required component should be funded at; the annual financial statement tells you what's actually there. The gap is your exposure. We read these side by side, because a reserve study on its own is just a document and a balance on its own is just a number.

6. Count the days the way the statute counts them. Seven days excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays is usually nine or ten calendar days. Count seven calendar days and you give away a third of your window; assume three and you give away most of it.

7. If you're cancelling, cancel in writing. The statute contemplates written notice of your intention to cancel, delivered within the window — a phone call isn't notice. Keep proof of delivery, and copy the title company holding your escrow deposit so the release isn't disputed later.

Florida-specific nuances that trip buyers up

The 7-day window is a condo rule, not an HOA rule. The 3-business-day cancellation right buyers hear about so often comes from Chapter 720, which governs homeowners associations. Condominiums run under Chapter 718. If you're buying a condo and you apply the HOA number, you'll walk away from days you were entitled to. We cover the HOA side separately in our guide to evaluating a Florida HOA before buying.

The estoppel letter is a different document, and you still want it. The estoppel letter tells you what this specific unit owes right now — dues, pending special assessments, transfer fees, violations. The reserve study tells you what the building will owe later. Order both. And if the community sits inside a community development district, that bills independently again; see our breakdown of CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero.

Doc stamps and the title company don't change. Florida doc stamps run $0.70 per $100 of sale price and are customarily paid by the seller, and a title company — not an attorney — handles your closing, as we explain in our walkthrough of closing day in Florida. None of that shifts because the building has a compliance problem. What shifts is who absorbs the repair bill — and that's negotiable.

Lee County isn't one jurisdiction for this. Municipalities within Lee County act as their own local enforcement agencies, and Cape Coral and Sanibel publish their own resources — so a building in Bonita Springs, Fort Myers Beach, Estero, or Cape Coral can't be assigned a deadline from a countywide procedure. The authority named on the building's own notice governs. Confirm there, not from a general county page.

Red flags to watch for

  • No milestone inspection on a building that clearly qualifies. Three or more habitable stories, 30 years old, nothing on file — that's not a paperwork gap, that's a funding decision the board has been deferring.
  • A statement that no reserve study has been completed on a building whose milestone inspection was due on or before December 31, 2026. There is no legitimate path to completing one after that date.
  • A Phase 2 report with no permit application and no repair schedule. The clock on repairs is already running whether or not the board has acted.
  • A recent vote to pause or reduce reserve contributions. The law allows this narrowly — a milestone inspection completed within the prior two calendar years, majority approval of total voting interests, funds directed to milestone-related repairs, two consecutive annual budgets at most, and only for budgets adopted on or before December 31, 2028. Legal, but it means the money isn't there yet.
  • A seller who resists putting Paragraph 6(b) requests into the contract. Sometimes it's inconvenience. Sometimes it's knowledge.

How a buyer's agent protects you here

This is a deadline problem disguised as a document problem. The documents are public, the statute is public, and the arithmetic isn't hard. What goes wrong is that nobody tracks the delivery date, nobody papers the record requests into the contract, and the window quietly expires while everyone waits for the board to respond.

What we do on a condo file: get the statutory request out the day we go under contract, get 6(b) into the agreement before the seller has a reason to say no, log every delivery with a date, read the milestone summary and reserve study against the financial statement rather than in isolation, and calendar the cancellation deadline the way the statute counts it. If the numbers don't work, your written notice goes out in time. If they do work, you get a price conversation grounded in a quantified repair figure instead of a rumor. We're not attorneys and this isn't legal advice — on a contract question with this much money on it, a Florida real estate attorney is worth the call.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to cancel a Florida condo resale contract?

On a resale by a unit owner other than the developer, Florida Statute 718.503(2) gives you 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, measured from execution of the agreement and receipt of the association's documents. For contracts entered into after December 31, 2024, the same window is tied to receipt of the milestone inspection summary, turnover inspection report, or structural integrity reserve study where the association has completed one. The right to void ends at closing.

Is the condo cancellation window 3 days, 7 days, or 15 days?

For a condominium resale it's 7 days excluding weekends and legal holidays. The 3-business-day window buyers often hear about comes from Chapter 720, which governs homeowners associations, not condominiums. Applying the HOA number to a condo contract is one of the most common and costliest mistakes we see.

What happens if the seller never gives me the reserve study?

If the association hasn't completed a structural integrity reserve study, it must provide a statement saying so — and that statement is itself a disclosure you're entitled to. If the statutory documents aren't delivered within the required period, the contract is voidable at your option regardless of other contract terms. Missing documents are not a reason to proceed on faith.

Why are so many Southwest Florida condo reports landing in late 2026?

Buildings reaching 30 years in 2026 generally must complete their milestone inspection by December 31, 2026, and where that inspection is due on or before that date the reserve study may be done concurrently — with none permitted to be completed after December 31, 2026. Collier County's 25-year trigger for buildings within three miles of saltwater pulls much of the coastal inventory forward, concentrating a large volume of reports, and the assessments that follow, into a narrow window.

Does a bad milestone inspection mean I should walk away?

Not automatically. A building that has completed its inspection, priced the repairs, and funded them is often a safer purchase than one that has done none of those things, and the price usually reflects the known cost. The decision turns on whether the assessment is quantified, who pays it, and whether that figure is already in the price you negotiated.

The short version: Florida hands condo buyers a real escape hatch, open for roughly nine or ten calendar days from the moment the documents arrive. Knowing it exists is most of the protection. Knowing how to count it is the rest.
