Compass Landing, Naples FL: is buying resale worth it in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 28, 2026·9 min read

Compass Landing in North Naples sold through its last new-construction homesite in the past couple of years, which means every purchase there today is a resale — no builder sales center, no design center upgrades, no incentive negotiation. If you're circling this gated Mattamy Homes community on Zillow or Realtor.com, the questions have shifted: what does a 2026 resale actually cost, what do the HOA dues cover now that there's no builder subsidizing anything, and how does the buying process change once you're negotiating with a homeowner instead of a builder's sales rep?

Here's what we tell buyers who ask us about Compass Landing specifically, and how it stacks up against two other low-fee, non-golf North Naples communities worth cross-shopping.

This one comes up often enough that it's worth addressing directly: Compass Landing isn't a distressed or forgotten community. It's a fully built-out, well-maintained gated neighborhood that simply moved from its "new construction" phase into its "established resale" phase faster than some buyers expect, because Mattamy sold through inventory efficiently. The community itself isn't the question mark — how you weigh new construction against resale in general is.

Compass Landing at a glance: from builder community to resale-only

Compass Landing sits in North Naples off Collier Boulevard (CR-951), close to its intersection with Immokalee Road, in the 34120 zip code. Mattamy Homes — a Canadian builder with developments across Florida — built the community out to roughly 273 single-family homes across ten floor plans before closing out new-home sales. That makes Compass Landing meaningfully younger than most of the resale inventory in North Naples, but it also means the builder relationship buyers rely on for warranty questions, design center change orders, or incentive timing simply isn't part of the equation anymore.

The community is gated, with three lakes and preserve views woven through the site plan, and it sits about 10 minutes from I-75 via Immokalee Road and roughly 20 minutes from the Gulf beaches. Because it's inland rather than coastal, flood insurance costs tend to run lower here than in beachfront Naples communities — but flood zone determinations are property-specific, not neighborhood-wide, so pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map for any address you're serious about before you assume anything.

The Immokalee Road corridor around Compass Landing has been one of the more active growth areas in Collier County, with ongoing intersection and roadway improvements planned in the vicinity to keep pace with new rooftops pushing east. That's generally a good sign for long-term property values, but it also means construction traffic and periodic road work are part of living in this part of North Naples for the next several years — worth a drive-through at different times of day before you commit.

The question we get most about Compass Landing isn't "is it a good community" — it clearly is. It's "since the builder's gone, what am I actually buying into?" That's a fair question, and the answer is: a well-built, low-fee resale community where you're now dealing entirely with individual sellers instead of a builder's sales office.

Homes and pricing: what resale actually costs in 2026

Mattamy offered ten floor plans at Compass Landing — Admiral, Alee, Banyan, Capstan, Mariner, Pennant, Commodore, Regatta, Trimaran, and Windward — ranging from about 1,994 to 3,747 square feet, with three to four bedrooms, two to three-and-a-half baths, and two- or three-car garages. Coastal and Craftsman-style exteriors were both offered, and most homes came with open-concept layouts, kitchen islands, granite counters, and covered lanais.

As of this writing, active resale listings in Compass Landing range from around $728,000 for a 2,530-square-foot, 4-bedroom-plus-den layout up to $1.76 million for a larger 3,445-square-foot model, which works out to roughly $288 to $511 per square foot depending on size, lot, and finish level. That's a meaningfully small, moving sample — resale inventory in a 273-home community turns over slowly — so treat any specific number as a snapshot, not gospel. This is exactly the kind of detail where a current comparative market analysis on the specific floor plan and lot you're considering matters more than any published range.

  • Signature homes: HOA dues run about $225/month
  • Executive homes: HOA dues run about $242/month
  • No CDD fee — Compass Landing was never inside a Community Development District, so you won't see a separate CDD line on your tax bill the way you would at many newer North Naples and Estero communities
  • HOA dues include lawn maintenance, which is worth factoring in when you're comparing all-in monthly costs against a community that charges a lower HOA fee but leaves landscaping to the homeowner

For context on how far Compass Landing has appreciated, quick move-in inventory homes were pricing in the roughly $409,000-$580,000 range when the builder still had active availability. Today's resale figures reflect several years of price growth on top of that base, plus whatever updates individual owners have made since closing — another reason a floor-plan-specific comparative market analysis matters more than the community-wide range.

Naples overall was firmly in buyer's territory as of August 2026 — Redfin put the market's Compete Score at just 5 out of 100, with homes averaging 124 days on market. Compass Landing's inland, mid-price positioning generally moves faster than Naples's ultra-luxury coastal segment, but the citywide conditions still give resale buyers real room to negotiate on price, closing costs, or timeline.

Amenities and lifestyle: who Compass Landing fits

The clubhouse anchors the amenity package: a resort-style pool and spa, a fitness center, and a playground, plus sidewalks and internal streets designed to be walked, jogged, and biked rather than just driven. Three lakes and nature preserve frontage give a number of homesites water or green-space views without the premium you'd pay for true waterfront.

There's no age restriction and no golf course, which keeps the HOA structure simple — you're not paying into a golf operating budget or a bundled membership the way you would at a community like Bonita National or Colonial Country Club. That makes Compass Landing a fit for buyers who want a gated, amenity-rich address without a country club price tag: young families, remote workers who want walkable streets, and downsizers who want low-maintenance living without an active-adult age requirement.

For buyers with children, Compass Landing is zoned to Bear Creek Elementary, Oak Ridge Middle, and Gulf Coast High School within the Collier County Public Schools system — but assignment boundaries can shift, so confirm zoning for the specific address you're considering rather than relying on the neighborhood name alone.

The buying process now that the builder is gone

This is where Compass Landing diverges from the newer sales-center communities we cover most often. When Mattamy was still building here, buyers negotiated directly with an on-site sales representative whose job was to represent the builder's interests, not the buyer's — which is exactly why we always recommend bringing your own buyer's agent to a builder sales center, ideally before your first visit, so your representation is in place before you sign anything.

That builder relationship is gone now. Every Compass Landing purchase runs through the standard Florida resale process instead:

  • You're using the FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract (or a similar standard resale contract) rather than a builder's proprietary purchase agreement, which changes your contingency structure, inspection period length, and deposit schedule — all of it is negotiable in a way builder contracts typically aren't.
  • An HOA estoppel letter becomes essential. Florida law requires the seller to provide one before closing, and it discloses exactly what's owed to the association, any pending special assessments, and whether the account is current. Don't waive your right to review it.
  • Ask for the HOA's most recent budget and reserve study, not just the current monthly due — here's our full walkthrough of evaluating a Florida HOA before you buy. A healthy reserve fund now is what keeps a $225-$242/month due from turning into a special assessment in five years.
  • A standard home inspection matters more here than it would on a brand-new build, since these homes are now several years into their life and any deferred maintenance is the seller's responsibility to disclose, not a builder's warranty to fix.
  • A wind mitigation inspection is still worth ordering even on newer construction — it documents hurricane-resistant features for your insurer and can meaningfully reduce your homeowners premium.

The upside of resale-only status: you're not competing against a builder's next phase of incentives, and you have far more room to negotiate price, closing cost credits, and timeline directly with a motivated seller than you ever would across a builder's sales desk. With Naples homes averaging over four months on market this year, sellers who priced ambitiously in spring are often ready to talk by the time an offer lands in their inbox — timing your offer around days-on-market, not just list price, is one of the more useful things a buyer's agent brings to a resale negotiation like this one.

How Compass Landing compares to Naples Reserve and Azure at Hacienda Lakes

Two other non-golf, gated Naples communities come up constantly when buyers are cross-shopping Compass Landing:

Naples Reserve is a larger, Lennar-built lagoon community with a very different lifestyle hook — a private beach club on its 125-acre lake, kayaking, and Freedom Boat Club access. HOA dues run higher, roughly $350-$500 per month, but that covers lake activities, fitness, and pickleball on top of the pool. Like Compass Landing, it carries no CDD fee. If lakefront recreation matters more to you than walkability and a lower monthly due, Naples Reserve is the stronger fit.

Azure at Hacienda Lakes, built by Toll Brothers, sits at a wider price range — roughly $529,000 to $2.4 million across villas, coach homes, and detached single-family models from about 1,500 to over 4,000 square feet. The amenity center ("The Port") includes a resort pool and spa, tennis, and pickleball. The key difference from Compass Landing: Hacienda Lakes sits inside the Hacienda Lakes Community Development District, so buyers there do pay a CDD assessment on top of HOA dues — something to run the full numbers on before you compare sticker prices side by side.

Against both, Compass Landing's pitch is simplicity: a lower, flat HOA fee that includes lawn care, zero CDD debt, and a smaller, already-established community where you know exactly what you're moving into because there's no next phase still being built around you. Naples Reserve trades that simplicity for a bigger lifestyle amenity, and Azure at Hacienda Lakes trades it for a wider range of home sizes and price points — neither is wrong, they're just answering a different question than Compass Landing is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Compass Landing in Naples still selling new construction homes?

No. Mattamy Homes has sold through its new-home inventory at Compass Landing, so every current purchase is a resale transaction through the MLS rather than a builder contract.

What are the HOA fees at Compass Landing?

HOA dues run about $225/month for Signature homes and $242/month for Executive homes, and they include lawn maintenance. There is no CDD fee.

Does Compass Landing have a CDD fee?

No. Compass Landing was never established inside a Community Development District, which sets it apart from several newer North Naples and Estero communities that carry CDD debt assessments on top of HOA dues.

What schools is Compass Landing zoned for?

Compass Landing falls within Collier County Public Schools and is currently zoned to Bear Creek Elementary, Oak Ridge Middle, and Gulf Coast High School. Confirm zoning for your specific address, since assignment boundaries can change.

Do I still need a buyer's agent if Compass Landing has no builder sales office anymore?

Yes — if anything, representation matters more in a resale transaction. Without a builder's sales process to lean on, you're relying entirely on your own agent to negotiate price, review the HOA estoppel letter and reserve study, and manage your inspection contingencies.
