Grandezza vs Wildcat Run, Estero FL: which club actually sets your dues? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 3, 2026·10 min read

What is the real difference between Grandezza and Wildcat Run in Estero, FL?

The homes are comparable. The courses are both private. The difference that matters most is who owns the golf club — and that single fact changes who decides what you pay next year, and how much of the club's finances you can read before you make an offer. The Club at Grandezza was acquired by Heritage Golf Group on September 13, 2023, and Heritage has been owned by KSL Capital Partners, a private equity firm, since February 2020. Wildcat Run's club is a member association, organized as a 501(c)(7) social and recreational club, which means five years of its summary financials are sitting in a public IRS database you can open right now. One of these communities gives a buyer a document trail. The other gives you a phone number.

By Mark & Dawn Borg | November 3, 2026

We get this comparison in a very specific form: a buyer has narrowed to two Estero country club communities, likes a home in each, and wants to know which is the better long-term financial decision. Our guides to Grandezza and Wildcat Run answer the lifestyle half. This post answers the governance half, because that is the part nobody publishes.

Quick snapshot: both communities at a glance

Grandezza sits on roughly 550 acres, with a course that opened in 2001 designed by Darwin Sharpe III, and construction running from about 2001 to 2007. It is a varied community — single-family homes, villas, carriage homes, and condominiums — with home sizes reported from 1,396 to 5,021 square feet and a median sale price around $605,000 at roughly $301 per square foot, on Southwest Florida MLS data compiled by a listing aggregator carrying a 2026 copyright. Current listings have run from $275,000 to $2,499,900.

Wildcat Run is smaller and more uniform: 450 homes around a single Arnold Palmer and Ed Seay course that opened in 1983, with a 26,000-square-foot clubhouse that completed a $3.4 million renovation in November 2018. Condos have listed from the low $200,000s, single-family homes well past $2 million. A brokerage page reporting the twelve months to September 2025 recorded 15 sales at an average sale price of $981,568, 91% of list, $214 per square foot, and 195 average days on market.

Both are gated, both are resale-only, and both sit in the Estero area of Lee County. One thing we do not state for either: whether a specific parcel falls inside the incorporated Village of Estero or in unincorporated Lee County. That changes the municipal lien search and the flood insurance discount, so confirm it address by address using the method in our guide to checking Village of Estero city limits.

Price and value: the fee that moves, and the fee you live with

Here is where the documents get interesting. We were able to read both clubs' own published dues schedules — not agent summaries of them, the schedules themselves.

Grandezza's own 2023 Schedule of Dues and Fees, marked revised 11/2022, lists four tiers:

  • Golf — $55,000 non-refundable initiation, $11,227.83 annual dues
  • Sports — $7,500 initiation, $6,565.65 annual dues
  • Club — $5,000 initiation, $4,092.82 annual dues
  • Social — $1,500 initiation, $4,092.82 annual dues

Add a $1,000 annual food and beverage minimum per membership, a $31 per person cart fee, a $2,250 trail fee, and 6.5% Florida sales tax on dues and non-refundable fees. The packet is explicit that Social membership "is mandatory for all Grandezza residents who do not have higher category of membership." Run the arithmetic on the floor: $4,092.82 plus tax is $4,358.85, plus the $1,000 minimum is $5,358.85 a year — which is why our Grandezza guide describes a baseline of about $5,300 whether you ever pick up a club. Two independent documents, one number.

Now compare the 2022 schedule, revised 12/2021, from the same club: Golf initiation $35,000, Golf dues $10,493.30, food and beverage minimum $750, trail fee $2,100. In one year, the dues rose about 7% and the initiation rose $20,000. The recurring number crept. The entry number jumped.

Wildcat Run's own 2019 Membership Categories document is structured differently, and the structure is the story:

  • Resident Non-Refundable Golf Equity — $45,000 initiation (inclusive of the $10,000 social initiation); $9,130 operational dues plus $2,645 capital dues; $12,368.45 total with tax; $1,200 food and beverage minimum
  • Resident Social Equity — $10,000 initiation; $4,375 operational dues plus $1,485 capital dues; $6,144.38 total with tax; $1,200 minimum
  • Junior Golf Equity — $22,500 initiation, half dues until age 45
  • 90-day lessee trial golf — $2,500

Notice what Wildcat Run does that Grandezza's schedule does not: it names the capital line separately. A golf member there could see that $2,645 of a $12,368 bill was capital rather than operations. Grandezza's 2023 packet lists no capital or reserve assessment at all, and markets that absence — "No Assessments from The Club at Grandezza." Those are two different promises. One shows you the capital contribution and calls it by name. The other tells you there isn't an assessment, which is a genuine selling point and also means the capital spending is funded somewhere you cannot see on the fee sheet.

And now the honest part. Neither club's current schedule was reachable. Both clubs' own websites are closed to automated access, and Heritage's own Grandezza membership brochure publishes no figures at all — it says "Under NEW Ownership" and routes you to a phone number and an email address. So the current-year numbers available anywhere are agent-page figures, and they are much higher than the last club-published ones:

  • Grandezza golf — $35,000 initiation (club, 2022 schedule) → $55,000 (club, 2023 schedule) → $65,000 initiation and $14,520 dues (agent page, last modified March 29, 2026)
  • Wildcat Run golf — $45,000 initiation and $12,368.45 all-in (club, 2019) → $100,000 initiation and $20,685 dues (agent page, last modified January 4, 2026), with a stated golf membership cap of 350

We are not picking one of those numbers and calling it current. That is a spread with vintages, and the only figure that governs your purchase is the one the club's membership office gives you in writing this week. What the spread does tell you, reliably, is direction and magnitude: at both clubs, the cost of entry has moved a lot, and faster than the dues.

Amenities head-to-head

Grandezza's advantage is optionality. Four tiers mean a buyer who does not golf can hold Social and stay at the floor, and the Sports tier serves people who want some golf without full golf dues — the 2023 packet gives Sports members unlimited golf from May through October for the $31 cart fee alone. For a seasonal owner, that is a real structural feature, not a brochure line.

Wildcat Run's advantage is access. One Arnold Palmer course, 450 homes, a 26,000-square-foot clubhouse, five lighted Har-Tru courts, and a membership described in 2019 as "just over 300 members." The golf membership cap on the current agent page — 350 — is consistent with a club sized deliberately small, and fewer members per hole is the whole proposition. For the market-wide picture these two sit against, see our Estero buyer's guide and Estero relocation guide.

Who each community is best for

Grandezza fits the buyer who wants a lower entry point and a tier to grow into, values a varied home inventory from condominiums to large single-family homes, and is comfortable that the club is a professionally operated business with a national operator's resources behind it — and that the operator, not the residents, sets the price.

Wildcat Run fits the buyer whose first question is "how soon can I actually play," who wants a small community, and who wants to belong to the organization that owns the course. It also fits the buyer who does diligence by reading documents, because this is one of the few clubs in Southwest Florida where you genuinely can.

Buying process differences: what you can read before you sign

In both communities the real estate side is the same Florida process — title company closing, doc stamps at $0.70 per $100 of sale price paid by the seller, the Seller's Property Disclosure, a four-point and wind mitigation inspection on anything with age on it, and an HOA estoppel letter that settles the association's numbers. Our guide to the HOA approval process covers the estoppel and the approval window, and our walkthrough on how to evaluate a Florida HOA before buying covers the reserve study and funded ratio. Neither covers what follows, because what follows is not an HOA question. It's a club-ownership question.

At Wildcat Run, the club files publicly. Wildcat Run Country Club Association Inc is registered with the IRS as a 501(c)(7) social and recreational club under NTEE code N6A (Golf), formed in 1985, with 154 employees. Its Form 990 summaries report, by fiscal year ending in September:

  • FY2025 — revenue $8,365,133; expenses $10,030,802; assets $33,778,811; liabilities $12,084,432 (filed August 17, 2026)
  • FY2024 — revenue $7,249,137; expenses $8,883,816; assets $34,788,244; liabilities $13,400,020
  • FY2023 — revenue $5,015,768; expenses $7,337,444; assets $33,213,637; liabilities $11,921,808
  • FY2022 — revenue $6,381,796; expenses $8,028,207; assets $27,660,911; liabilities $7,180,069
  • FY2021 — revenue $5,890,753; expenses $7,082,406; assets $28,353,084; liabilities $8,141,091

Read those narrowly. A Form 990 summary is not a financial statement, and for a social club the revenue line does not necessarily capture member capital contributions, so the gap between revenue and expenses in those years is not evidence of anything on its own. What the series does show is scale and trend: reported revenue up from $5.0 million to $8.4 million across three years, assets around $33.8 million, and liabilities down from $13.4 million to $12.1 million in the most recent year. That is a club of knowable size with a knowable balance sheet, and the complete filing plus the association's own financials settle the detail.

At Grandezza, there is no equivalent to read — and that is a structural consequence, not an oversight. A privately owned club does not file a public return. What exists instead is a documented ownership history: Heritage Golf Group announced its acquisition of the club on September 13, 2023, buying from Gravitas, LLC, with Heritage's president and CEO Mark Burnett quoted saying the company was "extremely pleased to be part of Grandézza and add to our Florida collection of clubs." It was Heritage's 33rd club. The purchase price was not disclosed. KSL Capital Partners had acquired Heritage itself in February 2020, when the portfolio was six properties, with a KSL partner describing the deal as aligned with "our strategy of purchasing high-quality assets in the travel and leisure industry." By May 2026, The Real Deal reported Heritage operating 47 courses and KSL agreeing to acquire Invited Clubs — over 150 clubs — for about $3 billion including debt.

None of that is a criticism of the club. A well-capitalized operator can be a real advantage: capital projects get funded, agronomy budgets get met, and the club doesn't have to pass a special assessment to resurface greens. It is simply a different answer to the question of who votes on your dues — and a buyer is entitled to know which answer they are buying.

What would we look at first if you called us about these two?

Not the amenity lists. We'd start with the same two documents in both communities, then diverge.

First, the current membership schedule in writing from each club's membership office, dated — because everything public on both clubs is between one and seven years old, and we've just shown you how far these numbers travel in a year. Second, the estoppel, because the association's assessment, any capital contribution due at closing, and any pending special assessment live there and nowhere else. Then the paths split. At Wildcat Run, we'd read the association's governing documents and its own financials against the Form 990 series, because when the members own the club, their documents tell you what the next five years of capital spending looks like. At Grandezza, we'd ask the operator a different set of questions — what the dues history has actually been since the 2023 acquisition, what capital work is planned, and what, if anything, is contractually fixed. A club's business plan isn't in a recorded document, so you ask for it directly and note what you're told.

What that produces isn't reassurance, it's a number you can underwrite: the real annual carrying cost, the real cash due at closing, and a clear-eyed view of which way the dues are likely to move. That's the part we take care of for you.

Our recommendation

If your priority is the lowest cost of entry, the widest range of homes, and the flexibility to hold a non-golf tier — Grandezza, with your eyes open about who sets the price.

If your priority is playing golf soon, in a small community, as a member of the body that owns the course, with real financial transparency available before you commit — Wildcat Run, and budget for the higher entry.

Frequently asked questions

Is club membership mandatory at Grandezza in Estero, FL?

Yes, at the Social level. The Club at Grandezza's own 2023 membership packet states that Social membership "is mandatory for all Grandezza residents who do not have higher category of membership." On that 2023 schedule, Social dues were $4,092.82 plus 6.5% Florida sales tax, with a $1,000 annual food and beverage minimum — roughly $5,359 a year before any golf. Because that schedule is from 2023, confirm current figures with the club before you write an offer.

Who owns The Club at Grandezza in Estero?

Heritage Golf Group announced the acquisition on September 13, 2023, buying from Gravitas, LLC; it was Heritage's 33rd club. Heritage was itself acquired by KSL Capital Partners in February 2020, when it held six properties. The practical point for a buyer is that the course is a business asset owned by a national operator, not an amenity owned by the homeowners.

Can I read a golf club's financials before I buy a home there?

Sometimes, and Wildcat Run is the clearer case. Because Wildcat Run Country Club Association Inc is a 501(c)(7) social and recreational club, its IRS Form 990 is public — the FY2025 summary reports revenue of $8,365,133 and assets of $33,778,811. A 990 summary is not a full financial statement, so treat it as scale and direction and ask the club for its own financials.

How much is a golf membership at Wildcat Run in Estero, FL?

The published figures disagree, and the spread is the honest answer. The club's own 2019 schedule listed a $45,000 golf initiation with $12,368.45 in all-in annual dues; an agent page last modified January 4, 2026 lists $100,000 and $20,685, plus a golf membership cap of 350. No current club-published schedule was reachable, so verify directly with the club.
