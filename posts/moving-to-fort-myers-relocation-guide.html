Moving to Fort Myers, FL: the 2026 complete relocation guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 5, 2026·9 min read

Fort Myers, FL is one of Southwest Florida's most practical relocation destinations in 2026 — combining Gulf Coast lifestyle, no Florida state income tax, and a genuine buyer's market where the median home sells for around $329,835 at roughly 5% below asking price. As the county seat of Lee County, Fort Myers offers a range of communities from master-planned gated neighborhoods near RSW airport to luxury waterfront enclaves along the Caloosahatchee River. Out-of-state buyers should plan for Florida-specific costs including homeowners insurance ($3,000-$8,000+/yr), property taxes (~0.79% effective rate with homestead exemption savings), and potential HOA and CDD fees that vary widely by community.

Fort Myers keeps showing up on relocation shortlists, and not just because of the obvious reasons like warm winters and Gulf beaches. It's showing up because, unlike Naples to the south, it's still within reach for a wide range of buyers. And unlike Cape Coral across the river, it has a more urban core, proximity to Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW), and a diverse mix of communities that work for families, retirees, remote workers, and second-home buyers alike.

Why Fort Myers: the case for this market in 2026

Florida's biggest draws are well-documented: no state income tax, a low overall cost of living relative to the Northeast and Midwest, warm weather year-round, and access to the Gulf of Mexico. Fort Myers delivers all of that, but it also has a few specific advantages worth calling out.

First, the market is firmly in buyer's territory right now. The Redfin Compete Score for Fort Myers sits at 28 out of 100 as of mid-2026. Homes are averaging 86 days on market and selling at approximately 95% of list price. About 40% of active listings have seen price reductions. That's a stark contrast to the frenzy of 2021-2022, and it's creating real opportunity for buyers who know how to use it.

Second, RSW is genuinely one of Southwest Florida's greatest practical assets. The airport offers direct flights to over 80 destinations, including nonstop service to Chicago, New York, Cleveland, Detroit, Boston, Pittsburgh, and Philadelphia, which means snowbirds and part-time residents can get here and back without connecting. If you're buying a second home or planning a gradual transition, that matters.

Florida state income tax: None. Florida does not tax personal income, wages, retirement distributions, pension income, or capital gains at the state level. For buyers relocating from high-tax states — New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Michigan, California — this difference alone can represent thousands to tens of thousands of dollars per year in annual savings.

Fort Myers rewards the buyer who does their homework. Flood zones, CDD fee structures, HOA financials, insurance quotes — these aren't fine print. They're the numbers that define your monthly cost and your long-term equity position.

Schools, healthcare, and everyday life

The Lee County School District serves Fort Myers and is the fifth-largest school district in Florida. For buyers with children, research specific schools for the address you're considering rather than the neighborhood name alone — assignment boundaries within the district vary.

Healthcare in Fort Myers is anchored by Lee Health, a major regional health system with multiple hospital campuses, including Gulf Coast Medical Center and Lee Memorial Hospital. Lee Health has announced a major expansion of its Fort Myers campus to meet the region's growing population.

Day-to-day living is well-served. Fort Myers has a complete retail and restaurant ecosystem, multiple Publix and Whole Foods locations, and a growing arts and entertainment scene in the River District. The IMAG History & Science Center, Barbara B. Mann Performing Arts Hall, and the Edison & Ford Winter Estates give the city a cultural depth that smaller SW Florida markets don't have. Spring training baseball (the Boston Red Sox and Minnesota Twins both train in the Fort Myers area) is a seasonal bonus many relocators genuinely enjoy.

Traffic is real. US-41 (Tamiami Trail) and Daniels Parkway back up during snowbird season — January through April — and US-41's intersection with Colonial Boulevard is one of the more congested points in Lee County. I-75 provides the faster north-south spine. If you're commuting or frequently traveling to the airport, proximity to I-75 is worth prioritizing when you're selecting a community.

What out-of-state buyers need to know before closing

Fort Myers is not a complicated real estate market, but it has Florida-specific mechanics that catch out-of-state buyers off guard.

Flood zones are property-specific, not neighborhood-wide. Two homes on the same block can be in different flood zones. Pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) for any property you're serious about. If it's in Zone AE or VE, budget for flood insurance, typically $1,500-$4,000+/year in addition to your standard homeowners policy.

The four-point inspection and wind mitigation inspection are standard. These are separate from the general home inspection and serve your insurance underwriter, not just you. The four-point evaluates the four major systems (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC) and is required by most Florida insurers for homes over 10-15 years old. The wind mitigation inspection documents hurricane resistance features and can earn you meaningful insurance discounts. Budget $300-$500 combined for both.

Title companies handle closings in Florida, not attorneys. Homeowners insurance typically runs $3,000-$8,000+ per year depending on the home's age, construction, flood zone, and wind mitigation status. Homes built after 2002 with hurricane straps and impact-resistant windows or shutters earn meaningful discounts.

Best communities near Fort Myers

Leading communities near Fort Myers include Gateway (master-planned, near RSW, $350K-$650K), Colonial Country Club (golf community, $350K-$700K), Gulf Harbour Yacht & Country Club (luxury waterfront, $600K-$2M+), Pelican Preserve (55+, active adult, $350K-$700K), Verandah (resort-style golf plus river access, $350K-$900K), and Reflection Isles (gated, $350K-$600K). The right fit depends on your lifestyle, budget, and whether you want golf, waterfront access, age-restricted amenities, or proximity to the airport.
