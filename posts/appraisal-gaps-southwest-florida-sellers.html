Appraisal gaps in Southwest Florida: what sellers need to do when a home appraises low in 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·April 24, 2026·6 min read

What should you do if your home appraisal comes in below the contract price in Southwest Florida? When a home appraises below the agreed purchase price, sellers typically have four options: reduce the price, negotiate appraisal gap coverage, split the difference, or challenge the appraisal using stronger comparable sales.

Why appraisal gaps are common in Southwest Florida

If you're selling in Naples, Bonita Springs, Estero, Fort Myers, or Cape Coral, a low appraisal is not unusual in today's market. We're seeing this more often in 2026 because of rapid price movement in micro-markets (especially waterfront and gated communities), limited comparable sales in unique or luxury properties, out-of-area appraisers unfamiliar with hyper-local value differences, and seasonality, where appraisals lag behind peak-season buyer demand.

In simple terms: buyers may be willing to pay more than what past sales support on paper.

The 4 smart strategies for sellers

1. Reduce the price (strategically)

Lowering the price to match the appraisal keeps the deal intact and avoids delays. Best used when you've had extended days on market, you want certainty over risk, or the gap is relatively small.

2. Negotiate appraisal gap coverage

Some buyers — especially in competitive Southwest Florida markets — are willing to bring extra cash. This is common with relocation buyers, cash-heavy buyers, and luxury or second-home purchases.

3. Split the difference

Often the most realistic solution. Example: on a $30,000 gap, the seller reduces $15,000 and the buyer covers $15,000. This keeps both parties invested and moving forward.

4. Offer seller concessions

Instead of lowering price, offer closing cost credits or a rate buydown. This can help the buyer financially while preserving your sale price.

A low appraisal doesn't mean your deal is lost. It means you've reached one of the most important decision points in your sale.

Should you challenge the appraisal?

Yes — if the data supports it. A Reconsideration of Value (ROV) allows you to submit better comparable sales, proof of upgrades or features missed, and local market trends. In Southwest Florida, this is especially important for waterfront properties, golf communities, and unique floorplans or remodeled homes. Timing matters — this should be done immediately.

What happens if you cancel the deal?

Walking away is an option — but it comes with risk. Your days on market increase, you may enter a slower seasonal cycle, and the next buyer may face the same appraisal issue. Unless you have strong evidence the appraisal is wrong, solving the gap is usually the better strategy.

How to avoid appraisal issues before they happen

The best strategy starts before you accept an offer. Smart sellers in Southwest Florida price with appraisal reality, not just buyer emotion; favor cash offers or gap coverage terms; ensure their agent provides a strong comp package to the appraiser; and evaluate the buyer's financial strength, not just their offer price.

Southwest Florida seller insight (2026)

We are seeing more appraisal gaps in waterfront homes in Cape Coral, golf and bundled communities in Estero and Bonita Springs, and move-up and luxury segments in Naples. As prices stabilize and interest rates fluctuate, appraisals are becoming more conservative — making strategy more important than ever.

This is a negotiation moment, not a deal breaker

The right strategy can protect your net proceeds, keep your timeline intact, and preserve a strong buyer. Every appraisal situation is different — especially in a market as nuanced as Southwest Florida. If you're preparing to sell or currently navigating a low appraisal, having a strategy in place before and after the appraisal can make a significant difference in your outcome.
