Moving to Estero, FL: the 2026 complete relocation guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 24, 2026·8 min read

Estero, FL is a planned village in Lee County, positioned between Naples (20 minutes south) and Fort Myers (15 minutes north), with Southwest Florida International Airport less than 5 miles away. In 2026, the median home price is $484,736 — down 7% year-over-year, creating favorable conditions for relocating buyers. Most of Estero's housing sits in FEMA Flood Zone X (lower flood risk), and the area features 25+ gated communities ranging from affordable to ultra-luxury.

HOA and insurance: what it really costs

Every gated community in Estero carries HOA fees, and the range is wide. Lower-amenity communities might run $300-$500/month. Full-resort communities like Miromar Lakes and WildBlue can run $800-$1,200/month or more when you account for all components. Some communities also carry CDD fees — Community Development District assessments that fund infrastructure and appear on your property tax bill as a separate line item. Always verify the full monthly cost picture before making an offer.

Here's the honest truth: Florida homeowner's insurance has become significantly more expensive over the past several years, and that's unlikely to fully reverse. In Estero, being in FEMA Zone X helps — you're generally not required to carry flood insurance, though many buyers choose to anyway. Wind coverage is a different matter; budget for it. When we run relocation cost projections for clients, we typically plan for $4,000-$8,000/year in insurance depending on the home, its age, and its construction type. If you're coming from Ohio, Michigan, Illinois, or New York, the income tax savings alone can offset a meaningful portion of your annual housing costs.

This is where relocation buyers most often get tripped up — not on the lifestyle, but on the process.

Schools, healthcare, and everyday life

If you have school-age children — less common among Estero's primary buyer profile, but certainly not unusual — the area is served by Pinewoods Elementary, Three Oaks Middle School, and Estero High School. Florida Gulf Coast University (FGCU) sits on Estero's eastern edge, which adds cultural programming and events that surprise a lot of newcomers.

Healthcare is strong and growing. Lee Health Coconut Point is right in the heart of Estero with emergency and specialty services. Southwest Florida Proton Therapy Center, which opened in December 2025, brings specialized cancer treatment to the region for the first time — a meaningful addition for a community with an older median demographic. NCH and Cleveland Clinic Naples are accessible within 20-30 minutes to the south for additional specialist access.

For everyday life: Coconut Point Mall anchors the area's dining and retail scene and is walkable from several Estero communities. Miromar Outlets is a short drive. For serious cultural amenities — performing arts, major museums, specialty medical — Naples and Fort Myers both fill that role within easy distance.

One honest note: Estero is car-dependent. There is no walkable street grid. You will drive to everything. During the November-April season, US 41 and Ben Hill Griffin Parkway can be genuinely frustrating at peak hours. It's manageable once you know the workarounds, but plan for it.

Matching your lifestyle to the right community

WildBlue offers boating and watersports on its 500-acre recreational lake. Verdana Village is known for its indoor sports complex, including pickleball courts. Corkscrew Shores offers kayaking, paddleboarding, and a lakefront pavilion. The right fit depends on your lifestyle priorities — we recommend touring two or three before committing to one.

Frequently asked questions

How bad are hurricanes in Estero, FL?

Hurricane season runs June through November. Estero is an inland community — it doesn't face the direct coastal exposure of Fort Myers Beach or Marco Island — but major storms still bring wind damage and potential flooding. Most of Estero sits in FEMA Flood Zone X, a lower-risk designation, though storm surge from major events can extend inland. Proper wind coverage is essential, and many communities require current 4-point and wind mitigation inspections at purchase.

Is 2026 a good time to buy in Estero, FL?

As of mid-2026, Estero is in a buyer's market. The median home price has dropped approximately 7% year-over-year to $484,736, and homes are averaging 71 days on the market before going under contract. Sellers are actively negotiating on price and terms. That said, market conditions shift, and the right time to buy ultimately depends on your personal timeline and financial readiness, not on trying to call a bottom.
