Gateway, Fort Myers FL: which neighborhood fits your budget in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 9, 2026·11 min read

Gateway is not one homeowners association — it's more than a dozen. This roughly 3,000-acre master-planned community in Lee County is governed at the infrastructure level by the Gateway Services Community Development District (CDD), established in 1986, but each named neighborhood inside Gateway — Pinewood Lakes, Sabal Dunes, The Plantation, Stoneybrook at Gateway, Fairway Isles, Summergate, and others — has its own separate HOA, dues, and rules. Homes across Gateway span the high $100,000s to the $500,000s, with a 2026 trailing-12-month median sold price of $512,500. Before you make an offer, you need to know which specific sub-neighborhood a listing sits in, because that answers the HOA question — "Gateway" alone does not.

Buyers searching for homes in Gateway run into this confusion constantly: they'll see one listing with a higher monthly HOA fee and another two streets over with a lower one, both "in Gateway." That's not a data error. It's the nature of the community, and it's the first thing you need to understand before you compare a single home price against anything else on the market.

Gateway at a glance

WCI Communities founded Gateway in the mid-1980s on roughly 3,000 acres just outside the Fort Myers city limits, in the 33913 ZIP code. The Gateway Services Community Development District was established on May 22, 1986, under Chapter 190 of the Florida Statutes — the same legal framework behind CDDs throughout Southwest Florida — and it's run by a five-member Board of Supervisors elected by residents.

The CDD doesn't manage your lawn or your gate code. It funds and maintains the infrastructure every property in Gateway shares:

  • Stormwater management
  • Potable and irrigation water supply
  • Street lighting
  • The Commons Pool
  • The Sherman Soccer Complex
  • More than 15 miles of walking, jogging, and biking trails

Everything beyond that — landscaping, private pools, gated entries, reserve funds, architectural review — falls to whichever HOA governs the specific neighborhood a home sits in. That's the distinction almost every national real estate site glosses over when it lists Gateway as a single community.

Gateway rewards the buyer who asks "which neighborhood?" before "how much?" The sub-association, not the community name, is what actually sets your monthly cost.

Gateway's location is one of its strongest selling points for buyers relocating from out of state: it sits on strategically high, well-drained ground, with easy access to I-75, Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW), and reasonable commutes into both downtown Fort Myers and Cape Coral.

Homes and pricing across Gateway's neighborhoods

Because Gateway spans so many distinct subdivisions, its price range is wide by design — not because any one neighborhood is inconsistent. As of our research for this guide:

  • Overall range: high $100,000s to the $500,000s
  • Trailing 12-month median sold price (through September 2026): $512,500
  • Median price per square foot: $198
  • Median days on market: 100
  • Sale-to-list ratio: homes are selling at roughly 95% of original asking price
  • Long-term trend: median prices have risen about 60% since 2014 and sit roughly flat compared to the 2022 peak of around $520,000

That 100-day median is worth sitting with. It's longer than what you'll see in newer, single-HOA communities nearby, and part of the reason is Gateway's age and variety — buyers take longer to compare across sub-neighborhoods with different fee structures, home vintages, and amenity packages before committing.

Home types vary just as much as price. Condominium and townhome-style neighborhoods, such as Summergate, tend to anchor the lower end of Gateway's price range, while single-family sections like Stoneybrook at Gateway and The Plantation typically run toward the middle and upper end. If you're comparing two Gateway listings and the price-per-square-foot numbers don't line up, check whether you're actually comparing the same type of neighborhood — you're often not.

On property tax: at the current median sold price, expect roughly $8,180 a year before any exemption, or about $7,520 with a full homestead exemption, using Lee County's current millage rate of 15.97. If you're moving from another Florida homestead, ask us about Save Our Homes portability before you assume your new tax bill will match your old one — the math rarely works out even.

Amenities and lifestyle: who Gateway fits

The centerpiece for golfers is The Club at Gateway, a private 18-hole course designed by Tom Fazio and opened in 1989. Membership is separate from any HOA due and isn't bundled into your closing costs the way it is in some bundled-membership communities — budget for it separately, and confirm current initiation and dues figures directly with the club, since public listings don't publish them. Beyond golf, the club offers bocce ball, pickleball, and tennis.

Outside the private club, the CDD-run amenities are open to residents across Gateway regardless of which HOA governs their street: the Commons Pool, the Sherman Soccer Complex, trail access, and several neighborhood amenity centers. There's also a business park within Gateway's boundaries, which is unusual for a residential master plan this size and adds a layer of local employment and services most purely residential communities don't have.

On schools, Gateway includes four public Lee County schools within its boundaries that offer the Cambridge AICE (Advanced International Certificate of Education) program, along with STEM and arts coursework. Crosspointe Academy operates as a private K-12 option inside the community. As with any school question, confirm current attendance zones and program availability directly with the Lee County School District before you buy — zone lines shift, and we never want you making a six-figure decision on outdated boundary data.

Gateway tends to fit buyers who want golf-course and resort-style amenity access without paying resort-level HOA dues across the board, and who are comfortable doing a bit more homework in exchange for more flexibility in price point and home style. If you want one clean number to budget around instead, keep reading — that's exactly where Hampton Park and Colonial Country Club come in below.

The buying process in Gateway: why "which neighborhood" matters more than "which listing"

This is the step buyers skip, and it's the one that causes the most surprise at closing.

Before you fall in love with a listing, confirm the exact sub-neighborhood name — not just "Gateway." Ask your agent or check the MLS listing for the specific subdivision (Pinewood Lakes, Sabal Dunes, Stoneybrook at Gateway, and so on). That name is what tells you which HOA actually governs the property.

Once you know the sub-neighborhood, here's what to do before you write an offer:

  1. Request the HOA estoppel letter from that specific association — not a generic "Gateway HOA." Florida law requires associations to deliver an estoppel certificate within 10 business days of a written request, and it will show current dues, any pending special assessments, and whether the account is in good standing.
  2. Confirm the CDD assessment on the property's tax bill. It shows up as a non-ad valorem line item on the Lee County Property Appraiser's record for that specific parcel — pull it for the actual home, not for "Gateway" as a whole, since bond repayment schedules can vary by platted unit and phase.
  3. Order a four-point inspection and wind mitigation inspection if the home is more than a few years old, both to support your insurance quote and to flag deferred maintenance before you're under contract.
  4. Get a flood elevation certificate for the specific parcel rather than relying on Gateway's "high and dry" reputation. Marketing language is a starting point, not a substitute for a FEMA flood zone determination on the actual lot.
  5. Review the Seller's Property Disclosure closely for any mention of past insurance claims, assessments, or HOA disputes specific to that sub-neighborhood.

If you've never gone through a Florida HOA estoppel process before, our guide to evaluating a Florida HOA before you buy walks through exactly what that letter should tell you and what red flags to watch for. And if the CDD assessment line on a tax bill has you asking how that number was calculated in the first place, our CDD fees guide for Bonita Springs and Estero buyers breaks down the difference between the bond portion and the operations-and-maintenance portion — the same structure applies here.

We'll be direct about one thing: we could not find a single published dollar figure for either the current CDD assessment or any individual sub-neighborhood's HOA dues during our research for this guide. That's not unusual for a community this fragmented, but it means you should treat any number you see on a listing site as a starting point, not a final answer. The estoppel letter and the Property Appraiser's record are the only sources we'd ever rely on before writing an offer.

How Gateway compares to Hampton Park and Colonial Country Club

If Gateway's layered structure sounds like more homework than you want, two nearby alternatives are worth a look.

Hampton Park, just outside Gateway, operates under a single HOA rather than a patchwork of sub-associations. It's a newer, Pulte-built community, and its market moves differently: a trailing-12-month median sold price around $555,000, a median of 47 days on market, and roughly 30 sales in the past year — several times Gateway's sales volume in the same window. For buyers who want one predictable monthly number and a more liquid resale market, Hampton Park trades some of Gateway's price flexibility for simplicity.

Colonial Country Club, closer to central Fort Myers, offers a different trade-off: a single country club structure with bundled golf and social membership rather than Gateway's separate, pay-as-you-choose approach to The Club at Gateway. We cover that community in detail in our Colonial Country Club buyer's guide, including its Golf Village and Lakes Village distinction — a useful read if bundled club membership matters more to you than Gateway's broader price range.

The honest answer for most buyers: Gateway makes sense if you want access to a wide range of price points and home styles under one general location, and you're willing to confirm fees neighborhood by neighborhood. Hampton Park or Colonial Country Club make more sense if you'd rather have one clean number from day one, even if it means a narrower set of homes to choose from.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Gateway in Fort Myers have one HOA fee?

No. Gateway is governed by a single Community Development District (CDD) for shared infrastructure like roads, water, and the Commons Pool, but each named sub-neighborhood inside Gateway — Pinewood Lakes, Stoneybrook at Gateway, The Plantation, and others — has its own separate HOA with its own dues. Always confirm the fee for the specific sub-neighborhood a listing sits in, not for Gateway as a whole.

What is the Gateway Services Community Development District and what does it pay for?

The Gateway Services CDD is a special taxing district established in 1986 under Florida Statute Chapter 190. It funds and maintains shared infrastructure across the community, including stormwater management, potable and irrigation water, street lighting, the Commons Pool, the Sherman Soccer Complex, and the trail system. It does not cover private landscaping, gated entries, or individual neighborhood amenities — those come through each sub-neighborhood's own HOA.

Is Gateway in a flood zone?

Gateway is often marketed as a "high and dry" location, but flood zone designation varies by specific parcel. Before you buy, request a flood elevation certificate for the exact home rather than relying on the community's general reputation, and confirm the parcel's FEMA flood zone designation through Lee County's GIS records.

Is The Club at Gateway open to the public?

No. The Club at Gateway is a private 18-hole golf course designed by Tom Fazio and opened in 1989, with membership required for golf access. It also offers bocce ball, pickleball, and tennis. Membership dues and initiation fees are separate from any HOA payment and from Gateway's CDD assessment, so budget for them independently if golf access matters to you.

What's the best way to compare homes across Gateway's different neighborhoods?

Start by identifying the exact sub-neighborhood for each listing you're considering, then compare homes within the same sub-neighborhood or similar home type (condo, townhome, or single-family) rather than across Gateway as a whole. Price per square foot, HOA dues, and amenity access can all differ significantly between neighborhoods just a few streets apart.
