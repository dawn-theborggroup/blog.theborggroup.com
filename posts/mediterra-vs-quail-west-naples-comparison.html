Mediterra vs. Quail West, Naples FL: which luxury golf community is right for you? (2026)

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 25, 2026·9 min read

Both Mediterra and Quail West sit in the prestigious North Naples corridor along Livingston Road, serve buyers well above $1 million, and offer private-club golf with world-class amenities. The key differences come down to golf structure, beach access, and carrying costs. Mediterra has two Tom Fazio courses and a private beach club on Little Hickory Island but carries an 8-year golf waitlist and reset its membership fees significantly in August 2026. Quail West delivers equity golf with a transferable membership, no CDD fees, and a more intimate single-club atmosphere — at a higher average price per square foot. The right answer depends entirely on how you weight those trade-offs.

The two most frequently compared luxury golf communities in North Naples aren't Grey Oaks. They're not Pelican Bay. For the buyer who's already decided they want a private-club lifestyle in the 34110 corridor, the conversation almost always comes down to two communities: Mediterra and Quail West.

Both sit within a few miles of each other along Livingston Road. Both serve buyers well above the $1 million range. Both have clubhouses, tennis, pickleball, and private golf. And both have waitlists. But they are not the same community — and choosing wrong can cost you years of frustration and tens of thousands of dollars in the wrong membership.

Market snapshot: Mediterra vs. Quail West

Quail West: average sold price $4.447M at $920/sqft, roughly 5 months of supply, 100 days on market average, record sale $11.3M (April 2026). Fully built out — resale only, no new construction available. Estate homes ranging roughly 4,000-7,000 sqft.

On a pure price-per-square-foot basis, Quail West runs about $70/sqft more than Mediterra. Quail West also skews toward larger estate homes with fewer smaller entry-point units in inventory. If you're targeting the 34110 corridor near $1M, Mediterra is more likely to have options. If you're in the $3M-$7M range for a large estate, Quail West delivers more selection.

Both markets are showing healthy demand. Neither is a buyer's market in the traditional sense — well-priced listings in both communities move.

They are not the same community — and choosing wrong can cost you years of frustration and tens of thousands of dollars in the wrong membership.

Membership costs: the number most buyers underestimate

Membership costs are where most buyers get surprised, and where the two communities differ most significantly.

Mediterra membership tiers (August 2026 fee reset — all tiers increased):

MembershipInitiationAnnual Dues
Full Golf$350,000~$25,700/yr
Limited Golf$300,000—
Sports & Beach$175,000~$10,300/yr
Lifestyle$87,500~$4,000/yr

⚠️ Important: Mediterra reset its membership fees in August 2026. The Full Golf initiation rose from $250,000 to $350,000 — a $100,000 increase. If you're buying a Mediterra listing that includes a transferred membership, confirm membership transfer terms before you make an offer; it can be worth more than the difference between competing offers on the same property.

The Full Golf waitlist at Mediterra is approximately 8 years as of August 2026. Most buyers entering today will choose Sports & Beach or Lifestyle membership and join the golf waitlist from there.

Quail West membership tiers:

MembershipInitiationAnnual Dues
Equity Golf$250,000~$24,940/yr

Quail West's Equity Golf membership is capped and full — but the waitlist here is generally more predictable than Mediterra's 8-year timeline. The equity structure means your membership has tangible value; when you sell, you may be able to transfer it with the property. House/Social membership is available without a long wait.

Annual carrying cost comparison for context — a buyer entering at Sports & Beach (Mediterra) vs. House/Social (Quail West): Mediterra's HOA (~$9,060/yr) plus Sports & Beach (~$10,300/yr) totals approximately $19,360/yr in club/HOA fees, before CDD. Quail West carries no CDD fee at all, which simplifies the comparison meaningfully once you factor in Mediterra's parcel-specific CDD assessment on top of that total.

Beach access vs. clubhouse scale

Mediterra's private beach club on Little Hickory Island is genuinely significant for buyers who want beach access as part of their club lifestyle without buying directly on the water — it gives Mediterra members something no other golf community in this corridor offers.

Quail West counters with a larger clubhouse — 100,000 sqft to Mediterra's 60,000 — and what regulars describe as a tighter, more socially cohesive membership. With just 660 homes feeding one clubhouse, everyone tends to know each other. At Mediterra, with 31 sub-neighborhoods spread across nearly 2,000 acres, the social energy is more diffuse.

If golf is the primary driver and you want variety, Mediterra's two-course structure is a meaningful advantage. If you want one excellent course and a close-knit social atmosphere, Quail West is hard to beat.

Who each community is best for

Buy in Mediterra if: beach club access is a genuine lifestyle priority — it's Mediterra's clearest differentiator; you want two Tom Fazio courses, or simply two courses to rotate between; you're comfortable entering at Sports & Beach or Lifestyle membership and joining the golf waitlist; new construction is appealing — London Bay is still actively building in Mediterra; you prefer a larger community feel with 31 distinct neighborhoods, preserve views, and more privacy between homes.

Buy in Quail West if: equity golf with a transferable membership structure matters to you; you prefer one flagship course and a tighter, more intimate social club atmosphere; you're targeting a larger estate home in the $3M-$7M+ range; no CDD fees simplifies your carrying cost model; red clay tennis and an indoor pool are important amenities for you.

Buying process: what to know before you make an offer

Both communities require HOA approval before closing. This involves a buyer application, background check, and in some cases an interview or visit. Plan for 30-60 days in most cases — this process needs to start the day you go under contract, not the week before closing.

At Mediterra, confirm whether an existing membership transfers with the property. Given the August 2026 fee reset, a transferred Golf Membership (bypassing the $350,000 new initiation) adds significant value to the transaction. Address this in the contract explicitly.

At Quail West, verify waitlist position for Equity Golf membership if regular course access is your priority. House/Social membership is immediately available, but if you expect to be playing regularly within your first year in the community, confirm realistic timelines before you commit.

Both communities span two counties — Collier and Lee. This affects your property tax calculation, your homestead exemption application, and which county's title insurance customs apply at closing. You'll file your homestead exemption with whichever county contains the majority of your parcel — confirm this with the listing agent and verify on the appropriate county property appraiser's website before you close.
