Stoneybrook in Estero, FL: is it still one of the best values in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 2, 2026·9 min read

If you've been circling Estero's golf communities and flinching at the entry price — Grandezza's mandatory $5,300-a-year club dues, WildBlue's seven-figure waterfront minimums — Stoneybrook is worth a second look. This gated community, built out between 2000 and 2007 around an 18-hole public golf course, charges no mandatory membership at all: you pay to play when you want to play, and nothing when you don't. HOA dues run about $235 a month, there's no Community Development District fee in the records we reviewed, and a sample of addresses across the community came back 100% in FEMA's Zone X — the designation that keeps flood insurance off your required-purchases list with a federally backed mortgage.

That combination — low carrying costs, no club obligation, and a clean flood map — is the first thing worth knowing about Stoneybrook, because it's the first thing that surprises buyers who assume every golf community on this stretch of Corkscrew Road comes with a country club price tag attached. Here's what we tell clients who ask us about this one, and how it stacks up against Wildcat Run and Grandezza, the two communities right next door.

Stoneybrook at a glance: pay-as-you-play golf and a clean flood map

Stoneybrook sits east of I-75 along Corkscrew Road in Estero's 33928 zip code — directly across the street from Grandezza and adjacent to Wildcat Run, which makes it one of three golf communities buyers in this stretch almost always cross-shop against each other.

Construction ran primarily from 2000 through 2007, with the bulk of homes built in the early 2000s. Like Grandezza, Stoneybrook is fully built out today, so every purchase runs through the resale market rather than a builder's sales office — there's no design center, no incentive timing to negotiate around, and no new-construction warranty to lean on.

The community's 18-hole, par-72 public golf course — designed by Gordy Lewis and Jed Azinger and opened in 1999 — is one of the longer courses in Southwest Florida, and listings describe it as beginner-friendly. The defining feature, and the one buyers ask us about most, is access: it's pay-as-you-play. There's no mandatory club membership, no initiation fee, and no tiered structure to sort through the way there is at Grandezza. You can live in Stoneybrook and never set foot on the course, or play as often as you like and pay greens fees as you go.

The question we get most about Stoneybrook isn't "is the golf any good" — it's "what's the catch?" There usually isn't one. No mandatory club dues, no CDD fee we could find, and a flood map that works in your favor. It's one of the more straightforward carrying-cost pictures in Estero.

On flood risk — a question we're hearing more from Southwest Florida buyers after a few active hurricane seasons — the sample of Stoneybrook listing addresses we reviewed came back 100% in Zone X, FEMA's designation for areas outside the Special Flood Hazard Area. That doesn't eliminate flood risk entirely, and flood zone designations are property-specific rather than neighborhood-wide, so pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map for the exact address you're considering before you rely on it. But it's a meaningfully different starting point than many coastal and canal-front Bonita Springs communities, and it's one more reason Stoneybrook's insurance costs tend to run lower than buyers expect. For the fuller picture on how flood zone status affects your total insurance bill, see our guide to flood insurance costs in Southwest Florida.

Homes and pricing: what a 2026 purchase actually costs

Stoneybrook's housing stock mixes single-family homes, low-rise condominiums (concentrated along Lancaster Run), and attached villas (Portrush Run, Windham Run, and similar streets), built mostly between 2000 and 2007. Estimates of the total home count vary depending on whether condos and villas are counted alongside single-family parcels — inventory records we reviewed put the number anywhere from roughly 400 to 650 — so treat any total as an approximation rather than a precise count.

As of October 2026, active listings span roughly $250,000 for a two-bedroom condo up to around $670,000 for a larger single-family home, with recent single-family sales clustering in the $400,000s to $500,000s. Square footage runs from about 1,000 square feet in the smaller condos to 3,000-plus in the largest single-family floor plans.

On carrying costs:

  • Single-family HOA dues we confirmed on recent listings run $706 per quarter — about $235 a month, or roughly $2,824 a year — and include cable and high-speed internet through Xfinity, which is low for an Estero golf community.
  • Condo buyers should confirm their specific association's fee structure separately. Units along Lancaster Run and similar condo buildings may carry a distinct condo association assessment on top of or instead of the master HOA figure above — ask your agent to pull the exact budget for the building, not just the community-wide number.
  • No CDD fee turned up in the records we reviewed for Stoneybrook, which — like Grandezza — would set it apart from newer CDD-financed communities such as Verdana Village or Corkscrew Shores. Confirm CDD status directly on the Lee County property tax bill for the specific address, since it's parcel-specific.
  • No mandatory golf or club dues — the single biggest cost difference from Grandezza, two minutes away.

Recent market activity shows median sold prices around $420,000 to $505,000 depending on the data window and product mix, with homes trading noticeably softer than the 2021–2022 peak — one analysis put the community down roughly 12% year-over-year, alongside a broader cooling across Lee County's resale market. That's good news if you're buying: homes are sitting longer than they did a few years ago, which gives buyers real room to negotiate on price and terms.

Amenities and lifestyle: who Stoneybrook fits

Beyond the golf course, Stoneybrook's amenity package centers on a renovated clubhouse with a fitness room and library, three community pools and a spa, courts for tennis, pickleball, and bocce, plus basketball, volleyball, horseshoe pits, and baseball and soccer fields. Walking and biking paths wind through the community, and the gated entrance is staffed.

That field-and-playground mix is a tell: unlike Pelican Preserve or Valencia Bonita, Stoneybrook is not an age-restricted community. It's a family-friendly, all-ages neighborhood that happens to be built around a golf course — a different lifestyle fit than Estero's 55+ options, and worth knowing whether you specifically want, or specifically want to avoid, a community with school-age kids and youth sports fields in regular use.

The location puts you close to Miromar Outlets, Hertz Arena, Coconut Point, and Florida Gulf Coast University, with RSW International Airport roughly 15 to 20 minutes away — similar proximity to what Grandezza and Wildcat Run offer, since all three sit within a few minutes of each other on Corkscrew Road.

The buying process: resale due diligence on a built-out community

Because Stoneybrook sold out of new construction years ago, buying here means a standard Florida resale transaction — typically the FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract — negotiated through your own buyer's agent rather than a builder's on-site representative. A few things to confirm before you write an offer:

  • Request the HOA estoppel letter. Florida law requires the seller's association to provide one before closing, showing what's currently owed, any pending special assessments, and whether the account is in good standing.
  • Ask for the HOA's budget and reserve study, not just the current due. Our guide to evaluating a Florida HOA before you buy walks through exactly what to request and why a thin reserve fund today can turn into a special assessment in a few years, especially on a community built more than 20 years ago.
  • If you're buying a condo, get the condo association's budget and reserve study separately from the master community HOA, and ask specifically about any pending or recent special assessments — Florida's post-Surfside condo reserve requirements have pushed some older associations into catch-up funding.
  • Order a full home inspection. With most of Stoneybrook's housing stock now 20 to 25 years old, roofs, HVAC systems, and pool equipment are the kind of deferred-maintenance items a seller discloses but doesn't warranty.
  • Order a four-point inspection and wind mitigation inspection. Both are typically required by Florida insurers for homes in this age range and can meaningfully lower your premium if the home has hurricane-rated features.
  • If golf access matters to you, confirm current greens fees and any resident-rate structure directly with the course operator before you assume pricing — pay-as-you-play terms can change.

Walk the specific section you're considering — single-family, villa, or condo — rather than the community as a whole before you make an offer, since fees, building age, and association structure can vary block to block.

How Stoneybrook compares to Wildcat Run and Grandezza

Three golf communities sit within a few minutes of each other on this stretch of Corkscrew Road, and buyers cross-shop them constantly:

Grandezza, directly across the street, requires mandatory baseline country club dues of roughly $5,300 a year on top of a separate neighborhood HOA, with golf membership itself a separate, capped tier. It's the most amenity-rich of the three — a 53,000-square-foot clubhouse, six Har-Tru tennis courts, two fitness centers — but it asks for ongoing financial commitment whether or not you golf.

Wildcat Run, next door, sits between the two on structure: a single-tier membership community with no golf waitlist, built around one Arnold Palmer-designed course, with single-family homes from roughly $425,000 to $2.3 million.

Stoneybrook is the value play of the three: the lowest HOA dues, no club obligation of any kind, and a genuinely pay-as-you-play relationship with its own course. What you give up is scale — fewer amenities than Grandezza's full country club package, and a less name-recognized course than Wildcat Run's Arnold Palmer design. If low fixed costs and flexibility matter more to you than amenity breadth, Stoneybrook is worth putting at the top of your list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is golf membership required to live in Stoneybrook?

No. Stoneybrook's 18-hole public golf course operates on a pay-as-you-play basis with no mandatory club membership, no initiation fee, and no tiered dues structure. You pay greens fees only when you play, which is a meaningfully different model than neighboring Grandezza's mandatory country club dues.

Does Stoneybrook have a CDD fee?

We did not find a Community Development District assessment tied to Stoneybrook in the sources we reviewed. Confirm this directly against the Lee County property tax bill for the specific address you're considering, since CDD status is parcel-specific.

Is Stoneybrook in a flood zone?

A sample of listing addresses across Stoneybrook came back 100% in Zone X, FEMA's designation for areas outside the Special Flood Hazard Area, meaning flood insurance is not mandatory with a federally backed mortgage. Flood zone status is property-specific, so pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map for the exact address before you rely on it.

Is Stoneybrook an age-restricted community?

No. Stoneybrook is an all-ages, family-friendly community with playgrounds, a baseball field, and soccer fields in regular use, unlike age-restricted Estero options such as Pelican Preserve or Valencia Bonita.

How much are HOA fees in Stoneybrook?

Single-family HOA dues confirmed on recent listings run $706 per quarter, or roughly $235 a month, and include cable and high-speed internet. Condo buyers should confirm their specific building's association fee separately, since it may differ from the master community figure.
