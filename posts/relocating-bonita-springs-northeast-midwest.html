Relocating to Bonita Springs from the Northeast or Midwest: what out-of-state buyers need to know

By Mark & Dawn Borg·May 28, 2026·10 min read

Out-of-state buyers relocating to Bonita Springs should plan for several financial factors that differ significantly from most northern states: your property taxes reset fully to your purchase price on day one, homeowners and flood insurance combined typically run $5,000–$9,000+ per year depending on the property, and Florida's March 1 homestead exemption deadline can save $750–$1,200+ annually if you don't miss it. As of May 2026, Bonita Springs is a buyer's market — homes are sitting at a median of 139 days on market — giving out-of-state buyers time to make a thorough, informed decision. Getting insurance quotes before making an offer is essential.

If you're coming from New York, New Jersey, Ohio, Michigan, or anywhere north of the Mason-Dixon line, you already know what's pulling you here — no state income tax, Gulf Coast beaches, year-round outdoor living. What northern buyers consistently underestimate is the financial picture that comes with ownership: insurance, property taxes, flood zones, HOA and CDD assessments. This isn't a warning against buying — it's the honest orientation we give every out-of-state client.

The financial surprises that catch out-of-state buyers off guard

Your property taxes won't match what the seller is paying. Florida assesses at full market value at the time of sale — the seller's Save Our Homes cap disappears, and your assessed value resets to your purchase price. Your first-year bill will almost certainly be higher than what the seller paid. See our homestead exemption and property tax guide for the full mechanics, including effective rates by county.

File for homestead exemption immediately. If Bonita Springs will be your primary residence, the exemption reduces assessed value by up to $50,000 and reactivates the Save Our Homes cap going forward. The deadline is March 1 of the year following your purchase — miss it and you wait a full year. Some Bonita Springs zip codes fall in Collier County rather than Lee County, so confirm which appraiser's office applies.

CDD fees surprise buyers in newer communities. Many Bonita Springs and Estero neighborhoods built in the last 20 years sit within Community Development Districts, adding $1,500–$4,000+ per year on top of HOA fees. Get this number upfront — see our full CDD fees guide.

The insurance equation — what northern buyers typically don't expect

You'll typically need three separate coverages. Homeowners insurance runs $3,600–$6,200+/year in Lee and Collier Counties depending on size, age, construction, and coastal distance. Wind coverage is sometimes bundled, sometimes not — hurricane deductibles are typically 2–5% of your insured dwelling value, not a flat dollar amount, so a 2% deductible on a $600,000 home means $12,000 out of pocket before coverage kicks in. Flood insurance is separate from homeowners coverage entirely, required in FEMA high-risk zones, running $1,000–$3,000+/year depending on your elevation certificate.

Two homes on the same block can have premiums that differ by $2,000 or more per year, based on flood zone, roof age, roof shape, impact windows, and elevation. Get quotes before you make an offer, not after.

Factors that meaningfully reduce cost: newer construction, a hip or metal roof, impact-rated windows and doors, and higher elevation documented with a certificate. The good news: Florida's insurance market has been stabilizing in 2026 — new carriers have entered, Citizens has reduced rates in places, and private options are expanding — but it still requires active management on your part as a buyer.

Understanding the Bonita Springs market in 2026

The timing is favorable for out-of-state buyers. As of May 2026, active inventory is up roughly 22% year-over-year, median days on market has stretched to 139, the sale-to-list ratio sits around 94% (most buyers negotiating below asking), and only 1.7% of homes are selling above list — a sharp contrast to 2021–2022. Median home prices run around $565,000, with condos starting in the low $300,000s and waterfront estates extending well past $5 million. You're not being rushed — there's time to visit, evaluate neighborhoods, and model total ownership costs.

One thing to understand about "season": Bonita Springs sees a significant population surge November through April as snowbirds return — traffic thickens, wait times grow, and desirable listings see more competition. Visit in July or August and you'll see the quieter side, also accurate and worth understanding before you commit. Many out-of-state clients start with a virtual search phase before a focused 3–5 day trip — see our buying sight unseen guide if you're deciding from a distance.

Finding your neighborhood fit

Bonita Springs isn't one community — it's a collection of distinct neighborhoods with different price points and lifestyles.

Bonita Bay is a 2,400-acre master-planned gated community with 90 holes of championship golf, a marina with Gulf access, 10 pickleball courts, an 18-court tennis facility, private beach access, and 12 miles of trails, spread across 60+ distinct neighborhoods. Prices range from the $400,000s for condos to well over $9 million for top waterfront properties — the benchmark if comprehensive amenities in a gated setting are your priority.

Bonita Beach — near Bonita Beach Road and Hickory Boulevard — offers direct Gulf frontage and canal-front properties with boat access. Hickory Boulevard carries the highest price tags, with real tradeoffs in flood zone exposure; an elevation certificate is essential for every property here. See our full Bonita Beach buyer's guide for the details.

Estero and surrounding communities like Pelican Landing, Bonita Del Sol, and Valencia Bonita offer newer construction, resort-style amenities, and more accessible price points for buyers coming from markets where $500,000 felt like a high number — with a quieter, more suburban character but easy access to the Coconut Point corridor.

Most gated communities involve an HOA buyer approval process — applications, background checks, sometimes a board interview. Build 30–60 extra days into your closing timeline; see our HOA approval guide for what to expect.
