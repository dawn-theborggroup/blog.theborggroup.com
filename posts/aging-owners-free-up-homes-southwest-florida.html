Will aging owners free up homes in Southwest Florida?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 6, 2026·7 min read

Will aging homeowners free up homes in Southwest Florida? Nationally, yes, but gradually, and mostly family-sized homes. A new Realtor.com report projects that Baby Boomer and Silent Generation owners will release about 13.9 million homes between 2026 and 2036, a 74% jump from the previous decade. Only about 380,000 of those are starter homes, and the report doesn't promise a local flood of listings. If you're buying or selling here, the takeaway is a steadier supply of 3- and 4-bedroom homes over time, not an overnight shift.

Florida Realtors shared the findings this week, and we've already had buyers and sellers asking what it means for them. Here's our plain-English read.

What did the Realtor.com report actually find?

Realtor.com's analysis, released October 5, 2026, uses American Community Survey data from 2014 to 2024 to track how long owners of different ages hold onto their homes, then projects forward to 2036. The headline numbers:

  • 13.9 million homes released by Baby Boomer and Silent Generation owners from 2026 to 2036, about 74% more than the prior decade.
  • A rising pace: roughly 1.27 million homes in 2027, growing to about 1.52 million in 2036.
  • A shrinking footprint: these generations own about 36.7 million homes today, projected to fall to about 22.8 million by 2036.

"Released" is broader than "listed for sale." It covers homes that change hands when owners pass away, move into care, combine households, or convert a home to a rental. Not every released home lands on the market as a traditional listing.

Which homes are coming, and which aren't?

This is the part most buyers miss. The homes are not spread evenly across sizes:

  • Starter homes (0 to 2 bedrooms): about 380,000 released, a small slice of the total.
  • Family-sized homes (3 to 4 bedrooms): about 9.9 million, roughly 71% of the total.
  • Large homes (5+ bedrooms): about 3.6 million.

Why so few starters? Owners of smaller homes tend to hold on longer, and the report notes that nearly three-quarters of starter-home owners in their 70s own their home free and clear. With no mortgage, there's less pressure to sell. The report's economist put it plainly: the handoff is welcome relief but not big enough to solve the housing shortage alone.

Will this change the market in Southwest Florida?

The Realtor.com report doesn't break out Florida, Naples, Fort Myers, or Cape Coral, so we won't pretend to give you a local number. What we can tell you comes from related research and our own market data.

  • Turnover here tends to be gradual. A National Association of Home Builders analysis covered by Florida Realtors in May found that in Naples-Marco Island, about 49% of households are led by someone 65 or older, and that many longtime owners stay put because they own outright, face high care costs, or lack affordable places to downsize. That points to a slow trickle, not a sudden wave.
  • Newcomers keep absorbing homes. The same research notes that continued retiree migration soaks up much of the supply in markets like ours.
  • Buyers already have leverage. As of mid-2026, homes in Bonita Springs average more than 75 days on the market, with about 37% of active listings carrying price reductions. In Estero, the median price is down roughly 7% year over year and homes average about 71 days on market. See our guide to selling in today's market.

So don't plan around a wave that may or may not arrive on your timeline. Plan around your own numbers.

What should you do with this information?

If you're buying:

  • Expect more 3- and 4-bedroom resale options to surface over time, and fewer starter homes. If you're after a smaller home, don't count on this trend to help.
  • Compare resale against new construction before you commit. Our new construction vs. resale guide lays out the trade-offs.
  • Use today's longer days on market to negotiate. Our buyer's market negotiation guide shows how.

If you're a longtime owner thinking about your next move:

  • You have more options than "sell now or never." Selling, renting, and staying are all real choices, and our sell-or-rent decision guide walks through them.
  • If you're moving within Florida, ask about Save Our Homes portability before you sell, since it can carry part of your tax savings to your next home.
  • If you've inherited a home, our guide to selling an inherited house covers the steps.

Every situation is different, and the only way to know what this means for you is to run your numbers against this market. That's what we do with our clients before they list or make an offer. Common questions

How many homes will aging owners free up by 2036?

A Realtor.com report released October 5, 2026 projects that Baby Boomer and Silent Generation owners will release about 13.9 million homes between 2026 and 2036, roughly 74% more than the prior decade. The pace is expected to rise from about 1.27 million homes in 2027 to about 1.52 million in 2036.

Will aging owners selling solve the housing shortage?

Not on its own. The report's economist said the handoff is a welcome relief but not big enough to solve the housing shortage alone. Related research from the National Association of Home Builders also concludes that more homebuilding is still needed.

Will this help first-time buyers in Southwest Florida?

Only a little. Of the 13.9 million homes expected to be released, only about 380,000 are starter homes with 0 to 2 bedrooms. Most of the supply is 3- and 4-bedroom homes, so buyers looking for smaller, lower-priced homes shouldn't count on this trend alone.

What does a home being released mean?

In the Realtor.com analysis, a home is released when it leaves the ownership of Baby Boomer or Silent Generation households through death, a move into care, household consolidation, or conversion to a rental. That's broader than a traditional listing, so not every released home becomes a for-sale listing.

Should I wait to buy in Southwest Florida because of this report?

Probably not for this reason alone. The report doesn't break out Florida, and related research suggests turnover in retirement-heavy markets is gradual. As of mid-2026, buyers in Bonita Springs and Estero already have negotiating room, so the better question is whether the numbers work for your budget and timeline.
