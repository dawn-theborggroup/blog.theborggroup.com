Buyer Tips

What the 2024 NAR settlement means for Southwest Florida home sellers and buyers

By Mark & Dawn Borg·December 25, 2025·5 min read

The 2024 NAR settlement introduces major changes to how real estate commissions are disclosed and negotiated. If you're buying or selling in Southwest Florida, understanding the NAR settlement and its impact on real estate commissions can help you navigate your next move more confidently.

NAR settlement explained for Southwest Florida homeowners

In March 2024, the National Association of REALTORS® (NAR) reached a landmark legal settlement that will change the way real estate commissions are handled across the United States. This agreement was the result of lawsuits alleging a lack of transparency in how commissions were offered and split between agents.

Key changes include: buyer agent compensation will no longer be automatically offered via the MLS; all commissions must now be independently negotiated and clearly disclosed; commission structures are no longer assumed or fixed and must be discussed openly with your agent. This shift is designed to promote consumer choice and transparency in real estate transactions.

How the NAR settlement affects home sellers in Southwest Florida

If you're thinking of selling a home in Naples, Bonita Springs, Estero, or Fort Myers, the biggest change is that you are no longer automatically expected to offer compensation to a buyer's agent. While many sellers may still choose to offer a buyer agent commission to attract more buyers, it is now entirely optional and must be clearly negotiated with your listing agent.

Benefits for sellers: more flexibility in structuring your transaction, greater control over total selling costs, and increased transparency during listing discussions.

However, it's still important to position your home competitively, and offering compensation to buyer agents may remain a strategic decision to ensure broad exposure. Southwest Florida remains a highly competitive real estate market, especially in gated golf communities, waterfront neighborhoods, and new construction zones — offering full-service representation and maintaining strong buyer incentives may still be key to a faster, more profitable sale.

What Southwest Florida buyers need to know about real estate commissions

If you're purchasing a home in Fort Myers, Naples, or Estero, you may now be asked to formally agree in writing with your buyer's agent about how they will be compensated.

Buyers may pay their agent directly, negotiate for the seller to contribute toward their agent's fee, or use commission rebates or other fee-based models. This makes it more important than ever for buyers to ask upfront about agent compensation and what services they are receiving.

Sellers should consider

Whether to offer a buyer agent commission as part of their strategy, and how their pricing stacks up against similar homes in the area.
