Harmony Pointe at Emerson Square, Fort Myers: what does $279,900 buy? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 18, 2026·8 min read

What does $279,900 buy at Harmony Pointe in Emerson Square?

At 17485 Old Harmony Drive #202, $279,900 buys a 3-bedroom, 2-bath, 2,188-square-foot second-floor corner condo with its own attached 2-car garage, a 2024 tile roof, and a screened lanai that looks out over a wooded preserve. It works out to about $128 per square foot. The home is in Harmony Pointe, the carriage-home section of Emerson Square, a gated community of about 284 residences on the west side of US-41 just south of Alico Road in South Fort Myers.

The question we hear most from buyers about this home is simple: can a condo really feel like a house? This one comes closer than most. Here’s what you get, what it costs to own, and what to look at before you write an offer.

Why this condo doesn’t feel like a condo

Most condos at this price give you a numbered parking space and a shared hallway. Harmony Pointe’s carriage homes were built differently.

Your own garage and front door

#202 has a full two-car garage on the ground floor, a private entry, and its own stairway up to the home. Groceries, golf clubs, beach chairs, and a kayak all have a place, and you never cross a parking lot in the rain. For buyers coming from a single-family house, that’s usually the moment the word “condo” stops bothering them.

Room for how you actually live

At 2,188 square feet, this is the size of a lot of houses. You get:

  • A great room with a tray ceiling and sliders to the lanai, open to the kitchen.
  • A kitchen with stainless appliances, long runs of cabinets and counters, a sunny breakfast nook, and a butler’s pantry.
  • A separate formal dining room with its own tray ceiling — room for a real holiday table or a home office.
  • A split floor plan. The primary suite has its own slider to the lanai, dual closets, and a bath with dual vanities, a soaking tub, and a separate walk-in shower.
  • Two guest bedrooms with high ceilings on the other side of the home, sharing a full bath with a glass-enclosed shower. One makes an easy office or den.

It’s a corner residence on the top floor, so you get extra windows and no one living above you. The living areas and bedrooms have carpet, and the kitchen, nook, and baths have tile.

The lanai

From the second floor, the long screened lanai sits right at treetop level. There’s no parking lot and no neighbor’s lanai to look into — just layers of green preserve. It’s reached from both the living room and the primary suite, with ceiling fans to keep the breeze moving.

A note on the listing photos

On the listing page, a few rooms show an AI-staged “Furnishing Idea” right next to the actual photo of the same room. The furniture, décor, and wood-look flooring in those concept images aren’t part of the home. Every photo labeled “Actual Photo,” and the full gallery, shows the home as it is.

What it costs to own, and what to check first

The roof and paint are done. The building has a new tile roof completed in 2024, and the home has fresh interior and exterior paint. A newer roof is one of the first things an insurer asks about, which matters for your premium. Our post on whether a home can be insured explains why.

Association documents. As a condo, you’ll want to read the budget, the reserves, the rules, and the fee schedule before you commit. We get the condo documents to you up front so you can decide with the full picture. Our guide on how to evaluate a Florida HOA before buying walks through what to look for.

Insurance. Condo owners carry their own unit-owner (HO-6) policy for the interior, while the association insures the building. Get a quote on this specific unit before you write an offer, and we’ll pull the flood determination for the exact building.

Property taxes. Your taxes reset based on your purchase price. If this will be your primary home, Florida’s homestead exemption and Save Our Homes cap can lower the bill — our property tax calculator runs your number.

Life in Emerson Square

The clubhouse is a short walk from #202, with a fitness room and a community room. The resort-style pool and spa is framed by tall pines, and there are barbecue and picnic areas, a playground, lakes, and sidewalks throughout the neighborhood.

Location is the other half of the value. Gulf Coast Town Center and Hertz Arena are about 10 minutes away, Coconut Point about 12, and Southwest Florida International Airport and FGCU about 15. Lovers Key State Park is roughly 20 minutes, and Fort Myers Beach and Bonita Beach are 20 to 25 minutes depending on traffic. Just up US-41, the Trailside at Dia Crossing mixed-use project is planned for residences, shops, and a hotel, with its first phase slated for early 2027.

If you’re still getting to know the area, our Fort Myers buyer’s guide covers the neighborhoods around it.

Who this home fits

It fits a buyer who wants house-size space and a private garage without the yard work, behind a gate, at a price that’s hard to match in South Fort Myers. It fits seasonal owners who want to lock the garage and head north, and anyone who works along the Alico corridor or flies out of RSW often. It fits less well if you want a ground-floor home or a private pool — #202 is upstairs, reached by its own stairway.

What would we look at first if you called us about this home?

We’d get you the condo documents, budget, and fees right away, and we’d have you get an HO-6 insurance quote on the unit. Those two things tell you the real monthly cost of owning it. Then we’d walk you through association approval so it doesn’t slow your closing — our post on HOA approval for buyers explains why we start early.
