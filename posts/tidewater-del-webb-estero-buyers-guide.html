Tidewater by Del Webb Estero FL: who can actually live in your home? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 2, 2026·11 min read

Who is allowed to live in a home at Tidewater by Del Webb in Estero, FL?

At Tidewater, the age rule is not the federal 80/20 standard almost every 55+ community guide quotes. The community's own recorded Declaration of Restrictions requires that "each occupied Unit shall at all times be Occupied by at least one person fifty-five (55) years of age or older," and that "no persons under the age of nineteen (19) shall Occupy a Unit." The document defines "Occupy" as staying overnight at least 90 days in any consecutive 12-month period. The restriction runs 30 years from recording, renews automatically in unlimited 10-year terms, and expressly cannot be amended into conflict with Lee County's school impact fee ordinance — so it is not a house rule the board can soften by vote.

Tidewater is a gated, built-out, resale-only active-adult community of 385 homes on roughly 132 acres in Estero, and on the surface it looks like every other Southwest Florida 55+ community: one clubhouse, one HOA fee, no golf, no CDD. Read the recorded documents instead of the brochure and it is measurably stricter than the law requires — and the reason is a county ordinance, not a lifestyle preference.

Tidewater at a glance, and what "built out" changes

Tidewater was built by Pulte Home Company under its Del Webb active-adult brand, east of I-75 near Hertz Arena and the Miromar Outlets. Construction ran from roughly 2015 into 2021, and it is finished — no model center, no sales office, no builder incentive to negotiate. The association's own website puts the community at 385 homes on 132 acres; third-party profiles report 380 homesites and a Business Observer piece on the land purchase referenced 387 planned homes. We use the association's figure because it is the association's. Management is KW Property Management (KWPMC), with an office at 10700 Tidewater Key Boulevard.

Two details in the public document file show where the community sits in its life cycle. The association was organized as a Florida corporation in 2015 — a developer-era entity — and its Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws were amended and restated on April 2, 2024. Board resolutions on the same page run 2021 through 2022 — a director code of conduct, electronic voting, a collection policy, strict enforcement of the declaration. That is a resident-run association doing its own housekeeping. For a buyer it cuts both ways: no builder warranty to inherit and no developer subsidy propping up the amenity budget, but also nobody between you and the people who set your assessment.

Homes, assessments, and the figures that disagree

Home types run from attached villas to single-family. Del Webb's plans start with the Serenity villa at about 1,542 square feet and run through single-family plans from 1,671 to roughly 2,850 square feet, two to three bedrooms typical. Pricing and fees are where published numbers stop agreeing, so here is the spread with its sources and dates rather than one tidy figure:

  • Active inventory: nine listings from $419,000 to $675,000, median around $449,000, per a national aggregator reading the local MLS, which also reports an average of 141 days to sell for homes closed in the past year, as of November 2026.
  • Trailing twelve months: an agent community page publishing an MLS-filtered report as of October 21, 2026 shows 30 closed sales, a median sold price near $520,000, a sold range of $357,500 to $849,900, about $301 per square foot, and a 60-day median days on market.
  • HOA assessment: community profiles put dues at roughly $301 to $546 a month, billed quarterly; one national profile shows a range topping out at $1,719 per quarter; a current Tidewater MLS listing we read directly shows $1,467 per quarter — about $489 a month — on a 2017-built attached home.

Those are not four conflicting answers. They are different measures read on different dates, and the assessment genuinely varies by home type and by a separate landscaping charge that tracks parcel size. What the MLS listing does settle is what the money buys: irrigation water, grounds maintenance, management, legal and accounting, recreation facilities, security, and street lights. No golf line. No CDD line.

We found no Community Development District assessment for Tidewater on any listing, tax reference, or profile we checked, and agent pages state outright there is no district and no CDD bond. That is what we did not find, which is not a certified absence — the non-ad valorem section of the parcel's tax bill settles it, and that is a five-minute check. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero explains why that line matters so much here.

Amenities, and who this community fits

Tidewater is an amenity-center community, not a golf community, and that drives the cost structure: no course to maintain, no equity membership, no mandatory club dues on top of the HOA fee. One assessment covers nearly everything, while buyers in nearby bundled-golf communities reconcile two and three separate bills.

The two-story clubhouse at 10700 Tidewater Key Boulevard is reported anywhere from about 13,000 to 26,000 square feet depending on the profile; the association publishes no figure, so we are not asserting one. Consistently documented across sources: fitness center, movement and yoga studio, gathering and activity rooms, lending library, catering kitchen, heated resort pool and spa, outdoor bar and patio, pickleball, tennis and bocce courts, event lawn, dog park, community gardens, and paths around the lakes, with a lifestyle director running the calendar.

The buying process: the document that is not in the CC&Rs

Standard advice for vetting a Florida association is to read the CC&Rs, bylaws, rules, reserve study, and estoppel certificate — it is the advice in our own guide to evaluating a Florida HOA before buying. At Tidewater it would not reach the most consequential document in the file.

The age restriction lives in its own separately recorded instrument — a Declaration of Restrictions (its signature block calls it a Declaration of Use Restrictions) — sitting on the association's public documents page beside the bylaws and the bingo rules. A buyer who reads the covenants and the rules and stops there can close without ever seeing it. What it says, in its own words:

  • Every occupied home, all the time. "Each occupied Unit shall at all times be Occupied by at least one person fifty-five (55) years of age or older." Florida Statutes section 760.29 and the federal Housing for Older Persons Act require only that "at least 80 percent of the occupied units are occupied by at least one person 55 years of age or older." The statute sets a floor for the exemption. Tidewater's covenants go above it — and several widely-read profiles describe Tidewater as having the usual 20% buffer. The recorded document does not.
  • Nineteen, not eighteen. "No persons under the age of nineteen (19) shall Occupy a Unit." Anyone 19 or older may live with an age-qualified occupant; visitors under 19 are welcome when supervised at all times by someone 19 or older.
  • "Occupy" is measured. Staying overnight at least 90 days in a consecutive 12-month period. A long winter visit is counted, not assumed.
  • Notice, proof, and enforcement. Owners must notify the board in writing immediately after any occupancy change, with names and ages; the association has remedies if notice is not given within ten days, may run an occupant census, and may require proof of age. An owner may request a written hardship exception, at the board's sole discretion. Each owner appoints the association as attorney-in-fact to pursue removal of a non-compliant occupant.

Now the part that explains the rest. The duration clause sets an initial term ending on the thirtieth anniversary of the date of recordation, renewing automatically for unlimited successive ten-year periods. The amendment clause allows the developer and board to amend "solely for the purposes of maintaining these age restrictions consistent with the Fair Housing Requirements," correcting scrivener's errors, and correcting "any deviations from Lee County School Impact Fee Ordinance, Section 2-412." Then, flatly: "In no event shall an amendment to this Declaration conflict with Lee County School Impact Fee Ordinance, Section 2-412."

We read that ordinance. Lee County Ordinance 01-21, adopted November 27, 2001, added the school impact fee division to the county's Land Development Code, including Section 2-412 on exemptions. Subsection (a)(2) exempts dwelling units "operated as a community for older persons" in compliance with the Fair Housing Act and the Housing for Older Persons Act of 1995 "that also prohibit persons under the age of 18 years from residing within the dwelling units on the property as a permanent resident" — and requires the restriction "be evidenced by a recorded declaration of covenants and restrictions that are not subject to revocation or amendment for a period of at least 30 years from the date of recording," running with the land.

Line that up and the architecture is obvious. The 30-year lock is the ordinance's 30 years. The no-amendment clause is its non-revocability requirement. The age floor is its 18, set a year higher at 19 for cushion. The age restriction at Tidewater is not a lifestyle covenant — it is the recorded consideration for a county impact fee the development did not pay, which is why it is written to be hard to unwind. The ordinance dates to 2001 and the code has been amended many times since; we publish the mechanism and the section the declaration itself names, not a current fee figure. Confirm the operative code text with Lee County before relying on it.

The rest of the process is more ordinary. Tidewater's own Sale Application (Rev. 7.24.24) carries a $100 nonrefundable application fee, asks for the executed contract up front and a warranty deed, closing statement, and estoppel letter at closing, and states plainly: "This is an age restricted 55+ community; age verification is required." It wants a date of birth and stated relationship for every purchaser and occupant, asks whether you will live there full-time, part-time, or lease, and routes to an "Action of Board of Directors" block with approved and disapproved boxes. Put this line in your calendar: "Please submit this form at least twenty (20) days prior to occupancy to allow for processing time," and "Approval must be received prior to occupancy." Estoppel and questionnaire requests go through KWPMC via HomeWiseDocs.

That 20-day window is tighter than the 30-day cushion most Southwest Florida associations ask for, which is why our guide to HOA approval for buyers says start the application in the first week of the contract. One more figure to ask about rather than assume: listing remarks and agent pages report a resale capital assessment in the low thousands on top of the application fee, while the association's own form names only the $100. The estoppel letter is what settles it.

What would we look at first if you called us about Tidewater?

Three things, in this order, and the first is not the house.

We would start with who is going to sleep there. At Tidewater that answer can make a home unbuyable for a particular household, and it is far better learned in week one than week five. A buyer of 52 purchasing with a spouse of 57 is fine; a buyer planning to bring an adult child who is 18 is not, and the 19-year threshold surprises people who have shopped other 55+ communities. We would read the recorded declaration with you rather than summarize it, because the version circulating on profile pages is the federal 80/20 rule and this community's recorded rule is stricter.

Second, we would pull the estoppel and the current budget and reconcile the actual assessment for the actual parcel against the published ranges — including the landscaping charge and whatever the resale capital assessment really is. In a built-out community with no developer backstop, the association's own numbers are the only numbers.

Third, we would check the parcel's tax bill for a non-ad valorem district line and its flood zone, and confirm which jurisdiction the address sits in, because that changes both the flood insurance discount and the lien search. Our walkthrough on checking whether a home is inside the Village of Estero covers why "Estero" on the mailing address does not settle it.

That is the order that keeps a buyer from spending five weeks and an inspection fee on a home their household cannot occupy. That's the part we take care of for you.

How Tidewater compares

Cascades at Estero is the closest local comparison — the other established 55+ community in Estero, larger and older: 614 single-family homes by Levitt & Sons built roughly 2002 to 2007, a clubhouse reported near 28,000 square feet, an indoor/outdoor heated pool, six Har-Tru tennis courts, and dues reported at $1,180 per quarter. The buying-process difference is the number worth noticing: community pages report Cascades charging a $2,500 transfer fee per new owner plus a $150 application fee, against Tidewater's $100. On a resale that is a real swing in cash at closing — and both are agent-page figures, so both belong in the estoppel request rather than your spreadsheet.

Outside Estero, our guides to Pelican Preserve in Fort Myers (about 2,498 homes) and Valencia Bonita in Bonita Springs (about 997) cover the region's two larger 55+ options, and our head-to-head comparison runs their fee stacks side by side. Against all three, Tidewater's pitch is scale in reverse: 385 homes, one assessment, no golf, no district, and a clubhouse you can walk to. Whether that reads as intimate or limited is a preference, not a verdict — our Estero buyer's guide and Estero relocation guide set the wider baseline.

Frequently asked questions

Can someone under 55 buy a home in Tidewater by Del Webb?

The declaration restricts occupancy, not title. It requires each occupied home to have at least one occupant aged 55 or older at all times, and no occupant under 19. A buyer of 52 purchasing with a spouse of 57 satisfies that; a household with no one 55 or older does not. Have a Florida real estate attorney review the recorded documents.

Can my grandchildren stay with me at Tidewater?

Yes, as visitors. The declaration permits people under 19 on the property when supervised at all times by someone 19 or older, and defines "Occupy" as staying overnight at least 90 days in any consecutive 12-month period. The line between visiting and occupying is measured in nights — so count a long seasonal stay rather than guess.

Does Tidewater by Del Webb have a CDD fee?

We found no CDD assessment for Tidewater in any listing, profile, or tax reference we checked, and agent community pages state there is no district and no CDD bond. Verify it on the non-ad valorem section of the parcel's Lee County tax bill before treating it as settled.

What are HOA fees at Tidewater, and what do they cover?

Published ranges run about $301 to $546 a month; a current MLS listing we read shows $1,467 per quarter — roughly $489 a month — on a 2017 attached home, plus a separate landscaping charge by parcel size. The assessment covers irrigation water, grounds maintenance, management, legal and accounting, recreation facilities, security, and street lights. The estoppel letter gives the binding figure.

Tidewater is a finished, resident-run, 385-home community with one assessment, no golf, and no district — and an age restriction stricter than federal law, written into its own recorded instrument and locked by a county ordinance for thirty years at a time. That last fact is not in the brochure, and it is not in the CC&Rs either. It is the first thing we would hand you.
