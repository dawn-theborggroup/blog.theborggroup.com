Florida is now the world's 14th largest economy: what it means for Southwest Florida real estate

By Mark & Dawn Borg·July 17, 2026·7 min read

Florida's GDP has reached approximately $1.89 trillion, placing the state ahead of countries like Australia and Mexico and ranking it 14th among the world's economies. The Florida Chamber Foundation announced this milestone in July 2026, citing years of sustained population growth, business relocation, and economic diversification. For buyers, sellers, and investors in Southwest Florida markets like Bonita Springs, Naples, Estero, and Fort Myers, this ranking reinforces long-term housing demand, continued in-migration, and strong confidence in the region's property market.

Florida just crossed a threshold that most states never reach. The Florida Chamber Foundation announced that Florida's GDP has climbed to approximately $1.89 trillion, officially ranking the state as the 14th largest economy in the world. To put that in perspective: Florida now produces more economic output than countries like Australia, Mexico, and Spain.

For business journals and economic analysts, that's a compelling data point. For anyone watching the Southwest Florida real estate market, it's something more specific: it's confirmation that the fundamentals driving this region haven't changed.

Why a global economic ranking matters for local real estate

Here's the connection that matters: real estate doesn't exist in a vacuum. Property values, buyer demand, and long-term market health are all downstream of economic activity — jobs, migration, business confidence, and income growth.

When Florida's economy strengthens at this scale, a few things tend to follow: more people move here — a thriving economy attracts workers, entrepreneurs, and retirees who want to be where opportunity is growing, not contracting. Business relocation accelerates — companies that moved their headquarters to Florida over the last decade brought employees with them, and many of those employees needed homes in Naples, Bonita Springs, Fort Myers, and Estero. Investor confidence holds — institutional and individual investors alike look at state-level economic health before making long-term real estate commitments, and a $1.89 trillion economy is a convincing signal.

People aren't just moving here for the lifestyle; they're moving here for opportunity.

The 2030 goal and what it could mean

The Florida Chamber Foundation hasn't stopped at 14th place. Their stated goal is to move Florida into the top 10 global economies by 2030, a target that implies continued investment in infrastructure, workforce development, and business attraction.

If even part of that trajectory holds, the next five years in Florida real estate could be shaped by the same forces that drove the last five: population growth, job creation, and sustained demand for well-positioned homes. That's not a guarantee, no market ever is, but it's the kind of structural backdrop that makes Southwest Florida worth paying attention to. For homeowners already here, it's a signal that the decision to buy into this market was grounded in something real. For buyers still on the sidelines, it's worth factoring into your timing calculation.

Why are people still moving to Southwest Florida in 2026?

No state income tax, a growing job market, year-round warm weather, waterfront access, and a lower cost of living compared to many Northeast and West Coast metros continue to draw buyers to communities like Bonita Springs, Naples, Estero, and Fort Myers. Florida's strengthening economy adds another layer: people aren't just moving here for the lifestyle; they're moving here for opportunity.

Should I buy in Southwest Florida now or wait?

Timing is always personal and depends on your financial situation, goals, and how long you plan to hold the property. What Florida's economic momentum tells us is that the structural demand for housing here — from retirees, remote workers, and investors — remains intact. The best approach is to run the numbers on your specific situation rather than trying to time the broader market.
