Rapallo at Coconut Point Estero FL: who pays for the amenities in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 21, 2026·10 min read

Rapallo's 540 owners pay for everything themselves. There is no CDD on the tax bill, no golf course generating outside revenue, and no developer left to subsidize anything — so a staffed clubhouse, five pools, a 25-meter lap pool, and five lighted Har-Tru tennis courts are funded entirely by condo and association fees across 540 homes. Current listings report roughly $8,064 to $10,172 a year in total recurring fees, and the buildings turn twenty this year. The question that decides your purchase is not whether the amenities are good. It is whether the reserves behind them are.

Rapallo at Coconut Point is a gated community of 540 residences in Estero, FL, built out between 2005 and 2007 by Touchstone at Rapallo, Inc., with site design by WilsonMiller. It sits east of US-41 between Williams Road and Coconut Point Town Center, which means it is one of the only gated communities in Estero you can walk out of and into a regional shopping center.

The amenity package is the headline. Per the association's own site, owners get a large clubhouse, a lagoon-style resort pool, a 25-meter Olympic lap pool, five lighted Har-Tru tennis courts with a pro shop, a renovated fitness center, a fishing pier, and miles of jogging and biking paths. Add bocce, a sauna and spa services, group fitness classes, a botanical garden, a freshwater beach, a picnic gazebo, a 24-hour gate, and a shuttle to Bonita Beach. There is an on-site Club Manager, an Activities Director, and a Fitness Director.

Now the sentence that should change how you shop this community: every dollar of that is divided by 540 homes.

That is a small denominator for a country-club-grade operation. Har-Tru is clay — it needs continuous maintenance, not periodic resurfacing like hard court. Five of them, lit, with a pro shop, is a tennis program most bundled-golf communities twice Rapallo's size would struggle to carry. And unlike Verdana Village or The Place at Corkscrew, there is no Community Development District spreading the capital cost across a bond on your tax bill. Unlike Pelican Sound or Breckenridge, there is no golf operation. The fee is the whole funding mechanism.

What the homes are, and what they cost

Rapallo is all resale. There is no sales center, no builder representative, and no model row — the last builder inventory sold years ago.

Four home types, in Mediterranean Revival style:

  • Terrace condominiums — roughly 1,342 to 1,436 square feet
  • Coach homes — roughly 1,606 to 1,836 square feet
  • Carriage homes — roughly 1,966 to 2,594 square feet
  • Courtyard villas — roughly 1,824 to 2,376 square feet

As of late October 2026 there are five active listings running $300,000 to $730,000, with a median list price of $375,000 and an average of about $274.75 per square foot. Average days on market across those listings is about 92 — slower than Estero's 66-day median, which matters when you negotiate.

Closed sales tell a steadier story. Inside The Enclave at Rapallo, a 1,732-square-foot coach home closed on September 14, 2026 at $475,000 — full list price. A comparable unit closed in April 2026 at $365,000. Across fourteen Enclave sales since 2023 the range is $300,000 to $515,000. Price per square foot swings from about $186 to $297 on near-identical floor plans, which tells you condition, floor, and view are doing most of the work here.

The fee, and the structure underneath it

Rapallo is not one association. It is a master association — The Club at Rapallo, Inc., with its office at 8551 Via Rapallo Drive — sitting above several separate condominium associations. Legal descriptions on individual units reference Rapallo Three, Rapallo Four, and The Enclave at Rapallo, and The Enclave is itself 90 coach homes with its own private pool and lake house on top of the master amenities.

This matters because of how the bill arrives. On a 1,732-square-foot coach home in Rapallo Three, the condo fee was reported at $2,016 a quarter — $8,064 a year — with the master association fee included inside that figure rather than billed separately. On a 2,584-square-foot carriage home on Via Lungomare, the 2026 listing reports $2,543 a quarter, or $10,172 in total annual recurring fees.

What that fee covers, per the MLS field list on one of those units: cable, building insurance, internet, irrigation water, lawn and landscape maintenance, legal and accounting, management, the master association fee, exterior pest control, recreation facilities, repairs, reserves, security, sewer, street lights, street maintenance, trash, and water.

So the number looks large and is doing a great deal of work. One brokerage data benchmark puts Rapallo's monthly ownership cost at about $0.48 to $0.51 per square foot against an Estero average near $0.17 — roughly three times the area norm. That spread is not a red flag by itself. It is what a fully inclusive fee on a staffed amenity package looks like. If you want the apples-to-apples version of that comparison, we worked it through in detail in our Villagio Estero buyer's guide, Estero's other no-CDD condo community.

There is no CDD at Rapallo — it is one of sixteen Estero communities with none, which sets it apart from Verdana Village at $1,414.90 to $3,337.07 per unit, The Place at Corkscrew at $1,185.41 to $1,717.32, and WildBlue at $2,851.13 to $5,411.60 for fiscal 2027. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero explains why that line matters at resale.

Real tax bills on Rapallo units for 2025: $4,482 on a unit that last traded at $310,000, $5,980 on another, and $6,175 on the $475,000 Enclave sale.

The twenty-year question

Here is the part no listing will tell you.

Rapallo's buildings, pools, courts, and clubhouse all date to 2005 to 2007. Everything has now run roughly one full replacement cycle on roofs and is heading into the next one on pool shells, equipment, court surfaces, and clubhouse systems. Listings in the community have advertised roof assessments as paid in full by the seller — which confirms the cycle has already run here, and confirms that it arrived as an assessment rather than purely out of reserves.

That is the real buyer question at Rapallo, and it is a good one, not a scary one. A mature community with an honest reserve position and a recent capital cycle behind it can be a safer purchase than a new community whose first big bill has not landed yet. The reverse is also true. What separates the two is the documents.

Two more things worth knowing before you tour:

Building height is not uniform, and the sources disagree. One Rapallo building — Rapallo Three, Building 34 — reports two total stories, ten units, and no elevator. At the community level one aggregator lists two floors throughout. But a unit at 8597 Via Garibaldi Circle is marketed as a third-floor residence with elevator access and parking in an under-building garage. Florida's milestone inspection and Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirements apply only to condominium buildings with three or more habitable stories, so this is not a trivia question — it decides which structural-reserve regime governs the building you are buying into. Confirm it for your specific building, not for the community. Our guide on what to request before you close on a Southwest Florida condo covers the document list itself.

Flood looks favorable, and Rapallo has no published community-level figure. A terrace unit at 8597 Via Garibaldi reads FEMA Zone X at roughly 16 feet of ground elevation, and Enclave listing remarks state the community is not in a flood zone. Estero overall samples about 91% Zone X. But Rapallo does not appear in the ten-neighborhood Estero flood-zone sample at all, so there is no community-wide percentage to quote, and we are not going to invent one. Zones are assigned parcel by parcel. Pull the determination for the address. Our how-to on checking flood risk in Estero walks through the Village's own free tools.

One more detail on jurisdiction, because it carries a dollar consequence: Engage Estero's published list of which communities sit inside the incorporated Village of Estero does not name Rapallo either way. Inside the Village, the Community Rating System Class 6 rating earns a 20% NFIP discount inside the special flood hazard area and 10% on preferred-risk policies. In unincorporated Lee County the discount is an automatic 25%. Your tax bill or voter registration card settles which one applies to you.

The buying process here, and what to do before you tour

Because Rapallo is all resale with no builder, nobody in this community is paid to hand you the documents. There is no sales office that produces a fee schedule on request. The master association's budget and reserve information sit behind an owner login on its resident portal. The listing agent represents the seller.

That changes the order of operations. Do not tour first and ask about money second.

What would we look at first if you called us about Rapallo?

The fee is the easy part — it is published on the listing. We start with the two things that are not.

First, which association actually bills you, and what its reserve position looks like. A master-plus-condominium structure means more than one set of books, and at Rapallo the layer that owns your roof and your building envelope is the one most likely to assess you. We want to see where that association sits on structural reserves, what it has already spent on the roof cycle, and what the next big-ticket item is. Second, the building itself — height, elevator, and therefore whether the milestone and reserve-study rules govern it at all.

Then we read the amenity math honestly. Five pools and five clay courts carried by 540 homes is a genuinely excellent deal when the reserves are funded and a genuinely expensive one when they are not. The same fee can mean either. The documents tell us which, and they tell us before you are emotionally attached to a lanai view.

What that gets you is a real monthly number and a real five-year number, not a listing figure — and a clear answer on whether a given building is the strong buy in this community or the one to walk away from. That is the part we take care of for you.

How Rapallo compares

Against Villagio at Estero: Villagio is the cheaper entry, with units from the low $200,000s and a flat $580 monthly fee across all 514 units, no sub-associations, and no fee inversion. Rapallo costs more per month and delivers a far larger, staffed amenity package plus the Coconut Point location. Villagio's flood picture is also meaningfully worse on the limited sample available. If you want the lowest carrying cost in a gated Estero condo, Villagio. If you want resort amenities and walkable retail, Rapallo.

Against Breckenridge Golf & Tennis Club: Breckenridge gives you bundled golf for a modest master assessment, but its village-level fees swing from $7,780 to $13,000 a year depending on building height, and its homes date to 1988 through 2005. Rapallo has no golf, a tighter fee band, and newer construction. Breckenridge if you play; Rapallo if you swim, play tennis, and walk to dinner.

Against new construction in Estero: A new home in Verdana Village or The Place at Corkscrew gets you a builder warranty and amenities with no deferred maintenance — but a CDD assessment on your tax bill for decades, and a first capital cycle still ahead of you. Rapallo's capital cycle has started. Our Estero community comparison puts the whole field side by side.

One local item worth watching

The Village of Estero adopted Resolution 2026-15 at its July 2, 2026 Council meeting, approving a maintenance agreement for FDOT-funded improvements along Via Coconut Point at the Williams Road and Pelican Colony roundabouts — wider sidewalks and bicycle ramps, aimed squarely at pedestrian safety. FDOT required the agreement before final design and construction could proceed. A companion item, Resolution 2026-14, covers an FDOT sidewalk along the north side of Coconut Road from Oakwilde Boulevard to Via Coconut Point, with final design expected in late 2026.

If Rapallo's single best non-amenity feature is that you can reach Coconut Point without crossing US-41, these are the projects that make that feature better. The Village's millage held flat at 0.7300 mills for fiscal 2026-27, 0.49% below the rolled-back rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much are the fees at Rapallo at Coconut Point?

Current listings report roughly $2,016 to $2,543 per quarter, which is about $8,064 to $10,172 a year in total recurring fees depending on the home type and association. The master association fee is bundled inside the condo fee rather than billed separately, and the fee covers cable, internet, building insurance, water, sewer, trash, landscaping, reserves, security, street maintenance, and all recreation facilities.

Does Rapallo have a CDD?

No. Rapallo is one of sixteen Estero communities with no Community Development District, so there is no CDD line on your tax bill. That is a real resale advantage over Verdana Village, The Place at Corkscrew, WildBlue, Bella Terra, and Stoneybrook, all of which carry one.

Can you really walk to Coconut Point from Rapallo?

Yes. Rapallo sits east of US-41 between Williams Road and Coconut Point Town Center, with direct access toward the center via Via Villagio rather than through the US-41 intersections. Listing remarks routinely cite walking access to Coconut Point and its restaurants, and the Village of Estero has a maintenance agreement in place for FDOT-funded sidewalk and bicycle-ramp improvements along Via Coconut Point.

Is Rapallo in a flood zone?

A sampled terrace unit reads FEMA Zone X at about 16 feet of ground elevation, and Enclave listing remarks describe the community as not in a flood zone. Rapallo does not appear in the published ten-neighborhood Estero flood-zone sample, so no community-wide percentage exists. Zones are assigned parcel by parcel — pull the determination and any existing elevation certificate for the specific address.

Do Rapallo's buildings need a milestone inspection or a Structural Integrity Reserve Study?

It depends on the building, and the available sources disagree on Rapallo's heights. One building reports two stories with no elevator, while another address is marketed as a third-floor residence with elevator access. Florida applies both requirements only to condominium buildings with three or more habitable stories, so confirm the story count for the specific building before you write an offer.

Is Rapallo a good value compared to newer Estero communities?

It can be, and the fee alone will not tell you. Rapallo delivers a staffed, country-club-grade amenity package with no CDD, which is unusual at a $300,000 to $730,000 price point. The trade is that the buildings and amenities date to 2005 through 2007, so the reserve position and the capital-project history are what decide whether the fee is a bargain or a warning.

The bottom line

Rapallo gives you more amenity per dollar than almost anything in Estero at this price, with no CDD and a location you can walk out of. The cost of that is a small denominator — 540 homes carrying a staffed operation that is now twenty years old.

So shop the association, not just the unit. Ask which body bills you, where its structural reserves stand, what the roof cycle already cost, and whether your specific building sits above or below the three-story line. Then get the estoppel letter before you commit.
