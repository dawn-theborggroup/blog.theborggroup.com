Should you take your home off the market for the holidays? Southwest Florida 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 26, 2026·8 min read

Should you take your home off the market for the holidays in Southwest Florida?

Only if it comes back in January as a different listing. Pausing from mid-December to early January can give you quiet weeks to fix price, condition, or photos before seasonal buyers return. But buyers here are still writing contracts in December, a pause doesn’t erase your listing history, and a home that relaunches unchanged usually sits again. If nothing is going to change, staying on the market with sensible showing limits often makes more sense.

“Should we just take it off until after the holidays?” is one of the most common questions we hear from Southwest Florida sellers in late November. It usually comes from a homeowner who’s tired. The showings have been steady, or they’ve been thin, and family is coming to town.

That’s a fair feeling. The real question isn’t whether you deserve a break, though. It’s whether the break leads to a sale in January or just a reset that changes nothing.

What the market looks like heading into the holidays

Here’s where things stand, using the newest numbers we could open today.

  • Estero single-family: our Southwest Florida MLS market ticker (updated November 25, 2026) shows 294 active listings, 83 pending, and 85 sales in the last 45 days, at a $725,000 median sale price and 96.7% of list price. By our arithmetic, that’s roughly 5 months of supply.
  • Bonita Springs single-family: 277 active, 77 pending, 79 sold in 45 days, a $725,000 median, and 96.8% of list. Also about 5 months of supply.
  • Naples single-family: 2,096 active against 427 sales in 45 days — closer to 7 months of supply, with homes selling at 96.2% of list.
  • Condos are the slower side: by the same arithmetic, Naples condos sit near 8.6 months of supply and Bonita Springs condos near 8.1.
  • County level: Florida Realtors’ October 2026 report shows Lee County’s single-family median flat at $389,900 on 1,115 sales, while Collier’s rose 4.9% to $780,000.
  • Time on market: the Naples Area Board of Realtors reported an average of 99 days on market in September, with 5.5 months of inventory.
  • Rates: Freddie Mac’s 30-year fixed average was 7.28% on November 19, 2026.

Two things stand out. First, buyers haven’t gone anywhere — 83 Estero single-family homes are under contract right now, heading into Thanksgiving. Second, homes are selling a few points under list, not at it. In plain terms, the homes that sell are priced close to where buyers already see value.

What happens if you pause — and what doesn’t

Most sellers picture a pause as a clean slate. It rarely works that way.

Your listing history stays visible

Buyers’ agents can usually see when a home went off the market and when it came back, and many portals show a listing’s price and status history. Every MLS has its own statuses and its own rules for how days on market are counted after a pause. Ask your listing agent exactly which status your home would go into, whether the day count keeps running, and what buyers will see when it returns.

We wrote more about the trade-offs of a full reset in cancel, relist, or ride it out. The short version: a reset only helps when something real changes.

Your listing agreement still has an end date

A pause and a cancellation aren’t the same thing, and neither one automatically changes your agreement. If your listing agreement happens to end around year’s end, a few weeks off the market can eat up most of what’s left. Read your agreement, ask your agent and broker how a pause affects it, and talk to a real estate attorney if anything is unclear. This is general information, not legal advice.

Your costs keep running

Your mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and utilities don’t stop for the holidays. A three-week break is three weeks of carrying costs with no chance of an offer. We broke those numbers down in the real cost of carrying a home that isn’t selling.

Timing: why January matters here more than most places

Southwest Florida doesn’t follow the national calendar. Many seasonal residents arrive after the holidays, and January through April is when the most buyers are physically here touring homes. Our seasonal guide to selling in Southwest Florida walks through each window.

That cuts both ways for a home that hasn’t sold:

  • The case for a pause: the slow weeks between mid-December and New Year’s are a good time to repaint, fix what keeps coming up in feedback, or reshoot photos without juggling showings.
  • The case for staying on: the buyers touring between Thanksgiving and Christmas tend to be serious, and you face less competition before new listings arrive in January.
  • The middle ground: stay active but limit showings on the days your family is home. Most buyers’ agents will work around a short blackout.

Either way, the January relaunch is what matters. If your home comes back at the same price with the same photos, a returning buyer sees the same home — just older on the market.

Common seller mistakes over the holidays

  • Pausing without a plan. Coming off the market is a decision to change something. If you can’t say what will be different in January, you probably shouldn’t pause.
  • Blaming the season. “It’s just slow” is comfortable. But if similar homes nearby are going under contract and yours isn’t, the calendar isn’t the main issue. Our 2026 pricing strategy guide covers how to read that gap.
  • Holding firm until January “to see what happens.” A price adjustment made in early December reaches the buyers already here. The same cut made after New Year’s lands in a crowd of fresh listings.
  • Going dark on presentation. Heavy decor, closed blinds, and stacked boxes can make a home photograph and show smaller. If you stay on, keep the main rooms simple.
  • Ignoring insurance questions. Buyers who get a high insurance quote often walk quietly. Having a recent wind mitigation report and roof details ready can keep a buyer moving.

How The Borg Group markets listings through the holidays

We’ve been selling homes in Southwest Florida since 2005, and the holiday stretch is one of the few times a quiet listing can quietly get better. We treat December as a working month, not a lost one.

What would we look at first if you called us about a home that hasn’t sold?

First, we’d look at what buyers did instead. Not just the closed sales near you, but the homes that went under contract while yours sat. That usually tells us more than any showing feedback form.

Next, we’d look at how the listing is performing online — whether people are finding it, saving it, and asking to see it. Each of those points to a different problem: price, photos, or something buyers learn once they walk in.

Then we’d flag the Southwest Florida details that quietly stall deals: roof age, insurance, flood zone, HOA or condo fees, and anything a buyer’s inspector is likely to find.

From there, the answer to “should we pause?” usually becomes obvious. Sometimes it’s a short break to fix two things and relaunch with fresh photos in January. Sometimes it’s staying on with a sharper price so December’s buyers see it first. When it’s handled well, you head into season with a home that looks new to buyers instead of one they already scrolled past. That’s the part we take care of for you.

If your home is listed with another brokerage right now, your listing agent is the right first call — these are good questions to ask them. If your listing has expired, or you’re planning to list in the new year, we’re happy to talk.

The bottom line

Taking your home off the market for the holidays isn’t right or wrong on its own. It works when you use the time to change what buyers see, and it backfires when the same listing simply comes back in January.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a good idea to take your house off the market during the holidays in Florida?

It can be, if you use the break to change price, condition, or presentation before seasonal buyers return in January. If the home will come back exactly the same, staying on the market with limited showing days is often the better choice, since serious buyers are still touring in December.

Does taking a house off the market reset days on market?

Not always. Each MLS has its own rules for how days on market are counted after a home is paused, withdrawn, or relisted, and buyers’ agents can usually see a listing’s history. Ask your listing agent which status your home would use and what buyers will see when it returns.

Do people buy homes in December in Southwest Florida?

Yes. Our Southwest Florida MLS market ticker showed 83 Estero single-family homes and 77 in Bonita Springs under contract on November 25, 2026. December buyers tend to be serious, and they face fewer new listings than they will in January.

Does pausing my listing extend my listing agreement?

Not automatically. A pause, a withdrawal, and a cancellation are different, and how each affects your agreement depends on its terms. Read your agreement, ask your agent and broker, and consult a real estate attorney if anything is unclear.

When is the best time to relist a home in Southwest Florida?

Early January is a common relaunch point, because many seasonal residents arrive after the holidays and peak demand runs from January through April. A relaunch works best when the price, photos, or condition have genuinely changed.
