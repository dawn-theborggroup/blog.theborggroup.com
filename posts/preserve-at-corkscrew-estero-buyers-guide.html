The Preserve at Corkscrew Estero FL: what does the guard at the gate actually cost you? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 30, 2026·11 min read

What does the staffed gate at The Preserve at Corkscrew cost a homeowner?

About $498 per home per year — and it is not on your HOA bill. The Preserve at Corkscrew sits inside the Cypress Shadows Community Development District, whose own budget carries a Gatehouse category adopted at $219,500 for fiscal 2026 and proposed at $224,555 for fiscal 2027, spread across 441 platted lots. Inside that sits a Contracts-Security Services line the district describes as "the cost of the district's security personnel and equipment" — $208,500 adopted for FY2026, $214,755 as proposed for FY2027. That single contract is bigger than the district's lake maintenance and utility budgets combined, and it arrives on your Lee County property tax bill rather than through the association.

Nearly every community along Corkscrew Road is gated. Almost none pays a person to sit at the gate. That difference is the most interesting number in this community's paperwork, and no listing sheet will show it to you.

What The Preserve at Corkscrew actually is

It is a built-out, gated community of 441 single-family homes on Cypress Shadows Boulevard, roughly two miles east of I-75 off Corkscrew Road in Estero, developed by Cameratta Companies — the same developer behind Corkscrew Shores, Verdana Village, and Kingston further east — with Pulte Homes and Lennar Homes as the builders. The homes date from the early-to-mid 2010s; Homes.com puts the median year built at 2013, and Cameratta's own project page notes it was the top-selling Lee County community in 2013 and 2014.

That history matters more than it sounds. Because the community finished selling years ago, there is no sales center, no builder incentive calendar, and no phase pricing — every purchase is a resale. It also means the district's costs are fully allocated and sitting in a public budget you can read today, which is not true of a community still adding lots.

The community is reported to sit inside the incorporated Village of Estero rather than unincorporated Lee County — worth confirming for your address, since it changes which government issues your permits and which flood-insurance discount class applies. Our guide on how to check if a home is inside the Village of Estero walks through it.

The homes, the pricing, and the thin inventory

Floor plans run roughly 1,641 to 3,472 square feet across two-, three-, and four-bedroom layouts, on a lot mix of 50-, 60-, and 75-foot widths — approximately 197, 179, and 65 lots respectively on the district's assessment roll.

Supply is genuinely tight:

  • Active listings: about three homes as of late September 2026, median asking price near $609,000
  • Closings, trailing twelve months to September 29, 2026: 15 sales, median sold price $627,800, median 84 days on market, closing at a median 97.0% of final list price
  • Separate read: Homes.com, using its own neighborhood boundary, reports a 12-month median sale price of $627,650 across 28 sales, averaging 118 days, at about $304 per square foot and 1.70 months of supply

Those two medians landing within $150 of each other is a useful signal. The days-on-market figures differ because the sources draw different boundaries and measure median against average, so treat the price as well corroborated and the timing as directional.

Reading the district's budget, line by line

Here is where this community separates itself from its neighbors. The district's general fund was adopted at $981,086 for FY2026 and proposed at $974,034.82 for FY2027. Side by side:

  • Field: $269,250 adopted FY2026 → $288,297 proposed FY2027
  • Gatehouse: $219,500 → $224,555
  • Administrative: $165,976 → $167,822.82
  • Utility Services: $127,000 → $105,000
  • Lakes and Ponds: $109,360 → $108,360
  • Reserves: $90,000 → $80,000

The gate is the second-largest thing this district does — about 23% of the entire general fund at the proposed FY2027 figure. The security contract alone, $214,755, exceeds Lakes and Ponds plus Utility Services together ($213,360). The remaining gatehouse lines are small: $7,000 for gate repairs, $1,800 for water, $1,000 for telephone and internet.

A staffed gate is a payroll line, not a capital item. It does not depreciate, it does not get paid off, and the board resets it every year. The bonds behind the roads and lakes have maturity dates. The guard does not.

Two things in those columns deserve attention. First, the security contract rose while reserves fell — $208,500 to $214,755 on one line, $90,000 down to $80,000 on another, in the same cycle. Districts move money for ordinary reasons and one year is not a trend, but it is the kind of trade-off you should see before you own a share of it.

Second, the number moved mid-cycle. The April 2, 2026 package still showed FY2027 security services at $208,500 and total gatehouse at $219,500; by the May 7, 2026 package — the version approved for a public hearing — those had become $214,755 and $224,555. Both are the district's own documents, five weeks apart. The later supersedes the earlier, and we could not reach the final adopted FY2027 budget, so we are not publishing a single FY2027 per-home figure as settled. The adopted budget and your lot's tax bill settle it.

On the per-lot assessment, published figures put the FY2026 final adopted range at roughly $2,643.92 to $3,479.85 per lot per year across the 441 units, with operations and maintenance near $2,157.16 to $2,439.85, and an approved tentative FY2027 range around $2,633.94 to $3,447.33. Those come from a competing brokerage's fee tables, which name the district's own budgets as sources and were read on September 12, 2026; they line up with our own reading of the FY2027 O&M figures and the 441-lot count, which is why we repeat them with attribution. The range exists because the assessment varies by lot width and bond series — a Series 2012 issue with about $2,195,000 outstanding maturing November 1, 2043, and a Series 2022 refunding issue with about $970,000 outstanding maturing November 1, 2042. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero explains why the operations portion and the debt portion behave differently over time.

Amenities, lifestyle, and who this community fits

The amenity campus is substantial for a community of this size. Cameratta's own description lists a clubhouse with a large fitness center and yoga studio, a gathering room, a resort-style pool and spa, cabanas, an outdoor fireplace, a playground, tennis and pickleball courts, a lakeview wood deck, and nature preserves. Other published descriptions add bocce, a dog park, and a tot lot, and put the count at two tennis and four pickleball courts. The sixteen lakes are decorative — no fishing, swimming, or boating — and there is no golf course or marina.

Who it suits: a buyer who wants a finished community with mature landscaping, a full amenity package, and a staffed entry, and who is willing to pay for that entry on a tax bill. Who it does not: a buyer shopping on lowest carrying cost, or one who wants a new build with a warranty and incentives.

One local item worth watching, and it is good news here: the stretch of Corkscrew Road serving this community — Ben Hill Griffin Parkway to Bella Terra Boulevard — is the already-completed Phase 1 of the county's widening. Lee County DOT's Roadwatch report for August 20–26, 2026 also describes a Village of Estero project putting a 10-foot asphalt shared-use path on the south side of that same stretch, with "estimated completion in early 2027." Unlike communities further east, this one is not waiting on road capacity.

Buying a resale here, and what to settle before you tour

Because the community is built out, these are resale mechanics, and the usual new-construction warning about registering with your own agent before walking into a sales center does not apply. What replaces it is document work — there is no builder to hand you a fee sheet, and with roughly three homes on the market at a time, you will not get many chances to think it over.

The items that decide your real monthly cost are the HOA estoppel letter, the district's adopted budget for the current year, and the parcel's actual tax bill. Published HOA dues figures disagree and carry different dates — about $810 per quarter verified against MLS listing sheets in July 2026, and $920 per quarter reported as of February 2023. No association in this market publishes a current dues figure in a primary document, so treat every number online as a dated report rather than a fact. Our walkthrough on how to evaluate a Florida HOA before buying covers what else those documents should tell you.

On flood, the community is generally mapped in FEMA Zone X, the lower-risk classification, per Village of Estero mapping — but that is a community-level reading. Zones here are parcel-specific; our guide to checking a home's flood risk in Estero shows why two homes on one street can be mapped differently.

What would we look at first if you called us about The Preserve at Corkscrew?

The district's current adopted budget, before anything else — specifically the gatehouse block and the reserve line, read next to the prior year. We would want to know whether the security contract is on a renewal cycle about to reprice, and what the board has said about it, because the Gatehouse Contract Update has been a standing item on this district's agendas. Then we would set the district assessment for your exact lot width and bond series alongside the HOA estoppel, so you are looking at one all-in annual number rather than two bills arriving at different times from different places.

What we would flag is the shape of the cost, not just its size. A staffed gate is permanent operating expense that the board resets every year, and it behaves nothing like the bond portion of your assessment, which has a maturity date. A buyer who understands that is making a clean decision: you are buying a service, you can see what it costs, and you can decide it is worth it. A buyer who does not is usually surprised by the first tax bill.

How it compares to its Corkscrew Road neighbors

Two comparisons do the most work here, and both are same-corridor, same-commute.

Corkscrew Shores is the sharpest contrast — also a Cameratta community, about three miles east of I-75, carrying no CDD at all. Its published cost is roughly $1,782 per quarter to the master association plus a $480 annual food-and-beverage minimum at the Captain's Club, about $7,608 a year on one bill. Set that against The Preserve at Corkscrew's roughly $3,240 in annual HOA dues plus a district assessment in the $2,600 to $3,500 range, and the all-in totals land closer together than the headline structures suggest. What differs is where the money goes and who can change it.

The Place at Corkscrew is the new-construction alternative — same two builders, about two miles east of Alico Road, still selling into a 1,325-home build-out. Its CDD runs roughly $1,500 to $3,500 a year depending on lot and phase, with HOA dues around $300 to $350 a month. You get a builder warranty, incentives, and a bigger amenity campus, plus a community whose costs are still being allocated rather than settled.

For the wider corridor, our Estero community comparison lines up the main contenders, and Rapallo at Coconut Point is the closer-in option for a buyer who wants amenities without a district assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Does The Preserve at Corkscrew have a CDD fee?

Yes — the Cypress Shadows Community Development District, covering 441 platted lots, billed on the Lee County tax bill rather than through the HOA. Published figures put the FY2026 final adopted assessment at roughly $2,644 to $3,480 per lot per year by lot width and bond series. Verify your lot against the current tax bill before you write an offer.

Why is the gate here more expensive than at nearby communities?

Because it is staffed. The district budgets a Contracts-Security Services line — "the cost of the district's security personnel and equipment" — at $208,500 adopted for FY2026 and $214,755 as proposed for FY2027. With gate utilities and repairs, total gatehouse spending was $219,500 adopted for FY2026, about $498 per home across 441 lots. Most neighboring communities use unstaffed card- or code-operated entries.

Is the community sold out, and can you still buy there?

It is built out, so every purchase is a resale, not a builder contract. Inventory is thin — roughly three homes listed in late September 2026 at a median asking price near $609,000, against about 15 closings over the prior twelve months at a median sold price of $627,800.

What does the HOA fee cover, and what does it cost?

Published figures disagree and carry different dates: about $810 per quarter verified against MLS sheets in July 2026, and $920 per quarter reported as of February 2023. The HOA covers lawn care, exterior pest control, and the amenity campus; the gatehouse, lakes, and district infrastructure sit in the CDD budget. The estoppel letter settles it.

Is The Preserve at Corkscrew in a flood zone?

It is generally mapped in FEMA Flood Zone X, the lower-risk classification, per Village of Estero mapping — but that is community-level, not parcel-confirmed. Pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map and any elevation certificate for the exact address before you waive contingencies.

The bottom line

The Preserve at Corkscrew offers something unusual on Corkscrew Road: a finished community, a full amenity campus, and a person at the gate — with that person's cost sitting in a public budget anyone can read. Roughly $498 a home a year buys the staffed entry, it arrives on your tax bill rather than your HOA statement, and the board resets it annually. That is a perfectly reasonable thing to buy; it is just not a thing most buyers know they are buying until the first tax bill lands. The only number that matters is the one attached to your lot, and it comes from the estoppel letter, the adopted budget, and the parcel's own tax bill.
