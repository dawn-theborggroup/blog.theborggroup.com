Bella Terra in Estero, FL: what buyers need to know in 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·July 31, 2026·7 min read

Bella Terra is a fully built-out, 1,899-unit gated community in Estero, three miles east of I-75 on Corkscrew Road — condos, villas, townhomes, and single-family homes priced from approximately $265,000 to over $900,000. It sits in FEMA Flood Zone X, includes cable and internet in the master HOA, and carries no mandatory golf membership. Amenities run to six pickleball courts, a resort pool with waterfall and heated spa, four tennis courts, and a 24-hour fitness center. In the past 12 months, 105 homes sold at a $440,000 median with a 96.8% sale-to-list ratio.

Bella Terra punches above its weight class. In a market full of new-construction communities demanding $600K-plus just to get through the gate, Bella Terra delivers resort amenities, 24-hour security, 100 acres of lakes, and 400 acres of conservation preserve starting in the $260s — with no mandatory golf membership, no flood insurance requirement, and cable and internet already included in your HOA. That's a value proposition you don't see often in Estero.

The community at a glance

Development ran from 2004 to 2014, and Bella Terra is now fully built out — 1,899 homes across six distinct product types, all wrapped in Northern Italian-style architecture with mature landscaping, lake views, and nature preserve backdrops. The location puts you roughly 20 minutes from Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach, close to Miromar Outlets, Coconut Point Mall, Florida Gulf Coast University, and RSW International Airport. Pinewoods Elementary, Three Oaks Middle, and Estero High School all serve the community.

Two costs most buyers underestimate — both work in Bella Terra's favor

Cable TV and high-speed internet are included in the master HOA. Buyers from out of state frequently underestimate this: in most Southwest Florida communities, cable and internet run $150-$250/month separately. At Bella Terra, that's already covered. There's also no mandatory golf membership, no initiation fee, and no minimum spend — in golf communities, mandatory membership culture can add $3,000-$8,000-plus annually in lifestyle costs that Bella Terra simply doesn't carry.

Bella Terra isn't trying to be the most exclusive address in Estero. It's trying to be the smartest value — and for buyers who want real amenities without a golf-community price tag, that's exactly the calculation that wins.

What Bella Terra actually costs — HOA and CDD, broken down

Bella Terra has a two-layer HOA structure plus a CDD on the tax bill. For a single-family home: the master HOA runs approximately $1,688/year (covering cable, internet, clubhouse, amenities, and common area maintenance), and the sub-association HOA adds approximately $952/year — about $2,640/year, or $220/month, combined. Separately, the CDD appears as an assessment on your annual property tax bill, not your HOA payment: the operations and maintenance portion runs about $736/year across all property types, and a bond/debt service component of $179-$419/year (depending on property type) continues until May 2035, after which only the O&M portion remains.

What to know before you buy

Flood zone and insurance

Bella Terra sits in FEMA Flood Zone X, the lowest federal flood risk designation — flood insurance isn't required by lenders here. During Hurricane Ian, Bella Terra saw wind damage but no flooding, which its inland position (no tidal estuary connection) east of I-75 helped prevent. For context, flood and wind insurance in Flood Zones AE or VE can run $10,000-$20,000-plus per year in coastal communities; at Bella Terra you're primarily budgeting standard homeowner's insurance, typically at or below the Lee County average thanks to post-2004 concrete-block, hip-roof construction. A wind mitigation inspection can reduce your premium another 20-30%.

HOA approval takes time — start it early

Bella Terra requires buyer HOA approval before closing: an application, a background check, and a processing window. Submit it as early as possible after you go under contract — standard for most gated Estero communities, but timing it correctly protects your closing date. See our guide to HOA approval for buyers in Bonita Springs gated communities for what the process generally looks like.

Every transaction is a resale — and that's an advantage

Because Bella Terra is fully built out, you're buying from a homeowner, not a builder. That means no escalation clauses, no preferred-lender incentive pressure, and no design-center overruns inflating the final price. You can schedule an independent inspection without builder restrictions, and your buyer's agent represents only your interests — no competing sales rep dynamic from a builder's office.
