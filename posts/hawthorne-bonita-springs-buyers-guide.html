Hawthorne Bonita Springs FL: do you need flood insurance outside a flood zone? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 25, 2026·10 min read

Do you need flood insurance in Hawthorne if it isn’t in a flood zone?

Your lender probably won’t make you buy it — but Citizens might, and the flood map isn’t the whole story. All 16 Hawthorne addresses we checked against FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer on November 25, 2026 came back shaded Zone X: the 0.2 percent annual chance, or “500-year,” floodplain. That’s moderate risk, outside the special flood hazard area where federally backed mortgages require flood coverage. But starting January 1, 2027, Citizens Property Insurance requires flood coverage on every policy with wind coverage, Zone X included. So the real question for a Hawthorne buyer isn’t “am I in a flood zone?” It’s “who’s going to insure this home, and what will they require?”

That’s the question we’d want answered before you fall in love with a lake view here. Below is what Hawthorne is, what it costs, what “shaded X” really means for your insurance, and how to buy in a fully built-out community where every sale is a resale.

What Hawthorne is

Hawthorne is a gated, deed-restricted community in central Bonita Springs, set near Imperial Parkway and Shangri-La Road, in Lee County’s 34135 ZIP code. Centex began building it in the mid-2000s and Lennar finished it. It’s built out now, so there’s no sales center and no builder incentive to wait for.

  • 463 residences — about 307 single-family homes and 156 coach homes, according to our community guide.
  • Eight lakes and a greenbelt walkway connecting the neighborhoods, with two gated entrances.
  • Two amenity centers, each with a resort-style pool and spa, a fitness center, and a community room, plus tennis and pickleball courts.
  • No golf course. That means no club membership, no food minimum, and no assessment tied to running a course.

Downtown Bonita Springs, Riverside Park, and the restaurants along Old 41 are a few minutes away. Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach Preserve are roughly six miles west, and Coconut Point is a short drive north.

Homes and pricing in 2026

Hawthorne’s single-family neighborhoods — Ashmont, Chesapeake, Cobblestone, and Emory Oaks — range from two- and three-bedroom layouts to larger pool homes on the lakes. They generally sell from the $500,000s into the $900,000s. In late November 2026, the four single-family homes we saw listed ran from $489,000 to $639,000, from about 1,637 to 2,984 square feet.

The coach-home enclaves, Chesapeake Cove and Hide Away Cove, offer two-story coach homes with garages and their own pools, typically in the $400,000s.

Listing data we reviewed showed single-family HOA fees of roughly $339 to $522 a month, depending on the neighborhood, and an average annual property tax bill around $5,500. Depending on where you buy, dues typically cover lawn and landscape care, irrigation, cable and internet, and both amenity centers. The estoppel letter is the only number that binds anyone, so treat these as a starting point.

What “shaded Zone X” means for your insurance

Flood insurance is one of the top things buyers ask us about in Bonita Springs, and Hawthorne is a good example of why the answer is rarely a simple yes or no.

1. The map says moderate risk, not no risk

Every address we sampled sits on FIRM panel 12071C0657G, which took effect November 17, 2022. Shaded X means FEMA estimates about a 0.2 percent chance of flooding in any year. FEMA’s own FloodSmart site notes that from 2014 to 2024, nearly one-third of NFIP claims came from outside high-risk flood areas. The same FEMA map shows higher-risk AE zones not far from Hawthorne along the area’s river and coastal floodplains, which is why we check every parcel rather than trusting the community name.

2. Your lender and your insurer are asking different questions

A federally backed mortgage requires flood insurance only in the special flood hazard area — the A and V zones. Shaded X is outside it, so most lenders won’t require a policy here. Citizens works differently. Its flood requirement has been phasing in by dwelling value since 2024 — $600,000 and up in 2024, $500,000 in 2025, $400,000 in 2026 — and on January 1, 2027 it reaches every policy regardless of value. The threshold is measured on your Citizens dwelling coverage, not your purchase price, so the number on your declarations page is what counts. Our breakdown of the Citizens 2027 flood requirement covers how proof works and why the renewal date is what controls you.

3. Coach homes may follow different rules

Citizens exempts its condominium unit-owner forms, among others. Some coach homes are insured on a unit-owner form and some on a dwelling form, depending on how the building is legally set up and insured. In a community with 156 coach homes, that’s worth confirming early — the exemption follows the policy paperwork, not what the building looks like.

4. The price follows the house, not just the zone

Under FEMA’s current NFIP pricing, your premium depends on property-specific factors like elevation, distance to water, and the cost to rebuild — the zone letter alone doesn’t set it. Bonita Springs also helps here: the city holds a Class 5 Community Rating System rating, which gives NFIP policyholders a 25 percent discount, and FEMA confirmed in November 2024 that the city would keep it. If a quote for a Hawthorne home doesn’t show that discount, ask why.

One more document to watch for: Florida Statute 689.302 requires a residential seller to give you a written flood disclosure at or before contract. The version in effect since October 1, 2025 covers the seller’s knowledge of flood damage during their ownership, flood insurance claims, and flood-related assistance. It’s separate from the Seller’s Property Disclosure. For the basics behind the zone letters, see our guide to Southwest Florida flood zones.

Who Hawthorne fits

Hawthorne works well if you want a gated, amenity-rich address in the middle of Bonita Springs without paying for golf you won’t play. Two clubhouses mean the pool and gym are close to almost every street, and the location puts downtown, the beaches, and Coconut Point within an easy drive.

It’s a weaker fit if you want a golf membership attached to your home, a big lot, or new construction. Homes here were built from the mid-2000s through about 2013, so many original roofs are now in the 13- to 20-year range — and roof age is the first thing most Florida insurers look at.

Buying in Hawthorne

Because Hawthorne is fully built out, there’s no builder rep, no design center, and no incentive clock. The work moves to three places instead:

  • The neighborhood. Each neighborhood association has its own fee, rules on leasing, pets, and vehicles, and its own approval steps. Our guide to HOA approval for buyers in Bonita Springs explains how approvals usually work in gated communities here.
  • The house. Roof age, impact glass or shutters, and the wind mitigation report all move your insurance cost. Our post on four-point and wind mitigation inspections explains what insurers ask for.
  • The insurance. Get real quotes — homeowners and flood — during your inspection period, not the week before closing. Our Bonita Springs homeowners insurance guide covers what drives the price.

What would we look at first if you called us about Hawthorne?

We’d start with the insurance picture, because in Hawthorne it’s the number most likely to change your monthly cost. We’d confirm the flood zone for the exact parcel, then figure out which carriers are realistically going to write the home — because whether Citizens is in the mix decides whether flood coverage is optional or required for you.

Next, we’d look at the age and type of the roof, the openings, and any existing wind mitigation report, since those decide whether a private carrier is even an option. On a coach home, we’d confirm how the building is insured before you assume anything about which policy form you’ll need.

Then we’d read the neighborhood association’s budget, reserves, and estoppel early enough to still matter. When it’s handled well, you go into closing knowing your full monthly cost — HOA, taxes, wind, and flood — with no surprise at your first renewal. That’s the part we take care of for you.

How Hawthorne compares

Against Spanish Wells. Spanish Wells is an older gated Bonita Springs community where golf is optional, but its master association owns the 27-hole club, and every sale pays a $4,800 resale capital contribution. Hawthorne has no club at all, so the course is never part of your bill. Spanish Wells has a lower entry price on condos; Hawthorne has newer homes.

Against Bonita National. Bonita National is also a Lennar community in Bonita Springs, but golf is built into the deed on many homes and the community sits farther east on Bonita Beach Road. If you want golf bundled in, Bonita National makes sense. If you don’t, Hawthorne keeps your fees simpler and puts you closer to downtown.

Against Pelican Landing. Pelican Landing is the bigger, pricier step, with a private beach park on Big Hickory Island and a $6,000 resale transfer fee. Hawthorne trades the beach park for a lower price point and two neighborhood amenity centers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Hawthorne in Bonita Springs in a flood zone?

Not a high-risk one. All 16 Hawthorne addresses we checked against FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer on November 25, 2026 came back shaded Zone X, the 0.2 percent annual chance (500-year) floodplain, on Lee County FIRM panel 12071C0657G, effective November 17, 2022. Shaded X is moderate risk and sits outside the special flood hazard area. Always confirm the exact parcel before you offer.

Do I need flood insurance to buy in Hawthorne?

Your lender usually won’t require it, because shaded Zone X is outside the high-risk flood zone. But if your homeowners policy is with Citizens Property Insurance, Citizens requires flood coverage on all policies with wind coverage starting January 1, 2027, including homes in Zone X. Unit-owner forms are exempt, so coach-home buyers should check which policy form applies.

What are the HOA fees in Hawthorne Bonita Springs?

Listing data we reviewed in November 2026 showed single-family HOA fees of roughly $339 to $522 a month, depending on the neighborhood. Fees typically cover the two amenity centers and, depending on the neighborhood, lawn care, irrigation, and cable and internet. There’s no golf course, so there are no club dues or food minimums. Confirm the exact amount on the estoppel letter.

How much do homes cost in Hawthorne?

Single-family homes generally run from the $500,000s into the $900,000s, and coach homes typically trade in the $400,000s. In November 2026 the single-family homes listed ranged from $489,000 to $639,000. Every sale is a resale, because the community is built out.

Does Bonita Springs get a flood insurance discount?

Yes. The City of Bonita Springs holds a Class 5 rating in FEMA’s Community Rating System, which gives NFIP flood policyholders in the city a 25 percent discount. FEMA confirmed in November 2024 that the city would keep that rating. Make sure the discount appears on any NFIP quote you get for a Hawthorne home.
