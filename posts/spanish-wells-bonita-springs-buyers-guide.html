Spanish Wells Bonita Springs FL: do you pay for the golf club if you don’t join? 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 23, 2026·11 min read

Do you pay for the golf club in Spanish Wells if you don’t join?

Not in club dues — but you still own a share of it. In seven of Spanish Wells’ eight neighborhoods, golf membership is optional, so you can live behind the gates without paying golf or social dues. What’s not optional is the Spanish Wells Community Association, the master association every owner belongs to, and the recorded declaration says the 27-hole country club “is currently owned by the Association.” The master budget you pay through your neighborhood dues is kept separate from the club’s own budget, but the declaration refers to a “G&CC note payable,” and the association’s 2026 budget carried a debt-service line billed to all 1,361 owners. So the smart question isn’t “do I have to join?” It’s “what does owning the club put on the bill I pay either way?”

That’s the question behind almost every Spanish Wells conversation we have. Buyers hear “golf is optional” and stop there, because in Southwest Florida that usually means the club is someone else’s business. Here, it’s partly yours. Here’s what Spanish Wells is, what it costs, how the club ownership actually reaches you, and how to buy here well.

What Spanish Wells is

Spanish Wells is a gated golf community of about 600 acres in the City of Bonita Springs, in Lee County, with its main gate at 28001 Spanish Wells Boulevard just south of Bonita Beach Road. It’s one of the area’s older gated communities — the original declaration was recorded in 1995, and the club and neighborhoods go back further than that. Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach Preserve are roughly 15 minutes west, and I-75 is minutes east.

A few things define it:

  • Twenty-seven holes. Three nine-hole courses played in three different 18-hole combinations. The club is managed day to day by Troon, and the association describes a refresh of the North Course as underway.
  • About 1,361 residences. That’s the owner count the master association budgets against, spread across coach homes, condominiums, villas, and single-family homes.
  • A lot of associations. The declaration names eight neighborhoods — Units One, Two, and Three, the Golf Condominiums, Lake Club, Cordova, Las Brisas, and Marbella. Underneath those sit roughly 20 to 22 separate sub-associations, depending on which association document you read, each with its own management company and budget.
  • No CDD. Your costs run through the master association, your neighborhood, and, if you choose, the club.

Homes and pricing in 2026

Spanish Wells covers a wider price range than most gated golf communities in Bonita Springs:

  • Condos and coach homes — in enclaves like Las Brisas and Marbella, these generally start in the mid-$200,000s.
  • Single-family homes — from around the $500,000s to past $1.5 million for updated estate homes. Recent listings we reviewed this fall included single-family homes asking roughly $700,000 to $850,000.

Because every home here is a resale, condition and updates move value more than the street name does. Two homes at the same price can differ by a new roof, impact glass, and an updated kitchen — and by very different neighborhood fees.

What it costs to own

You don’t write a check to the master association. Your neighborhood association collects the master’s share inside your regular dues, along with its own costs. The 2027 master budget, adopted by the board and posted on the association’s site, works out to roughly:

  • About $1,818 a year per home (about $152 a month) in master association fees — $2,474,218 spread across 1,361 owners.
  • About $1,213 a year per home (about $101 a month) for the bulk cable and internet contract with Summit Broadband, budgeted at $1,640,265 across 1,352 owners.
  • Inside that master budget, the biggest items are the gatehouse and security contract (about $806,000), a $645,500 contribution to reserves, and association property insurance of about $140,000, up 23.5% from the year before.

Your neighborhood’s own fee sits on top of that, and it varies widely — some coach-home neighborhoods fold in nearly everything except electricity. On the way in, plan for a $4,800 resale capital contribution to the master association on every sale (in effect since July 1, 2024), and two estoppel certificates: one from the master ($299, or $418 with the rush fee) and a separate one from your sub-association.

How club ownership reaches a non-member

This is the part most buyers miss. Spanish Wells doesn’t just have a club next door. Its master association owns the club, and the declaration is built around that fact.

1. The club is association property, run by the board

The declaration defines the Country Club — the 27-hole course, cart paths, tennis and bocce courts, exercise facilities, and clubhouse — and states that it is currently owned by the association. While that’s true, the master board exercises the club owner’s rights, including setting user fees and access terms, and the bylaws add a ninth, at-large seat to the board. In plain English: the people who govern your master assessment also govern the club.

2. The budgets are separate — and there’s a note

The club has its own budget, and the master association’s published budget doesn’t include it. That’s good news for a non-golfer. But the declaration’s own language mentions a “G&CC note payable principal and interest,” and the 2026 budget listed “SWCA Debt Service Received from 1361 Owners” at about $364,000 — roughly $268 per home for the year. The 2027 budget presents revenue differently and doesn’t break out a separate debt-service line. Before you offer, you want to know what that debt is, what it’s for, its balance, when it ends, and where it sits in the 2027 numbers.

3. The course is meant to stay a course

The declaration says the club parcels continue as a 27-hole golf course and country club unless two-thirds of the members voting at a properly called meeting approve a change. For buyers worried about a failing course being redeveloped — a real concern in parts of Southwest Florida — that’s meaningful protection. It also means the community’s commitment to the course is long-term.

4. If the club is ever sold, the math changes

The declaration plans for a future where the association no longer owns the club. In that case, the club’s new owner would carry 20% of the master assessments, the club would become a voting parcel, and the club owner would appoint the ninth board seat. Nothing suggests a sale is coming. But it tells you how tightly the club and the master association are tied together.

None of this makes Spanish Wells a risky buy. It makes it a community where the club’s health is everyone’s business — and where reading two budgets, not one, is part of doing it right.

Who Spanish Wells fits

Spanish Wells works well if you want a gated, established golf setting close to Bonita Beach without being required to pay for golf you won’t play. It suits golfers who want 27 holes and some variety, and it suits non-golfers who like fairway views, tennis and pickleball nearby, and a price range that starts well below most gated golf communities in coastal Bonita Springs.

It’s a weaker fit if you want a short-term rental. Leases must be in writing, run at least 30 continuous days, and a new lease can’t start until at least 30 days after the last one began. You must lease the whole home — no room rentals or subleasing — and the lease goes through your sub-association first, then the master association.

Buying in Spanish Wells

The process looks like any Florida resale, with three local twists. First, neighborhood matters more than street. In one neighborhood golf membership is bundled with ownership, and each sub-association has its own fee, rules, and approval process. Cordova is the neighborhood most often reported as the bundled one — confirm it in writing with the club for the specific address. Second, approvals run through your sub-association — for leases, the sub-association approves first and then forwards the lease to the master — so ask each neighborhood what it requires of a buyer and how long it takes. Third, the money at closing includes the $4,800 master capital contribution, two estoppels, and possibly a sub-association contribution of its own.

What would we look at first if you called us about Spanish Wells?

We’d start with the neighborhood, not the house. Once we know which of the eight neighborhoods — and which sub-association — a home sits in, we know whether golf comes with it, what the full monthly fee really covers, and who has to approve you. That alone rules homes in or out faster than any showing.

Next, we’d read the master budget and the club’s numbers side by side. A non-golfer here is still a part-owner of a 27-hole club, so we’d want to understand the club note, the reserve picture, and whether anything on the horizon — like the North Course refresh — could show up on the master side. Then we’d look at the sub-association’s own reserves and insurance, because in a community this established, that’s where surprises tend to live.

When it’s handled well, you walk into closing knowing exactly what you’ll pay every month, what you’ll pay once, and what you co-own. That’s the part we take care of for you.

How Spanish Wells compares

Against Pelican Landing. Both are established, gated Bonita Springs communities where golf is optional. At Pelican Landing, golf at the adjacent club is a completely separate membership, and the master association includes its private beach park on Big Hickory Island. In Spanish Wells, the master association owns the club itself. If you never plan to play, Pelican Landing keeps the club further from your bill; Spanish Wells offers a lower entry price and gives you a say in the club through the association.

Against Spring Run at The Brooks. Spring Run in Estero is the opposite model: the golf club and the homeowners association are one and the same, and golf is bundled into ownership. If you’ll play several times a week, bundling can be the better value. If you won’t, Spanish Wells lets you skip the dues.

Against Bonita Bay. Bonita Bay is the luxury step up, with five golf courses, a marina, and a beach pavilion, at a much higher price point. Spanish Wells is the more attainable way into a gated Bonita Springs golf address.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is golf membership mandatory in Spanish Wells?

Not in most of the community. In seven of the eight neighborhoods, joining the golf club is optional, and you can choose golf, social, or no membership at all. One neighborhood bundles membership with ownership, so confirm the rule for the specific address in writing before you offer.

Who owns the Spanish Wells golf course?

The Spanish Wells Community Association, the master association every owner belongs to. The recorded declaration says the country club is currently owned by the association, its board exercises the club owner’s rights, and the club is managed day to day by Troon.

What are the HOA fees in Spanish Wells?

They depend on the neighborhood. The 2027 master budget works out to about $1,818 a year per home plus about $1,213 a year for bulk cable and internet, collected through your neighborhood association along with its own fee. Each of the roughly 20 sub-associations sets its own amount.

What does it cost to buy into Spanish Wells?

Beyond normal closing costs, the master association charges a $4,800 resale capital contribution on each sale and $299 for its estoppel certificate. Your sub-association issues a separate estoppel and may have its own transfer or capital charge. Club initiation fees apply only if you join.

Does Spanish Wells have a CDD?

No. Spanish Wells has no community development district. Your costs come through the master association, your neighborhood association, and the club if you choose to join.

Can you rent out a home in Spanish Wells?

Yes, with limits. Leases must be in writing, run at least 30 continuous days, and can start no sooner than 30 days after the prior lease began. You must lease the entire home, subleasing isn’t allowed, and the lease goes through your sub-association before the master association.
