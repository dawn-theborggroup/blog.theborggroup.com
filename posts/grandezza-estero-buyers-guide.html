Grandezza in Estero, FL: is the country club membership worth it in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 30, 2026·9 min read

Grandezza is one of the few Estero communities where the club isn't optional. Buy a home here and you're signed up for baseline country club dues — roughly $5,300 a year — whether or not you ever pick up a golf club, on top of a separate neighborhood HOA fee that varies depending on which of the community's 12 sections you're in. For buyers used to communities where golf and social membership are add-ons, that structure is the first thing worth understanding, not the last.

Grandezza also finished building out years ago, so every purchase today runs through the resale process rather than a builder sales office. Here's what we tell buyers who ask us about this Estero country club specifically, and how it stacks up against Wildcat Run and Bonita Bay — the two communities we hear it cross-shopped against most.

Grandezza at a glance: an established, resale-only country club

Grandezza sits off Corkscrew Road in Estero, in the 33928 zip code, with a gated entrance staffed around the clock. The community built out over the 2000s and has been fully resale for years now — there's no builder sales center, no design-center change orders, and no incentive timing to negotiate around. What you're buying is an established, landscaped, socially mature community rather than a growing one.

The neighborhood count matters here: Grandezza is organized into 12 distinct sections spread across 979 homes (the community's own materials round that to roughly 1,000), which is more than double the size of a small, single-section club like Wildcat Run. A handful of those sections — Avalon, Oakwood, and Sabal Palm among them — maintain their own private clubhouses and pools in addition to the master club, which is worth knowing if a listing mentions a "private pool" that isn't the main clubhouse pool.

Location-wise, Grandezza is about 15 minutes from Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW), close to Coconut Point, Miromar Outlets, and Florida Gulf Coast University, and zoned to Lee County schools.

The question we get most about Grandezza isn't "is the club nice" — it clearly is. It's "do I have to pay for it even if I don't golf?" The honest answer is yes, and that's exactly the number buyers need in front of them before they fall in love with a floor plan.

Homes and pricing: what a 2026 resale actually costs

Grandezza's housing stock is genuinely varied for a single community: single-family detached homes, attached villas, carriage homes, and condominiums, built between roughly 2001 and 2009. Community-wide, prices span from the low $200,000s up to the low $2 millions, with condos and coach homes typically starting in the $300,000s and single-family and custom estates running from about $800,000 to $1.8 million-plus. Median sold price came in around $615,000 as of April 2026.

Current MLS activity backs that up: as of this writing there are roughly 18 active listings in Grandezza ranging from about $229,900 to just over $1 million, working out to around $245 per square foot on average. Over the past year, about 55 homes changed hands at an average asking price of $690,629 and an average selling price of $656,227 — a 95% list-to-sell ratio, though homes are taking an average of 148 days to sell, which is slower than the flip-a-switch market of a few years ago and gives buyers real room to negotiate.

  • Neighborhood HOA dues run roughly $163 to $667 a month depending on the section, covering things like landscaping, irrigation water, cable, and high-speed internet in many neighborhoods
  • Mandatory baseline club dues add approximately $5,300 a year on top of that HOA fee, and apply community-wide regardless of golf participation
  • No CDD fee turned up in the records we reviewed for Grandezza — confirm this on the Lee County tax bill for your specific address, since it's a parcel-level detail rather than a blanket rule
  • Golf membership itself carries its own initiation fee and annual dues, separate from both the HOA and the baseline club dues — more on that below

That two-line, sometimes three-line fee structure — HOA, baseline club dues, and optional golf dues — is the single most common source of sticker shock we see with Grandezza. Treat the community-wide ranges above as a starting point, not a quote: a current comparative market analysis on the specific home and neighborhood you're considering is the only way to know your real all-in monthly number.

Amenities and lifestyle: three ways to belong to the club

The Club at Grandézza is built around a 53,000-square-foot clubhouse with full-service dining, an 18-hole championship golf course playing to a par of 72 across roughly 7,071 yards, a Junior Olympic-size pool with a cabana area, six lighted Har-Tru tennis courts, two bocce ball courts, a basketball court, two fitness centers, and walking and biking trails winding through 41 acres of preserve and 23 lakes. The club is now managed by Heritage Golf Group, which operates more than 40 clubs across 15 states and has opened Grandezza to non-resident golf members as well.

Membership comes in three tiers:

  • Social membership — clubhouse dining, tennis, bocce, pool, and fitness access; this is the baseline every homeowner effectively carries
  • Sports membership — adds seasonal golf privileges on top of the social tier
  • Golf membership — unlimited year-round golf, capped at 400 members community-wide

That 400-member cap is worth sitting with. Grandezza has nearly 1,000 homes and only 400 golf memberships available, which means golf access isn't guaranteed just because you close on a house — unlike Wildcat Run, a few miles away, which markets its lack of any golf waitlist as a selling point. If unlimited golf is the reason you're looking at Grandezza, confirm current membership availability and the initiation fee before you write an offer, not after.

The buying process: two due-diligence tracks, not one

Because Grandezza sold out of new construction years ago, every purchase runs through a standard Florida resale transaction — typically the FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract — negotiated directly with a seller through your own buyer's agent rather than a builder's on-site representative. That gives you real room to negotiate price, closing costs, and timeline, especially with homes averaging 148 days on market. But it also means the due-diligence checklist runs on two parallel tracks that don't exist in a simpler, single-HOA community:

  • Your neighborhood HOA estoppel letter — Florida law requires the seller's association to provide one before closing, disclosing what's currently owed, any pending special assessments, and whether the account is in good standing. This covers your section-level HOA (the $163–$667/month figure), not the club.
  • Your club dues verification, separately — because the mandatory $5,300-a-year club dues run through Heritage Golf Group's management rather than your neighborhood association, ask your agent to confirm club account status, any club-level assessments, and — if you want golf — current membership cap availability and the initiation fee as its own line item.
  • Request the HOA's budget and reserve study, not just the current due. Our guide to evaluating a Florida HOA before you buy walks through exactly what to ask for and why a healthy reserve fund today is what keeps a manageable monthly due from turning into a special assessment in five years.
  • Order a full home inspection. Grandezza's homes are now roughly 15 to 25 years old, so deferred maintenance — roofs, HVAC systems, pool equipment — is the seller's responsibility to disclose, not a builder's warranty to fix.
  • A wind mitigation and four-point inspection are worth ordering for homes in this age range; both are typically required by Florida insurers for homes over 10–15 years old and can meaningfully affect your premium.

On flood risk, we didn't find a published, Grandezza-specific flood zone designation in the sources we reviewed for this guide. Pull the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map for the exact parcel you're considering rather than assuming either way — designations are property-specific and can vary within a single community, and our guide to flood insurance costs in Southwest Florida covers how that number affects your overall budget.

How Grandezza compares to Wildcat Run and Bonita Bay

Two other Estero-area golf communities come up constantly when buyers are cross-shopping Grandezza:

Wildcat Run, also in Estero, is a smaller, simpler community — 450 homes on 514 acres around a single Arnold Palmer-designed course, with condos from about $230,000 to $599,000 and single-family homes from roughly $425,000 to $2.3 million. Its defining advantage is a single membership tier with no golf waitlist at all. If you golf regularly and want guaranteed, uncomplicated access without a tiered dues structure to sort through, Wildcat Run is the more straightforward fit.

Bonita Bay, in neighboring Bonita Springs, plays in a different league entirely — a 2,400-acre community with five championship courses (90 holes total), a full-service marina with Gulf of Mexico access, a private beach club, and more than 60 distinct neighborhoods, with prices from around $600,000 for condos to $8–9 million or more for waterfront estates. Grandezza can't match that scale, but it also doesn't ask for that price of entry: its low-$200,000s floor is roughly a third of Bonita Bay's condo starting point, for buyers who want country-club amenities and a single well-maintained course without marina-scale carrying costs.

Put simply: Wildcat Run wins on membership simplicity, Bonita Bay wins on amenity scale, and Grandezza sits in between — a bigger, more varied home inventory than Wildcat Run, a mandatory (if capped) three-tier club structure instead of Bonita Bay's larger but more expensive one, and the lowest entry price point of the three.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Grandezza still selling new construction homes?

No. Grandezza built out across its 12 neighborhoods by the late 2000s, so every current purchase is a resale transaction through the MLS rather than a builder contract, and there's no on-site sales center or design center.

Is country club membership mandatory at Grandezza?

Yes, in effect. Nearly every home carries mandatory baseline club dues of roughly $5,300 a year in addition to neighborhood HOA fees. Golf membership itself is a separate, optional, capped tier on top of that baseline — you're not required to golf, but you are required to pay into the club.

Does Grandezza have a CDD fee?

We didn't find a Community Development District assessment tied to Grandezza in the sources we reviewed, which would set it apart from several newer Estero communities. Confirm this directly against the Lee County property tax bill for the specific address you're considering, since CDD status is parcel-specific.

How many homes are in Grandezza?

Grandezza has just under 1,000 homes — 979 per current inventory records, with the community's own materials citing roughly 1,000 — spread across 12 distinct neighborhoods with a mix of single-family homes, attached villas, carriage homes, and condominiums.

Is there a waitlist for golf membership at Grandezza?

Golf membership at Grandezza is capped at 400 members, and current availability should be confirmed at the time of your purchase rather than assumed. That's a different model than nearby Wildcat Run, which markets itself specifically on having no golf membership waitlist at all.

Weighing Grandezza against another Estero country club?

We'll pull current comps for the exact neighborhood you're considering, confirm club and HOA account status before you write an offer, and check golf membership availability with Heritage Golf Group.
