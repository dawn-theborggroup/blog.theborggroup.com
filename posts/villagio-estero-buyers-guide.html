Villagio Estero FL: is Estero's lowest-priced gated community worth it in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 19, 2026·11 min read

Is Villagio in Estero a good buy in 2026?

Villagio is a 514-unit gated community of two-story coach homes on 70 acres off Three Oaks Parkway in Estero, FL, built between 2004 and 2006 by Royal Palm Communities. Current listings run roughly $219,700 to $350,000 with a $580 monthly condo fee and no CDD assessment — the lowest-priced gated address in Estero by a wide margin. The catch isn't the fee. It's that Villagio is the one Estero neighborhood where a majority of sampled listing addresses map into a FEMA high-risk flood zone, which makes the association's master flood policy the single number you need before you write an offer.

Estero's trailing-twelve-month median sale price is $503,750. You can buy a two-bedroom coach home at Villagio, gated, with an attached garage, for about $220,000.

That gap is not a mistake, and it is not a distressed community. It is what a 2004-to-2006 condo community looks like in a city where almost everything else is single-family new construction with a CDD bond attached.

Here is the sentence that should shape how you shop Villagio: the monthly fee is the cheapest part of this purchase to understand, and the flood determination is the most expensive part to get wrong.

Buyers ask us about Villagio constantly — it is usually the first thing that comes up when someone wants an Estero address under $350,000. So let's walk it in order.

What Villagio actually is

Villagio sits on Three Oaks Parkway, between Estero Parkway and Corkscrew Road, just west of I-75. The South County Regional Library is walking distance. Estero Community Park is a short ride. Coconut Point, Miromar Outlets, Gulf Coast Town Center, Hertz Arena, FGCU, and Southwest Florida International Airport are all inside a 15-minute drive.

514 coach homes on 70 acres, wrapped around five lakes with fountains. Developed by Royal Palm Communities, built out 2004 to 2006. Every sale is a resale — there is no builder here and no new construction phase, which means no builder incentives and no sales center to negotiate against.

The buildings are two stories, arranged in small eight-unit clusters rather than the sixteen-unit buildings common to the era. Every unit has an attached one-car garage — rare at this price in Estero.

Four floor plans:

  • 1 bedroom + den, 1,170 sq ft
  • 2 bedroom / 2 bath, 1,048 to 1,192 sq ft
  • 2 bedroom + den / 2 bath, 1,667 sq ft
  • 3 bedroom + den / 2 bath, 1,828 sq ft

The amenity package is the part that surprises people. Villagio is built around a Town Center holding a café serving breakfast and light lunch, an evening bar, a private cinema seating roughly 50, a fitness center, a library and club room, two pools plus a spa, and hard-surface tennis and pickleball courts. There is on-site management and a 24-hour gate.

For a community whose entry price starts with a 2, that is an unusual amount of built infrastructure.

Two 2026 closings tell you more than any list price does. A 1,828 sq ft unit listed at $330,000 closed at $310,000 in October. Another listed at $325,000 closed at $295,000 in April.

That is roughly 6% to 9% under ask, in a zip code where the trailing-twelve-month average is 92.9% of asking across 1,490 closed sales, with a median 66 days on market and 412 active listings against 130 pending. Estero is a buyer's market right now, and Villagio is not an exception to it. There is room to negotiate, and the $70,000 reduction sitting on one active listing tells you sellers here know it.

The fee: $580 a month, and what it is actually buying

Six current Villagio listings report the same condo fee: $580 per month, or $6,960 a year.

You will find older sources quoting $390. Treat that as stale — the current MLS fee field says $580, and that is the number to underwrite.

What the $580 covers, per the listings' own maintenance schedules:

  • Water and sewer
  • Trash removal
  • Cable television and high-speed internet
  • Building insurance
  • Exterior pest control
  • Landscaping and irrigation water
  • Private road maintenance and street lights
  • 24-hour gate and security
  • Reserves and repairs
  • Professional association management

Run that against a single-family home in Estero. You would be paying your own water and sewer bill, your own trash, your own cable and internet, your own lawn service, your own pest control, and — the big one — your own full homeowners policy on the structure. At Villagio, the building insurance sits inside the fee. Your personal policy is an HO-6, covering your interior, your improvements, and your contents, which is a materially smaller premium than insuring a whole house in Lee County in 2026. Our guide to homeowners insurance in Bonita Springs and Estero covers what drives those premiums.

The fee is not cheap. But it is not an HOA fee that only buys a gate and a pool.

And there is no CDD

This matters more in Estero than almost anywhere in Southwest Florida.

Villagio is one of sixteen Estero communities carrying no Community Development District assessment. Compare the FY2027 per-unit CDD figures on the communities Villagio competes with for your attention:

  • Verdana Village — $1,414.90 to $3,337.07
  • WildBlue — $2,851.13 to $5,411.60
  • Preserve at Corkscrew — roughly $2,634 to $3,447
  • The Place at Corkscrew — $1,185.41 to $1,717.32
  • Bella Terra — $860.69 to $1,279.22
  • Stoneybrook at Estero — $808.28 to $1,514.11

A CDD line is a bond repayment that rides on your tax bill for decades, so add a community's CDD to its HOA before you compare anything. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero walks the math.

Property taxes at Villagio are modest for the area — two 2025 bills on Villagio units came in at $2,773 and $3,073.92. The Village of Estero held its millage flat at 0.73 mills for FY2026-27 inside a $90.8 million budget. If this will be your primary residence, file for the Florida homestead exemption before the March 1 deadline.

The flood question — and this is the real work

Here is where Villagio requires more diligence than any other Estero community on your list.

In an October 2026 sample of 150 Estero listing addresses checked against FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer, 91% of Estero mapped outside the special flood hazard area in Zone X. Verdana Village, Bella Terra, Corkscrew Shores, The Place at Corkscrew, Stoneybrook, RiverCreek, and Belle Lago all sampled at 100% Zone X.

Villagio sampled at 75% A-series — the only Estero neighborhood in that study where high-risk addresses were the majority rather than the exception.

Two honest caveats, because they matter. That figure rests on only eight sampled addresses in a 514-unit community, so it is sensitive to a handful of lots. And a modeled third-party flood score on two individual Villagio units reads "minimal risk." Those two sources disagree.

That disagreement is the whole point. A neighborhood-level percentage and a modeled risk score are both guesses about your building. Neither is a determination. You pull the FEMA parcel determination for the exact address, and you ask the association for its flood policy — and you do both before you are under contract, not during inspection. Our step-by-step guide to checking a home's flood risk in Estero covers the Village's free elevation-certificate archive and the municipal instruments to use, and which Southwest Florida flood zones to think twice about puts the zone letters in context.

There is also geography worth knowing. The Village is moving ahead with a $6.5 million regional stormwater pond on Village-owned land behind the post office on Three Oaks Parkway, designed to hold water back from a constricted stretch of the North Branch of the Estero River. It is funded entirely by a $4.9 million FEMA Hazard Mitigation Grant and a $1.6 million CDBG-Disaster Recovery grant, with design and permitting due by March 31, 2027. The project exists because this corridor backed up badly in 2017. Read that as both a caution about the area's drainage history and a sign the Village is spending grant money to fix it.

Two condo-specific rules that work in your favor

Now the part almost nobody explains to buyers, and the reason a flood zone reads differently in a condo than in a house.

First: the building's flood policy is the association's problem, not yours. For a condominium, the structure is typically covered by an NFIP Residential Condominium Building Association Policy — a master flood policy held by the association. Its premium sits inside your $580, not on a bill with your name on it. Your own flood exposure is limited to contents and interior improvements under your HO-6. So the question to ask is not "what will flood insurance cost me," it is "does the association carry flood coverage on this building, at what limit, and what did it do to the budget this year." Ask for the declarations page. Not the summary — the declarations page.

Second: Citizens' 2027 flood mandate does not reach your unit-owner policy. Citizens Property Insurance has been phasing in a requirement that wind-covered homes outside a flood zone also carry flood insurance — $400,000-plus dwelling values from January 1, 2026, and all remaining personal lines residential policies regardless of value from January 1, 2027. That deadline is about to land on a lot of Estero single-family owners. Condominium unit-owner forms are expressly exempt. If you are weighing a Villagio unit against a house on a Citizens policy, that exemption is a real and dated advantage, and it is about ten weeks from biting the other side of the comparison. Verify your own situation with your insurance agent — and if you are buying from a seller who already carries flood coverage, read our note on Addendum H and flood insurance protection first.

The buying process at Villagio — before you tour

Villagio has no sales center and no builder rep, which means nobody in the transaction is paid to hand you the documents that decide whether this is a good buy. Here is what we pull for clients first.

  1. Order the estoppel certificate. Florida caps preparation and delivery at $299, plus $119 to expedite within three business days and $179 if the parcel is delinquent. The association has ten business days to issue it — and if it misses that deadline, it cannot charge you at all.
  2. Request the full resale document package — declaration, bylaws, rules, the current budget, the most recent financial statements, and the last twelve months of board meeting minutes. The minutes are where a coming assessment shows up before it reaches the financials.
  3. Read the insurance declarations pages — wind and flood, separately. Confirm the limit, the deductible, and the carrier.
  4. Confirm the roof and the capital history. Several Villagio listings reference new roofs in 2025, exterior painting, and road resurfacing paid by the community. Verify what was done, when, and how it was funded — reserves or special assessment.
  5. Ask the litigation question in writing. Pending association litigation can affect financing and warrantability. The estoppel and the resale package both address it. Get the answer on paper.
  6. Check the leasing cap and pet rules before you fall in love. Listings report pets as "call, conditional," with a limit of two. If you are buying this as a seasonal rental, the minimum lease term and any annual rental cap decide whether your plan is even legal here.
  7. One thing you can skip worrying about. Florida's milestone inspection and structural integrity reserve study requirements apply to condominium buildings of three or more habitable stories. Villagio's buildings are two. That whole regime — the one driving fee increases across Florida — sits outside this community. How Villagio compares

Against Villages at Country Creek. Country Creek is the other end of Estero's affordability spectrum — bundled golf attached to the deed, which Villagio does not have. Its total carrying cost runs higher once the golf assessment lands, and about 25% of its sampled addresses map A-series. If golf is the point of the purchase, Country Creek wins. If it isn't, you are paying for a course you don't use. Our Villages at Country Creek guide has the numbers.

Against Breckenridge. Breckenridge is also all-resale, also gated, also attached homes, with par-3 golf included. But it carries a two-layer fee structure — master assessment plus a village fee — putting total annual recurring fees between $7,780 and $13,000 depending on which building you buy in. Villagio's single $6,960 is below the bottom of that range.

Against a newer Estero single-family home. You get more square footage, a private yard, and a two-car garage. You also pick up a CDD bond, a full homeowners policy on the structure, your own utilities and lawn care, and — if it's on a Citizens policy outside a flood zone — that January 1, 2027 requirement. The honest comparison isn't price against price. It's total annual carrying cost against total annual carrying cost. Frequently asked questions

How much are the HOA fees at Villagio in Estero?

Six current 2026 listings report a condo fee of $580 per month, or $6,960 a year. That covers water, sewer, trash, cable, internet, building insurance, exterior pest control, landscaping and irrigation, private road maintenance, street lights, the 24-hour gate, reserves, and professional management. Older sources quoting $390 are out of date.

Does Villagio have a CDD?

No. Villagio is one of sixteen Estero communities with no Community Development District assessment, which separates it from Verdana Village, WildBlue, The Place at Corkscrew, Bella Terra, Stoneybrook, and Preserve at Corkscrew — all of which add a CDD line to your tax bill on top of association dues.

Is Villagio in a flood zone?

Possibly, and more likely than elsewhere in Estero. In an October 2026 sample of Estero listing addresses checked against FEMA's flood layer, Villagio was the only neighborhood where high-risk A-series zones were the majority, at 75% of eight sampled addresses — against 91% Zone X for Estero as a whole. That sample is small, and a modeled risk score on individual units reads lower. Pull the FEMA determination for the specific address and request the association's flood declarations page before you make an offer.

Do Villagio's buildings need a milestone inspection or a reserve study?

No. Florida's milestone inspection and structural integrity reserve study requirements apply to condominium buildings of three or more habitable stories. Villagio's buildings are two stories, which places them outside both requirements — a meaningful difference from the mid-rise condo buildings now absorbing those costs across Florida.

Can you rent out a condo at Villagio?

Villagio has an active seasonal and annual rental market, but the minimum lease term and any annual rental cap are set by the association's governing documents, and they change. Get the current leasing restrictions in writing from the association before you buy with rental income in your plan.

The bottom line

Villagio is the cheapest gated door into Estero, and at $580 a month with no CDD, the carrying cost holds up against every newer community in the city once you add their bonds and their full homeowners policies back in. What you are trading for that price is square footage, a yard, and a flood question that takes real work to answer.

That work is answerable. It takes a parcel-level FEMA determination, the association's flood declarations page, the estoppel, and twelve months of minutes — and it takes having all four before you are emotionally committed to a unit.

That is the analysis we run with buyers before they tour. If Villagio or another Estero community is on your list, let us pull those documents with you first.
