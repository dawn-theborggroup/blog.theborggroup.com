Fort Myers & Fort Myers Beach: what buyers need to know in 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 15, 2026·9 min read

Fort Myers is the cultural and commercial heart of Southwest Florida — a city offering something genuinely rare on the Gulf Coast: real downtown energy alongside world-class waterfront living. The River District anchors the city with restaurants, galleries, live music, and a revitalized waterfront. Beyond downtown, Fort Myers stretches across gated golf and marina communities, family-friendly master-planned developments near the airport corridor, and — on Estero Island to the southwest — Fort Myers Beach, in the midst of a meaningful rebuilding effort following Hurricane Ian in 2022.

Fort Myers Beach: a rare window into new Gulf-front construction

New construction is rising on Estero Island with stricter, storm-resilient building codes — elevated pilings and FEMA-compliant standards. Many buyers see this as a rare chance to enter a Gulf-front market before supply normalizes; it's among the only opportunities in Southwest Florida to purchase a brand-new Gulf-front home right now. Price range runs $1.5M–$5M+ for Gulf-front and canal-side new builds, with the broader Fort Myers Beach market (new and renovated) running $500K–$5M+.

For Fort Myers Beach new builds specifically, contract terms, elevation requirements, flood insurance costs, and builder warranty provisions require extra scrutiny — bring your own agent before your first builder visit.

Types of homes and price ranges in Fort Myers proper

  • Waterfront & marina ($500K–$3M+): Gulf Harbour Yacht & Country Club and Cape Harbour Marina offer private docks, direct Gulf access, and resort-caliber amenities
  • Golf & country club ($250K–$1.5M): from bundled communities like Colonial Country Club to the master-planned Gateway corridor near RSW, at price points that beat comparable Naples and Bonita Springs communities
  • Single-family & McGregor corridor ($280K–$1.5M): established neighborhoods along the iconic McGregor Boulevard, with historic character and proximity to the River District

Featured communities

Gulf Harbour Yacht & Country Club sits along the Caloosahatchee River with direct Gulf access via a private 186-slip yacht basin, an 18-hole championship course, spa, tennis, and dining ($600K–$3M+). Cape Harbour is a vibrant marina village on the Cape Coral/Fort Myers border blending boating, dining, and boutique shopping, with luxury condos and townhomes lining the marina ($500K–$2.5M). Gateway is one of the most established master-planned communities, popular with families and frequent fliers given its proximity to RSW ($350K–$900K). Colonial Country Club is a bundled golf community — membership included with ownership ($250K–$700K). The McGregor & River District corridor offers historic character along the royal-palm-lined boulevard planted by Thomas Edison, walkable to restaurants and galleries ($300K–$1.5M). And Reflection Lakes is a popular non-golf, family-oriented community with lake and preserve views and no mandatory club fees ($330K–$650K).

New construction beyond the beach

The Corkscrew Road corridor in southeast Fort Myers and the adjacent Estero/Lee County boundary is one of the most active new development zones in the region, with Lennar, Pulte, and Mattamy active across several master-planned projects ($400K–$1.4M).

Flood zones and insurance

Fort Myers spans a wide range of flood zones. Coastal areas, riverfront properties, and Fort Myers Beach carry higher FEMA risk designations (AE and VE), meaning higher flood insurance costs and more stringent elevation requirements. Inland neighborhoods and higher-elevation communities like Gateway have significantly lower exposure. Always verify flood zone and base flood elevation data before making an offer — see our flood insurance costs guide and Lee County FEMA flood map changes guide for the broader picture.

Flood zones, waterfront access rights, and the nuances of the post-Ian rebuild all affect value in ways that aren't obvious from a listing sheet.
