Pelican Preserve vs. Valencia Bonita: which 55+ community fits your life in Southwest Florida?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 22, 2026·9 min read

Which is better for 55+ buyers: Pelican Preserve in Fort Myers or Valencia Bonita in Bonita Springs?

Both are premier 55+ active adult communities in Southwest Florida, but they serve different buyers. Pelican Preserve is a larger, more established community with optional 27-hole golf and a full Town Center campus in Fort Myers, with home prices from the low $300s and a CDD fee on top of HOA dues. Valencia Bonita is a boutique newer community in Bonita Springs built by GL Homes — no CDD, higher price points averaging $754,000, and luxury finishes throughout. If golf and a sprawling social campus matter most, Pelican Preserve wins. If you want a tighter-knit community with no CDD and newer construction in Bonita Springs, Valencia Bonita is your pick — though inventory is limited since the community sold out in 2023.

If you've been researching 55+ communities in Southwest Florida, there's a good chance you've already bookmarked both of these. Pelican Preserve in Fort Myers and Valencia Bonita in Bonita Springs are two of the most sought-after active adult communities in the region — and for good reason. Both are gated, resort-style, and built for buyers who want more out of retirement than a quiet street.

But they're very different in scale, pricing, and lifestyle. One has golf; the other doesn't. One carries a CDD fee; the other doesn't. One opens at $300,000; the other starts closer to $550,000. The comparison is worth taking seriously before you book any tours.

Location: Two Very Different Southwest Florida Addresses

Pelican Preserve sits off Treeline Avenue near I-75 exit 128 in Fort Myers — close to RSW airport, the Colonial Boulevard shopping and dining corridor, and the Lee Health system. If your plan involves flying back and forth to a northern home, or if airport access matters to your family visiting, this location is hard to beat. Target, Costco, and Gateway Town Center are minutes from the gate.

Valencia Bonita is in Bonita Springs, off Bonita Beach Road near Veterans Memorial Boulevard. That address puts you closer to Coconut Point Mall, downtown Bonita Springs, and about 20 minutes from Naples. For buyers who want to be between Fort Myers and Naples without committing fully to either city, Bonita Springs threads that needle well.

Neither address is wrong. They're just different — and worth mapping against how you actually plan to live here and what medical facilities, airports, and shopping corridors matter to you.

Scale, Feel, and the Social Calendar Question

Pelican Preserve spans 2,498 homes across multiple phases and property types — single-family homes, attached villas, and coach homes ranging from compact floor plans to larger estate lots. The Town Center is the hub: a multi-building campus with a resort pool, Flip-Flops poolside restaurant, 99-seat theater, fitness center, and 12 pickleball courts. Activity directors run a packed social calendar, and clubs exist for nearly every hobby.

At that scale, Pelican Preserve functions less like a neighborhood and more like its own town. You can go weeks without leaving the gates if you don't need to. Some buyers love that; others find it overwhelming.

Valencia Bonita has 997 homes — all single-family, built by GL Homes between 2018 and 2023. The campus is more curated: 8 pickleball courts, the Zest! restaurant, yoga and culinary studios, and a resort pool. It's a premium feel without the sprawl. For buyers who want to know their neighbors by name and feel like part of a tighter community, that matters a great deal.

Amenity depth goes to Pelican Preserve. Finish quality and intimacy go to Valencia Bonita. The 99-seat theater and 12 courts at Pelican Preserve are real differentiators if you want maximum activity options. Valencia Bonita's newer amenities feel like a boutique resort rather than a community clubhouse — and the Valencia Bonita Stay & Play program ($99 for up to three nights on-site before purchasing) gives you a genuine trial run before you commit.

Golf: The Pelican Preserve Advantage

This is the clearest differentiator between the two communities, and there's no way to soften it: if golf matters, Pelican Preserve wins this outright.

Pelican Preserve has a 27-hole course managed by Heritage Golf Group, with optional membership for residents. You don't have to join — but the course is right there, walk-to-access, designed around a community built for people who want to golf in retirement.

Valencia Bonita has no golf. Residents drive to nearby public and semi-private courses — Eagle Ridge Golf Club, Shadow Wood Country Club, and Raptor Bay Golf Club are all within range, but none of them are steps from your front door.

If golf is on your list even occasionally, that walk-to-the-course access carries real weight in the comparison.

The CDD Question — What Most Buyers Don't Know

This is the one that catches buyers off guard, and it's worth slowing down here.

Pelican Preserve sits within the Gateway Services Community Development District (GSCDD). CDDs are a Florida financing mechanism used to fund infrastructure — roads, utilities, drainage, and common area improvements. When you buy a home in a CDD community, you take on that assessment with the property.

At Pelican Preserve, the CDD assessment typically adds $500–$1,200 per year to your property tax bill, depending on your lot size and location within the community. It appears as a separate line item on your Lee County property tax notice — not folded into your HOA dues. First-time buyers in CDD communities are frequently surprised when they see this on their tax bill.

CDDs can have two components: an ongoing operations and maintenance fee (permanent) and a bond repayment portion (which eventually pays off). On Pelican Preserve resale transactions, ask your agent to confirm the current CDD balance and whether any outstanding bond remains. That number affects your true cost of ownership.

Valencia Bonita has no CDD. GL Homes structured the community's infrastructure differently, and residents pay only their HOA. One fee, clearly stated, no tax-bill surprises.

Monthly Costs: Running the Real Numbers

HOA dues at Pelican Preserve range from roughly $64 to $670 per month depending on property type, phase, and optional add-ons. Attached villas carry different fee structures than single-family homes, and sub-associations within the master HOA can add another layer. Add the CDD assessment — and golf membership if you want it — and the true monthly cost picture at the high end can reach $800–$1,000+ per month in combined fees.

Valencia Bonita's HOA runs approximately $360–$450 per month — a single tier covering common area maintenance, amenities access, and landscaping standards. No CDD. No sub-association fees. What you see is what you pay.

At lower Pelican Preserve tiers (a smaller villa without golf membership), monthly fees can come in well below Valencia Bonita's all-in cost. At higher tiers with a larger single-family home and golf, Pelican Preserve exceeds it significantly. Run your specific scenario, not the community averages.

Home Prices and What You're Getting

Pelican Preserve's median price sits at roughly $400,000, with a range from the low $300s for attached villas and coach homes to $700,000+ for larger single-family homes on premium lots. There's genuine variety here, which is part of the appeal if you need budget flexibility or want to get into a 55+ amenity community at a lower entry point.

Valencia Bonita's median runs about $754,000, with homes ranging from roughly $550,000 to $1.2 million depending on floor plan, lot, and upgrades. Every home was built to GL Homes' recent construction standards — higher ceilings, open layouts, and elevated finishes as standard, not as upgrades. You're also paying a Bonita Springs location premium.

Both communities are resale-only in 2026. Valencia Bonita sold out from GL Homes in 2023; Pelican Preserve has been resale-only for years. Both markets are showing buyer-friendly conditions right now — Pelican Preserve prices are down roughly 5.9% year-over-year, and homes in both communities are averaging 89–106 days on market. That's meaningful negotiating room if you know how to use it.

One thing to verify in both communities: HOA estoppel letters — required in Florida resale transactions — can take 10 to 30 days to process. Build that into your timeline, especially if you have a hard move-in deadline. Your agent should order the estoppel early in the process, not at the end.

Which Community Is Right for You?

There's no wrong answer here. Both are excellent communities with real amenities, real social life, and real buyer demand. But they serve different buyers.

Pelican Preserve is the stronger fit if: golf is on the list — even optionally; you want a larger, more active social community with more courts, more clubs, and more scheduled events; your budget is in the $300,000–$600,000 range; and proximity to RSW airport or Lee Health facilities factors into your planning.

Valencia Bonita is the stronger fit if: you want newer construction finishes without paying new construction pricing; no CDD is a priority — you want one clean monthly fee without tax-bill surprises; a more intimate 997-home community feels right for your lifestyle; and your budget is $550,000 and above with Bonita Springs location working in your favor.

Either way, use Valencia Bonita's Stay & Play program before you commit. Spend three nights on-site. Have dinner at Zest!, play a morning of pickleball, talk to the people walking their dogs at 7 a.m. You'll know within 48 hours whether the scale and energy match what you're looking for. Do the same at Pelican Preserve — visit on a weekday morning when the Town Center is fully active.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Pelican Preserve have a CDD fee?

Yes. Pelican Preserve is within the Gateway Services Community Development District (GSCDD). The CDD assessment appears as a separate line item on your annual Lee County property tax bill, typically ranging from $500 to $1,200 per year depending on lot size and location within the community. Unlike HOA dues, the CDD fee is paid through your property taxes — not directly to the HOA — which is why it catches buyers off guard. Always confirm the current CDD balance before making an offer on any Pelican Preserve resale.

Is Valencia Bonita sold out?

Valencia Bonita is fully sold out from the developer, GL Homes. The community completed construction in 2023. All available homes are now resale, meaning you purchase from an existing homeowner through the standard FAR-BAR As-Is Residential Contract process. As of fall 2026, the median days on market runs around 106 days — buyers have reasonable time to evaluate options and negotiate without feeling rushed.

Which 55+ community is cheaper to live in — Pelican Preserve or Valencia Bonita?

Pelican Preserve has lower entry-level home prices (from the low $300s versus $550,000+ for Valencia Bonita), but the total monthly cost picture is more nuanced. Pelican Preserve adds a CDD fee to your property tax bill and carries a wide HOA range of $64–$670/month depending on property type. Valencia Bonita has no CDD and a single HOA tier of $360–$450/month. At comparable lifestyle configurations, the gap narrows significantly. Run your specific scenario — not community averages — to find your real number.

Can you play golf at Pelican Preserve without joining the golf club?

The Pelican Preserve golf course is managed by Heritage Golf Group and primarily serves residents and members. Golf membership is optional — you can live in the community without joining. The course is not open to the general public, and non-resident access is limited. If golf is central to your retirement lifestyle, factor in the annual membership cost when building out your monthly expense comparison.

What is the Stay & Play program at Valencia Bonita?

Valencia Bonita's Stay & Play program allows prospective buyers to stay on-site for up to three nights for $99 — giving you a real feel for community life before making any commitment. You can use all the amenities, meet current residents, and get a ground-level sense of daily life in the community. It's one of the most useful pre-purchase tools available when comparing 55+ communities in Southwest Florida, and something Pelican Preserve doesn't offer in the same format.
