Moving to Naples, FL: the 2026 complete relocation guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 1, 2026·8 min read

Naples is one of the most desirable — and most misunderstood — real estate markets in the country. Home prices range from the low $300s in inland golf communities to $40 million on the waterfront, the cost of living is actually competitive with major metros despite its luxury reputation, and the timing of your purchase can mean thousands of dollars in negotiating leverage. Before you move, you need a clear picture of the neighborhoods, the true cost of ownership, and how this market actually works in 2026.

Naples gets a reputation as a playground for the ultra-wealthy, and yes, Port Royal's waterfront estates regularly trade at $10M to $40M or more. But that picture misses a lot. Buyers are moving to Naples from New York, California, Illinois, and the Pacific Northwest every week, and many of them are genuinely surprised by what they find: world-class beaches, a thriving arts and restaurant scene, no Florida state income tax, and neighborhoods that range from $350,000 condos with golf views all the way to the most expensive zip codes in the Southeast.

If you're thinking about relocating here, here's what you actually need to know before you make a decision, or make an offer.

Naples is not one market, it's many

The single biggest mistake relocators make is treating Naples as a single price point. It isn't. The neighborhoods are distinct, and the lifestyle, and budget, required for each one is different:

Old Naples sits between downtown 5th Avenue South and the Gulf. Median prices hover around $1.1M, and beachfront "Millionaire's Row" properties go considerably higher. This is the walkable, historic core of the city with boutique shops, galleries, the iconic Naples Pier, and some of the best restaurants in Southwest Florida within a few blocks.

Pelican Bay is the marquee address for buyers who want luxury amenities plus private beach access. Prices run $1M to over $26M, and the community includes two private beach clubs, tram service, tennis facilities, and a shopping corridor at Waterside Shops.

Park Shore offers elegant condos and single-family options with access to the Village Shops on Venetian Bay. Waterfront properties here command a premium, but there are options across a wide price spectrum.

The Moorings draws buyers who want a quieter waterfront atmosphere — median prices around $1.8M, with a yacht club, private beach club access, and select condos in the $350K-$800K range that represent some of the best value for Naples beach proximity.

Coquina Sands sits just north of Old Naples with a more residential feel — quieter streets, Lowdermilk Park beach access, and a mix of estates and villas in the multi-million dollar range.

Indigo Lakes is one of the best entry points for buyers with a budget from $500K-$650K, located in North Naples with a median around $503,000.

Year-round residents vs. seasonal buyers

Year-round residents tend to prioritize hurricane resilience and practical infrastructure — properties with good utility efficiency, impact windows, and a generator connection are worth weighing heavily.

Seasonal buyers (typically December-April occupancy) care more about rental restriction policies, condo association rules on usage periods, and proximity to the social scene — 5th Avenue South, the Philharmonic, the galleries, the golf clubs. Some Naples communities prohibit rentals entirely. Others allow them with restrictions. Still others operate as vacation rental-friendly buildings. Know which category you're shopping in before you fall in love with a property, especially if generating seasonal rental income is part of the plan.

Is Naples right for you, or is the area right but the city wrong?

Naples commands a premium for its brand, its beaches, and its zip codes. But it's worth considering whether the lifestyle you're seeking might be found at a lower price point just to the north.

Bonita Springs and Estero — just 20-30 minutes up US-41 — offer access to the same Gulf beaches, similar resort-style communities, and the same Southwest Florida lifestyle at price points that often run 20-40% below comparable Naples properties. Many of the buyers we work with tour both markets before deciding.
