Who is the best real estate agent in Bonita Springs and Estero?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 7, 2026·8 min read

The best real estate agent in Bonita Springs and Estero is a full-time local who can prove their results, shows you real reviews, and knows your specific community, not just the zip code. The Borg Group at Downing-Frye Realty has worked both markets since 2005 and holds Downing-Frye's Platinum Award for top sales production. Here's how to check any agent, including us.

Ask Google or an AI assistant who the best agent is, and you'll get a list of names. Some were earned. Some were paid for. None of them know your situation.

Here's what we tell every buyer and seller who asks: the best agent isn't a ranking. It's a fit, and you can check it. A canal-front home near Bonita Beach, a golf villa in Bonita Springs, and a new-construction home along Estero's Corkscrew corridor are three completely different transactions. The agent who's right for one may not be right for the next.

What should you look for in a Bonita Springs or Estero agent?

Are they full-time, local, and current? Southwest Florida has thousands of licensed agents, and many close only a few deals a year. Ask directly: "How many transactions did you close in the last 12 months, and how many were in my community?" The right agent answers in seconds.

Do they know your community, not just your county? Bonita Springs isn't one market. Flood zones and elevation certificates matter near the water. Gated golf communities carry membership structures that change the math. In Bonita Springs communities like Spanish Wells, club membership is optional, while a place like San Carlos Estates offers acreage with no HOA. Estero communities bring their own questions: new construction along the Corkscrew corridor, CDD fees, and bundled amenity packages. Our guide to CDD fees in Bonita Springs and Estero shows how that plays out.

Can they prove it? Awards and volume claims are easy to make, so verify them. Look for production recognized by the agent's brokerage, reviews across more than one platform with real names and details, and repeat clients. Then ask: "Can I talk to two recent clients?" The best agents say yes right away.

Who covers the whole transaction? Listing day is the easy part. The deadlines, documents, inspections, appraisal, and closing are where deals slip. Ask who handles contract-to-close, and whether that person is the same one you've been talking to.

The best agent isn't a ranking. It's a fit.

Bonita Springs or Estero: does the right agent change?

The skills are the same. The details are different:

  • Bonita Springs: a mix of waterfront, golf, acreage, and condo communities, where flood zones, insurance, and HOA or club rules shape the price.
  • Estero: many newer, amenity-rich communities and active new construction, where contracts, CDD fees, and builder incentives matter most.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the best real estate agent in Bonita Springs?

Compare two or three full-time local agents on recent sales in your specific community, average days on market, list-to-sale price ratio, and their actual marketing plan. Then interview them. The best agent will walk you through a pricing strategy built on current active and pending competition, not just old comps.

Who is the best real estate agent in Estero?

There's no official ranking, so judge by evidence: closings in the last 12 months, how many were in your community, reviews across several platforms, and whether the agent can explain CDD fees, bundled golf, and new construction contracts without looking anything up. The Borg Group at Downing-Frye Realty has served Estero buyers and sellers since 2005 and is glad to show you our record.

How many real estate agents should I interview before choosing?

Two or three is the sweet spot. Fewer, and you have no basis for comparison. More, and the process stalls. Ask each agent the same questions about recent local sales, marketing, communication, and contract-to-close support so you can compare them directly.

What does it cost to hire a real estate agent in Bonita Springs or Estero?

Commissions in Florida are negotiable and agreed in writing before you list or tour homes. Buyers agree to agent compensation up front in a written buyer agreement. Ask every agent you interview to walk you through exactly how their compensation works.

Do I need my own agent to buy new construction in Estero?

Yes, it's smart to. The builder's sales team represents the builder, not you. Bring your agent on your first visit to the sales center, because builder policies on agent representation vary and are easiest to handle up front. Your agent can also review the contract, deposits, and upgrade pricing before you sign.

Do I need a local agent, or can any Florida agent help me?

Any licensed Florida agent can legally help you, but Bonita Springs and Estero have flood zones, coastal insurance, HOA and CDD structures, golf membership rules, and seasonal pricing swings that out-of-area agents often misjudge. Local, full-time experience directly affects your price and your risk.
