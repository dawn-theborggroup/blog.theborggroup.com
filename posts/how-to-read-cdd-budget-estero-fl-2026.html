How to read a CDD budget before you buy in Estero FL: 2026 buyer's guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 8, 2026·10 min read

How do you read a CDD budget before you buy a home in Estero, FL?

Find the Community Development District named on the home's tax bill, open its adopted budget for the current fiscal year (it starts October 1 and must be posted on the district's website), and read three things: the per-unit assessment table for your home type, the debt service schedule that shows when the bond is paid off, and the general fund that shows how the maintenance number is being held. At Copper Oaks in Estero, for example, the FY2027 adopted budget shows $1,352.67 a year for a single-family home — $416.67 for operations and $936.00 for bonds that mature May 1, 2035.

The tax bill tells you what a CDD charged. The adopted budget tells you what the number is made of, how long the biggest piece lasts, and what the board just decided. It's public, it's usually a short PDF, and most buyers never open it.

November is a good month to learn how. Lee County tax bills go out by November 1, the CDD's new fiscal year started October 1, and the 4% early-payment discount runs through the end of the month — so the number on a listing sheet right now may already be a year stale.

We've covered what a CDD fee is before. This guide is the next step: reading the district's own numbers before you offer.

Why this matters: the listing number isn't the budget

A CDD is a unit of local government under Chapter 190 of the Florida Statutes. Its board adopts a budget every year, and that budget sets two different assessments that land together as one line on your tax bill:

  • Operations and maintenance (O&M) — lakes, landscaping, pumps, insurance, management, and whatever else the district owns. The board resets it every year.
  • Debt service — repayment of the bonds that built the roads, drainage, and utilities. It follows a fixed schedule and ends when the bonds are paid.

The title company will find the CDD. The risk is pricing the home on one blended number without learning which half is temporary, which half can rise, or what the board is working on next.

Step-by-step: how to find and read the budget

1. Confirm the district by name

Start with the most recent tax bill for the property, not the listing. The Lee County Tax Collector's guide to reading a bill notes that the levying authorities are listed in the center of the bill with their phone numbers and rates, and that rate questions go to the levying authority directly. If a CDD is levying, it will be named. Lee County's special districts directory links to each CDD's website.

The district name often isn't the community name. The Brooks in Estero, for example, is served by two districts that still carry "Bonita Springs" in their names because they were created in 1998 and 1999.

2. Open the current adopted budget

Florida law does the work for you here. Section 189.069 requires every special district's website to post its budget and any amendments, its most recent final audit, and each meeting agenda at least seven days before the meeting. Section 190.008 requires the district manager to prepare a proposed budget by June 15 and the board to adopt the final budget before October 1, after a public hearing advertised in the newspaper.

So for a purchase today, you want the FY2027 adopted budget (October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027). If a district's site only shows a proposed budget in November, that's worth a question.

3. Find your home type in the per-unit table

Near the front of most CDD budgets is a table that divides the assessment by product type. This is the single most useful page in the document. Here's what Copper Oaks' FY2027 adopted budget shows:

  • Single-family (129 units): $416.67 O&M + $936.00 debt = $1,352.67 a year
  • Multi-family (163 units): $416.67 O&M + $898.00 debt = $1,314.67 a year
  • Both totals are unchanged from FY2026.

Read the year-over-year column — a flat number and a rising one look the same on a listing sheet.

4. Know which number is gross and which is net

The per-unit figures are gross — what's billed to you. The district then budgets to receive less. Copper Oaks deducts 6% for "Discounts & Collections" on both assessments, because tax-roll payers can take Florida's early-payment discount (4% in November, 3% in December, 2% in January, and 1% in February under section 197.162) and the collection process has costs. Pay in November and your cost runs a little under the gross figure.

5. Find the bond and its last payment date

The debt service section names the bond series and usually includes an amortization schedule. This is where you learn when the debt half of your CDD line ends.

Copper Oaks' debt is its Series 2021 Special Assessment Refunding Bonds — refunding means the district refinanced older bonds — at a 3.000% coupon. The schedule shows $1,976,000 outstanding after the May 1, 2026 payment and a final principal payment of $247,000 on May 1, 2035.

That date is the most useful fact in the document: $936.00 of a single-family owner's $1,352.67 runs on a known schedule through 2035, and the $416.67 O&M piece continues after that.

While you're there, check that the budget lines up with the schedule. In Copper Oaks' document, the FY2027 debt lines match the schedule's 2027–28 rows rather than its 2026–27 rows — a $180 difference on a $253,460 line that changes no one's bill, but exactly the kind of thing worth a question.

6. Read the general fund for how the O&M number is being held

The general fund is the operating budget. Look at three lines: total expenditures, net assessments, and carry-forward surplus.

At Copper Oaks, FY2027 spending is budgeted at $145,622 while net O&M assessments bring in $114,368. A $27,754 carry-forward surplus and $3,500 of interest close the gap. That's how the per-home O&M stayed flat. It isn't a red flag on its own — the district's own projection shows FY2026 coming in under budget, which rebuilt that surplus — but it's the line that tells you whether next year's number can stay flat.

7. Check the calendar against your closing date

Because the proposed budget is due by June 15 and adoption happens before October 1, a summer closing means next year's number has usually already been proposed. A buyer closing in August can be the one who pays a new number in November. The proposed budget and the hearing notice are both public — check them if your closing falls in that window.

8. Skim the last few agendas and minutes

The budget shows what's decided; the agendas show what's coming. Lowndes, an Orlando law firm, made the same point in July 2026 guidance on CDD due diligence: confirm the remaining bond principal, whether prepayment is available, and whether new bonds, refundings, or supplemental assessments are under consideration.

Florida-specific nuances

  • The statutory disclosure covers the first sale. Section 190.048 requires a boldface CDD disclosure, placed just above the buyer's signature, in each contract for the initial sale of a parcel or unit — the builder contract. It says the assessments are set annually by the board and are in addition to county taxes. On a resale, the budget is how you see what that disclosure only describes. Our guide to new construction contracts in Bonita Springs and Estero covers the builder side.
  • CDD and HOA are separate. The HOA estoppel letter covers association dues; it won't tell you about the district. Copper Oaks' budget notes that the HOA holds the lake and landscape contracts and the CDD reimburses the HOA for its share each quarter — so the two budgets are tied together, and you want to read both before you assume who pays for what.
  • Prepayment balances come from the district. Many districts allow an owner to prepay the remaining capital (debt) assessment. The balance for a specific parcel comes from the district manager, not the tax bill. The Brooks' districts, for example, tell owners to call with the district name and parcel ID.

Red flags to watch for

  • A listing that shows an HOA fee and no CDD line, when the tax bill names a district.
  • No adopted budget posted for the current fiscal year after October 1.
  • A flat O&M assessment held up by a shrinking carry-forward year after year.
  • Agenda items about new bonds, supplemental assessments, or large capital projects.
  • A final maturity date far beyond your expected ownership period that you hadn't priced in.

How a buyer's agent protects you

What would we look at first if you called us about a home in a CDD?

The maturity date. Before anything else, we want to know how much of the CDD line is debt and when that debt ends, because it changes how two otherwise similar homes compare. A home whose bonds retire in 2035 and one whose bonds run into the 2050s can carry the same tax bill today and very different costs over the time you'll own them.

Next, we look at whether the maintenance number is being held flat by savings or by a budget that actually balances, and whether anything on recent agendas could add a new assessment. Then we line all of it up next to the HOA dues, the insurance quote, and the tax bill, so you're comparing total monthly cost — not list price.

The outcome: before you offer, you know what you're agreeing to pay, which part ends and when, and whether anything pending should be addressed in the contract. If something doesn't add up, we track down the answer in writing. That's the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find a CDD's budget in Lee County?

On the district's own website. Florida law (section 189.069) requires every special district to post its budget and amendments, and Lee County's special districts page links to each CDD's site. The district's name appears among the levying authorities on the property's tax bill.

When does a CDD adopt its budget?

Under section 190.008, the district manager prepares a proposed budget by June 15, and the board adopts the final budget before October 1 after an advertised public hearing. The fiscal year runs October 1 through September 30.

How do I know when the CDD debt portion ends?

The adopted budget's debt service section and amortization schedule show the final payment date. At Copper Oaks in Estero, the Series 2021 refunding bonds make their last payment on May 1, 2035.

Is the CDD amount on my tax bill the same as the budget's per-unit number?

The per-unit number in the budget is the gross assessment that's billed. Paying early under Florida's discount schedule (4% in November down to 1% in February) lowers what you actually pay, which is why districts budget to collect a few percent less than they bill.

Can I pay off a CDD bond assessment early?

Many districts allow prepayment of the remaining capital assessment. The payoff balance comes from the district manager, not the tax collector — ask for it in writing with the parcel ID.

The bottom line

A CDD line on a tax bill is two different costs with two different futures, and the adopted budget is where you can see both. Find the district, open the current budget, read the per-unit table, the maturity date, and the general fund — and do it before you make an offer, not after you close.
