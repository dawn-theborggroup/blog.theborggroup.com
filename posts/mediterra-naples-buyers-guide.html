Mediterra Naples FL: what buyers need to know before making an offer in 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 24, 2026·10 min read

Mediterra sits on 1,697 acres of rolling North Naples land two miles north of Immokalee Road, hidden behind a Livingston Road gate that most drivers pass without noticing. Fewer than 950 homes are threaded across 31 neighborhoods, connected by nearly eight miles of walking and biking trails and anchored by two Tom Fazio championship golf courses. It's one of only a handful of Naples inland golf communities with its own private Gulf beach club — a 10,000-square-foot facility on Little Hickory Island that alone would justify a membership fee in most markets.

The community's fee structure changed on August 1, 2026. Golf initiation jumped from $250,000 to $350,000. Sports & Beach moved from $125,000 to $175,000. Lifestyle went from $62,500 to $87,500. Buyers touring Mediterra right now are pricing into a different community than the one that existed a year ago, and the resale market has not fully adjusted. If you're considering an offer here in 2026, the questions you ask before you walk into the sales gallery or write your first number matter more than the floor plan you fall in love with. Here's what you need to know.

The community: Bonita Bay Group roots, London Bay Homes present

Mediterra was master-planned in the late 1990s and opened for sales in 1999. The South Course debuted in 2000; the North Course followed in 2002. Both were designed by Tom Fazio, and Mediterra remains one of the few Naples-area communities to hold two of his courses on a single property. London Bay Homes is the current on-site builder and continues to deliver new estates in the Lucarno and Terrazza neighborhoods, with recent showcase residences starting in the mid-$3 millions.

The address of record for the club is 15836 Savona Way, Naples, FL 34110. The gated entrance is on Livingston Road, and the property line straddles the Collier-Lee county boundary, with most residences and the primary amenities on the Collier side. RSW airport sits roughly 25 minutes north; Mercato and Waterside Shops are within a short drive south; Vanderbilt Beach and Wiggins Pass State Park are about 15 minutes west.

Neighborhoods within Mediterra carry Italian names — Cabreo, Lucarno, Monterosso, Calabria, Verona, Porta Vecchio, Lagos, Terrazza — and each has its own architectural character, price point, and HOA sub-assessment. Some are coach-home enclaves; others are custom-estate villages with lot premiums in the seven figures. That distinction matters when you're shopping, because the neighborhood you buy in determines both your monthly carry and what a resale buyer will pay you five years from now.

Homes and pricing: $995,000 coach home to $13.25M estate

Mediterra offers a wider price spread than most luxury Naples communities. Current active listings run from approximately $995,000 to $5.2 million, but the true ceiling is higher — 2025's top recorded sale hit $13.25 million.

The amenities

The Club (approximately 60,000 sq. ft.). The main clubhouse hosts formal and casual dining, event space, and a recently added 1,850-square-foot outdoor covered bar terrace overlooking the golf course. The Board approved a $38 million Sports & Lifestyle Center reinvestment in February 2025, so the club is not standing still.

Private Gulf Beach Club (10,000 sq. ft.) on Little Hickory Island. Recently remodeled. Elevated pool with expansive sundeck, cabana service, indoor and al fresco dining, a bar with televisions, and a wine program that includes 20 wines at $20 per bottle. This is the amenity that separates Mediterra from most inland Naples golf communities — it also means Mediterra owners are driving up to Bonita Beach for their beach day rather than fighting Vanderbilt Beach traffic.

Sports Club. Fitness center, three spa treatment rooms, wellness programs, personal trainers, and a resort pool with a smoothie bar.

Tennis. Eight Har-Tru courts including stadium and coliseum courts, a dedicated pro shop, and an award-winning program.

Pickleball. Seven courts — a serious commitment for a golf-first community.

Other. Bocce courts with a beverage bar, nearly eight miles of trails connecting themed parks, and a full-time activities director running social clubs and events.

Who Mediterra fits best: active buyers who want golf and beach together in one membership, who value trail walking and social clubs, and who intend to be in Naples enough months of the year to use the amenities they're paying for. It fits less well for buyers who plan to be here six weeks a year and would rather trade a smaller fee footprint for a simpler lifestyle.

The buying process: what to do before you visit the sales gallery

This is the section where most Mediterra buyers make expensive mistakes. Read it carefully.

1. Understand the August 2026 membership reset

The fee schedule changed. Golf initiation is now $350,000, up from $250,000. Sports & Beach is $175,000, up from $125,000. Lifestyle is $87,500, up from $62,500. Any Mediterra material printed before August 2026 quotes obsolete numbers. Confirm every fee in writing on the day you write your offer.

2. Confirm membership category availability

As of 2026, Golf and Limited Golf memberships are at capacity. Multiple industry reports put the Full Golf waitlist at approximately eight years. This is not a rounding error. If you're buying Mediterra specifically for golf and you don't already have a written transferable-membership commitment from your seller, you're buying the beach and social lifestyle for the next eight years — which is fine if you know it going in. The workaround is buying a resale from an exiting member whose membership transfers to the new buyer at closing. Verify transferability in writing before you sign.

3. Ask for a full carrying-cost worksheet

Mediterra's all-in monthly carry includes: master HOA (approximately $9,060/year, or $755/month), your neighborhood sub-HOA, your club dues (Golf runs approximately $25,700/year, Sports & Beach and Limited Golf around $10,300, Lifestyle around $4,000), Mediterra CDD assessments on your tax bill, food-and-beverage minimums, cart fees, guest fees, and capital assessments when the club funds a project like the $38M Sports & Lifestyle Center. Ask for last year's actuals from the seller. Ask what capital assessments are approved or contemplated — dues are scheduled to increase 4.75% in 2027.

4. Pull the HOA estoppel and club membership packet early

A Florida HOA estoppel letter is the binding document that tells you exactly what you owe at closing — current assessments, transfer fees, capital contributions, and any special assessments. Order it as soon as you go under contract. Order the club membership packet at the same time; it defines what you inherit from the seller and what you're re-paying to the club.

5. Order a flood elevation certificate and get four-point and wind mitigation inspections

Most of Mediterra sits in FEMA Zone X, which does not require federal flood insurance, but individual parcels vary and lenders often require the certificate anyway. A four-point inspection and a wind mitigation inspection determine your homeowner's insurance premium — and on a house at Mediterra's price point, a good wind mitigation report can save $3,000-$8,000 a year.

6. Choose independent representation, not the on-site sales rep

London Bay's on-site sales team represents London Bay. Resale listing agents represent the seller. Whether you're buying new construction or resale, you need an agent whose duty is to you — someone who knows how to read a Mediterra contract, negotiate builder incentives, evaluate the transferable-membership picture, and walk you through the doc-stamp math ($0.70 per $100 of sale price, paid by the seller). Do this before you walk into the sales gallery, not after. Builders and listing agents both track who represented you first.

7. Time your offer around the seasonal calendar

March and April are peak snowbird selling months in Naples — inventory rises, price cuts follow, and by June the softest listings often have the sharpest concessions. Days on market for Naples luxury in 2026 has averaged well over 100 days, and Collier County months-of-supply is above five in the segments above $2M. This is a market where a patient buyer wins.

How Mediterra compares: two nearby reference points

Mediterra vs. Bonita Bay

Bonita Bay is larger (2,800 homes on 2,400 acres), older-established, and offers five golf courses with a much longer golf waitlist and a private marina and beach pavilion. Bonita Bay's social membership is mandatory (~$90K initiation, ~$12,930 per year); golf is a separate optional membership. Mediterra is smaller, more architecturally cohesive, has half the golf courses but tighter member caps, and its beach club is inland-focused rather than dock-focused. If you want boating, Bonita Bay wins. If you want intimate scale and a two-course Fazio experience, Mediterra wins.

Mediterra vs. Quail West

Quail West is smaller still (660 homes on 1,180 acres, mostly Collier), with an equity-based golf club structure (Full Golf ~$250K initiation plus ~$24,940/yr, currently full with a waitlist), a larger 100,000-square-foot clubhouse, and no private beach club. Quail West's 2026 average sale is running around $4.4M — comparable to Mediterra. The differentiator is Mediterra's beach club, which Quail West cannot match, versus Quail West's tennis, pickleball, and clubhouse-forward social program. Both communities cap membership; both have waitlists; both are best-in-class within their pricing tier.

Bottom line

Mediterra in 2026 is a two-Fazio-course, private-beach-club community that just repriced its membership ladder in a way most competing clubs have not. The reset makes new membership more expensive but preserves the resale value of transferable memberships held by existing owners — which quietly rewards resale buyers who choose the right listing. If you're buying Mediterra for the golf, verify the waitlist and the transferability language before you fall in love with a house. If you're buying it for the beach club and the Naples location, you're looking at one of the most complete luxury lifestyle packages north of Immokalee Road, and the current buyer's market gives you room to negotiate.

The single biggest mistake we see out-of-state buyers make at Mediterra is walking into the London Bay sales gallery without independent representation and without pulling the club fee schedule and HOA estoppel first. Every dollar of that mistake is on the buyer's side of the closing statement.
