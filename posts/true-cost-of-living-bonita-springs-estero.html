Moving to Bonita Springs or Estero, FL: the 2026 true cost of living guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·July 25, 2026·10 min read

Living in Bonita Springs or Estero typically costs a homeowner $4,500-$9,000 per month depending on community, home size, and lifestyle — and the purchase price is only part of the equation. New residents routinely face a first-year property tax surprise of $3,000-$8,000 more than they budgeted, plus homeowners insurance averaging $4,000-$9,000 per year in Lee County, CDD fees of $1,000-$5,000 annually in many new-construction communities, and HOA fees ranging from $150 to $1,200 per month. The upside: no Florida state income tax, no tax on Social Security or pension income, and the homestead exemption — once you know how to claim it — saves most permanent residents $1,200-$2,000 per year on property taxes.

Most people spend months comparing home prices before moving to Southwest Florida. Then they close, and the first property tax bill arrives. Or they discover the community they fell in love with carries a $3,400/year CDD fee that wasn't in the listing description. Or their homeowners insurance renewal comes in 40% higher than the quote they got before closing.

We've walked hundreds of out-of-state buyers through this market, and the surprises are almost always the same ones. This guide is designed to give you the complete financial picture before you buy, not after.

Why Southwest Florida still makes financial sense

Florida has no state income tax. For retirees, that means Social Security, pension income, and 401(k) distributions aren't taxed at the state level. If you're moving from Ohio, Michigan, Illinois, or New York, that alone can offset a meaningful portion of your annual housing costs.

Where to buy: community price tiers

At $500,000-$800,000: The Place at Corkscrew in Estero is a Lennar/Pulte community known for its resort pool, the Barefoot Bar & Grill, and social lifestyle. Wildcat Run in Estero offers Arnold Palmer golf with no waitlist — unusual in this market — and private club membership starting around $500,000.

At $700,000+: WildBlue in Estero is built around a 225-acre recreational lake with real boating and lakefront living. Vivid Shores in Bonita Springs is one of the newest Pulte communities, with lakefront lots from the $750s. Kingston in Estero is a master-planned community spanning 7,000 acres, still early in development, with significant long-term potential.

For buyers who want acreage: San Carlos Estates in Bonita Springs has no HOA and no CDD, AG-2 zoning, and acreage lots starting around $700,000. It's one of the few places in this market where you can have space and privacy without asking permission from a homeowners association.

Most of the "surprises" are fully manageable once you know what to ask and when to ask it. Our job is to make sure none of this catches you off guard.

Property taxes: the sticker shock is real

Florida's property tax system has a feature most don't learn about until it's too late. When a property sells, the assessed value resets to the purchase price. The previous owner may have had years of Save Our Homes protection, which capped annual assessment increases at 3%. That protection disappears the moment you buy.

So you purchase a home for $650,000. The previous owner's assessed value was $380,000, protected for a decade under Save Our Homes. Your first tax bill will be based on $650,000, not $380,000. In Lee County, the effective all-in property tax rate runs approximately 10-12 mills. On a $650,000 home, that's roughly $6,500-$7,800 per year before the homestead exemption.

The Florida homestead exemption reduces your assessed value by $50,000 for tax purposes, saving most homeowners $1,200-$2,000/year. But here's what trips people up: you must establish Florida homestead by January 1 to receive the exemption that tax year. Close in February, miss the March 1 filing deadline, and you pay full assessed value for the entire first year.

If you're buying new construction, be aware that your first year's tax bill may be based on land value only, then jump sharply in year two once the structure is assessed. File for homestead exemption as soon as you close and establish primary residence at the Lee County Property Appraiser's office (leepa.org). Don't wait.

CDD fees: which communities have them, and what you pay

A Community Development District (CDD) is a special taxing district that finances a planned community's infrastructure. The bonds are repaid through annual CDD fees on your property tax bill. These are separate from HOA fees and non-negotiable.

Flood zone designation and insurance requirements are determined by the specific parcel, not just the community — the same street can have multiple flood zone designations. Waterfront lots along canals or lakes carry different risk profiles than interior lots even within the same gate.

Homeowners insurance in Lee County has stabilized somewhat in 2026 but remains elevated compared to non-coastal states — plan for $4,000-$9,000/year depending on home value, roof age, construction type, and flood zone. Homes with newer roofs, impact-resistant windows or shutters, and hip rooflines qualify for the best wind mitigation credits.

Healthcare and lifestyle in Southwest Florida

Southwest Florida has seen significant healthcare infrastructure investment. NCH Healthcare System operates hospitals in Naples and Bonita Springs. Lee Health operates multiple facilities across Fort Myers and Estero. Physicians Regional and Gulf Coast Medical Center serve the broader Lee/Collier corridor.

The lifestyle draw is genuine: 35+ miles of Gulf beach within 20-30 minutes of most Estero and Bonita Springs communities, year-round golf on 90+ courses in the metro area, deep-water fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, and a dining and retail scene anchored by Coconut Point, Mercato, and Miromar Outlets, all within a short drive of most target communities.

What to expect buying from out of state

Most out-of-state buyers do their early research online — Zillow, builder websites, Google — and then fly down for a two- or three-day discovery trip. The mistake is scheduling builder sales center appointments without talking to an independent agent first. Builder representatives work for the builder. They'll answer your questions honestly, but they're not going to compare their community to the one next door that might suit you better.

For resale homes, the typical timeline in Bonita Springs and Estero right now runs 30-45 days from executed contract to closing. Title companies handle closings in Florida; no attorneys required for residential transactions. Remote closings via overnight mail or mobile notary are common. For new construction, inventory homes can close in 60-90 days; pre-construction contracts typically run 9-18 months.

Common relocation mistakes to avoid

Budgeting for purchase price only. Add 1.5%-2.5% of purchase price for closing costs, then layer in the annual cost picture — HOA + CDD + insurance + property taxes — before deciding what you can comfortably afford.

Skipping the homestead exemption filing. File the day you establish residency. You cannot recover a year of full assessed taxes after the fact.
