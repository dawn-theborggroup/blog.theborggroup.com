Relocation

Moving to Naples, FL: the 2026 complete school attendance zone guide for families

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 31, 2026·10 min read

If you're relocating to Naples with school-age children, you've probably already typed a listing address into a real estate portal and read the school names it printed back at you. That's a starting point, not an answer — and in Collier County it's a starting point with a moving part that catches relocating families off guard.

The short version: Collier County Public Schools assigns students by geographic attendance boundary tied to the home address, and you can look up any address with the district's Interactive Zoning Tool. But the district states plainly that "all school attendance areas are evaluated on an annual basis," and in December 2025 the School Board approved a new boundary that moved part of the Estates Elementary area to a school that had not opened yet. The zone comes with the house. It does not belong to the house.

In Collier County, the address decides — and the district decides the address

Collier runs on geographic attendance boundaries. Your home address sits inside an elementary zone, a middle zone, and a high school zone, and the district publishes printable boundary maps for all three levels plus an Interactive Zoning Tool at zones.collierschools.com/live/ that returns the assigned schools for a specific address.

This is genuinely different from how it works one county north. In Lee County — Estero, Fort Myers, Bonita Springs — assignment runs through proximity zones, a Choice Application, and a lottery, which we covered in detail in our Estero school enrollment guide for families. If you're comparing the two counties, that difference alone may shape where you look.

In Collier, the district's own language is direct: "All enrollees new to the District must attend the zoned school or apply as described below." If you're moving in from out of state, your zoned school is your default, full stop.

That sounds simpler. But it introduces a different risk, and it's the one most families miss.

The boundary is reviewed every year — and it has already moved

In December 2025, the Collier County School Board approved and established the attendance boundary for Ave Maria Elementary, the district's 33rd elementary school. Under that action, as reported locally at the time, students living east of a line drawn along Oil Well Grade Road and Oil Well Road were rezoned from Estates Elementary to Ave Maria Elementary for the 2026-2027 school year.

Ave Maria Elementary then opened on August 11, 2026, with roughly 400 students in its inaugural year.

Sit with that sequence, because it's the point. A family could have bought a home in eastern Collier in 2024, confirmed the zoned elementary school on the district's own tool, enrolled their child, and then — through no change of address at all — had that child reassigned to a campus that did not exist when they signed the contract.

The district also listed proposed boundary maps for Pinecrest, Naples Park, and Pelican Marsh Elementary in the same cycle, and referenced middle and high school modifications alongside it. An earlier cycle, effective 2025-2026, created a boundary for Bear Creek Elementary and adjusted several schools west of I-75.

Two consecutive years, two boundary actions — the normal operating rhythm of a county whose population keeps pushing east.

The zoned school is a real, verifiable fact about a property on the day you check it — and it's a fact the district can revise. Treat it the way you'd treat a tax bill or an insurance quote: accurate as of a date, worth re-checking, and not something a listing page is responsible for keeping current.

The exits exist, but they run on a calendar

If your zoned school isn't where you want your child, Collier gives you routes out. Each one has conditions worth knowing before you pick a closing date.

  • Out of Zone — for parents or guardians with documented needs or hardships as listed in Board Policy 5120.
  • School Choice — open to parents exercising choice, in the district's words, "without respect to or limitations upon their reasons."
  • Controlled open enrollment — under Florida Statute 1002.31, a district must allow a parent to enroll a child in any public school in the district that has not reached capacity, must update capacity determinations every 12 weeks and post them on its website, must maintain a wait list for students denied on capacity, and must accept students through the year as capacity opens up.

Now the conditions, all from the district's own Parental Choice page:

  • The window is dated. The Parental Choice window for the 2027-2028 school year runs January 6, 2027 through February 14, 2027. The 2026-2027 priority window is closed.
  • Transportation is not provided by the District for choice placements. That's consistent with the statute, which says a district may provide transportation — not that it must.
  • Approvals are subject to capacity and class-size restrictions. A route that exists is not a route that's guaranteed.
  • Pick three. Families are strongly encouraged to select three schools, and the district cautions that "no expectations should be made for acceptance into feeder pattern schools."
  • Changing levels means a new application. Moving from elementary to middle, or middle to high, does not carry your placement with you.
  • Notifications go out through the summer, which means a family closing in June may not know the outcome before the first day.

One useful wrinkle for relocating families: applications generally must arrive by June 30 of an open window, but first-time district enrollees are exempt from that deadline. If you're new to Collier, you're not locked out by a date that has already passed — though placement still depends on space.

When a boundary change does happen, the district has applied some protections. In the Ave Maria action, students already attending a school through approved parental-choice applications were allowed to remain, and bus transportation was provided for rezoned students unless the home was within two miles of the assigned school with a walking route deemed safe. Separately, under School Board Policy 5120.02, rising 11th and 12th graders who are rezoned may remain at their existing school — but the District will not provide transportation.

Why Southwest Florida, and why Naples for a family move

Florida levies no state income tax on wages, retirement distributions, pension income, or capital gains, which for a household relocating from New York, New Jersey, Illinois, or Michigan can be the single largest line item in the whole decision. Add warm winters, Gulf access, and a short drive to Southwest Florida International Airport, and the appeal isn't mysterious.

Naples specifically gives a relocating family a wide price gradient in one market. Per the September 2026 NABOR report, the overall median closed price in the Naples area was $575,000, with single-family at $747,500 and condos at $400,000. Inventory sat at 4,240 listings, down 17.3% year over year, with months of supply at 5.5 — down from 7.8 — and a median 99 days on market.

Still more negotiating room than families had in 2021 or 2022, but tightening as season approaches.

For where to actually look, our Naples relocation guide walks the neighborhood-by-neighborhood price gradient, and our Naples buyer’s guide covers what buyers need to know market-wide. Communities worth a look for families comparing price against commute include Naples Reserve, Compass Landing, Lely Resort, and Isles of Collier Preserve. Each sits in a different zone set, which is exactly why the zone check happens per address and not per community name.

What it actually costs to own here

Three Collier-specific costs deserve a line in your budget before you fall in love with a floor plan.

Insurance, and the honest state of the numbers. Published figures for Collier County disagree, and you should know that rather than be handed one. An Insurify estimate reported by WINK News put the county's average annual premium at $10,052, up 25.3%, ranking Collier second nationally for estimated growth in the first half of 2026. The Insurance Information Institute, working from Florida regulatory data, put the countywide average at $5,534 as of March 31, 2026, about 2% higher than the prior period. Asked about the 25.3% figure, the Institute's Mark Friedlander said, "I can't validate that," and added, "We are seeing rates flattening, premiums actually decreasing on average."

Those are different methods over different periods, not a contradiction to resolve by picking the one you prefer. The number that matters is the quote on the specific house, and that's the one to get in writing before you waive a contingency. Our guides to homeowners insurance in Southwest Florida and four-point and wind mitigation inspections cover what moves a premium up or down.

Property taxes and homestead. Your tax bill does not inherit the seller's. Save Our Homes resets at sale, so budgeting from the current owner's bill is how families end up short. The homestead and residency calendars are their own subject — see our posts on the Florida homestead exemption and establishing Florida residency and domicile, plus our Naples guide for Northeast and Midwest buyers, which walks the timing in detail.

HOA and CDD. Many newer Collier communities carry both — an HOA bill and, separately, a Community Development District assessment that arrives on your property tax bill rather than from the association. They're two different obligations with two different billing paths, and the combined figure varies widely by community.

What to expect as an out-of-state buyer, and the mistakes we see

In Florida, a title company closes your transaction rather than an attorney. The seller pays doc stamps at $0.70 per $100 of sale price. You'll receive a Seller's Property Disclosure, and in a community with an association you'll likely encounter HOA approval and an HOA estoppel letter before closing.

Collier's registration requirements are specific, and worth assembling early. The district asks for the registering parent's photo ID, an official sealed birth certificate or passport, a physical completed in the U.S. within 12 months of the first day of school, immunizations on Florida Form DH 680, previous school records, and an IEP or 504 plan if applicable. For proof of address, you'll need one document from a first list — homestead exemption card, property tax notice, home purchase contract, domicile filing, or current lease — plus two from a second list: a Florida driver's license or ID, your last two auto insurance statements, or your last two utility bills. New residents submit two months of bills within 30 days. The district recommends finishing online enrollment by July 15 so a new student is ready for day one.

The mistakes we see most often with relocating families:

  1. Trusting the school names on a listing page. Portals cache. Boundaries move. Check the address on the district's own tool.
  2. Checking the zone once and never again. Re-verify between contract and closing, especially in eastern Collier.
  3. Closing in June and assuming a choice outcome will land before August. Notifications go out across the summer.
  4. Assuming a sibling or a feeder pattern carries placement forward. The district says not to expect it, and changing levels requires a new application.
  5. Budgeting insurance and taxes from the seller's numbers instead of quoting the specific house.

What would we look at first if you called us about a Naples address?

Three things, in this order. First, the zone set for that exact address — elementary, middle, and high — pulled from the district's own tool rather than the listing, and dated. Second, where that address sits relative to the growth pushing east, because a property near the edge of a boundary in a fast-building corridor carries more revision risk than one in a built-out area west of I-75. Third, the calendar: how your target closing date lines up against the Parental Choice window, the district's enrollment recommendation, and the residency dates that affect your tax picture.

What that gets you is not a promise about a school. Nobody can honestly offer that — the district reviews boundaries annually and it's the district's call. What it gets you is a decision made with the actual mechanism in view: which addresses carry more revision risk than others, which routes remain open to you as a first-time district enrollee, and which closing dates quietly cost you an option.

We've been doing this in Southwest Florida since 2005, and the families who land well are almost always the ones who understood the sequence before they chose the house, rather than after. That's the part we take care of for you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out which school an address in Naples is zoned to?
Use the Collier County Public Schools Interactive Zoning Tool at zones.collierschools.com/live/, which returns the assigned school for a specific address, and cross-check against the district's printable elementary, middle, and high school boundary maps. Don't rely on the school names shown on a real estate listing — those are often cached and aren't maintained by the district.

Can Collier County change my child's school after I buy the house?
Yes. The district states that all school attendance areas are evaluated on an annual basis, and the School Board approved a boundary change in December 2025 that rezoned part of the Estates Elementary area to Ave Maria Elementary for 2026-2027. Rezoned students must attend their newly assigned schools, though the district has applied protections in past actions — including allowing students already attending through approved parental-choice applications to remain.

When is the Collier County school choice application window?
The Parental Choice window for the 2027-2028 school year runs January 6, 2027 through February 14, 2027. The 2026-2027 priority window is closed. Applications generally must arrive by June 30 of an open window, but families enrolling in the district for the first time are exempt from that deadline.

Does Collier County provide busing if my child attends a school outside our zone?
No. The district states that transportation is not provided for Parental Choice and out-of-zone placements. Under Florida Statute 1002.31 a district may provide transportation for controlled open enrollment students but isn't required to. Rising 11th and 12th graders who are rezoned may remain at their existing school under Policy 5120.02, also without district transportation.

What documents do I need to enroll a child new to Collier County?
The registering parent's photo ID, an official sealed birth certificate or passport, a U.S. physical completed within 12 months of the first school day, immunizations on Florida Form DH 680, previous school records, and an IEP or 504 plan if applicable. For address, one document from the district's first list — such as a home purchase contract or current lease — plus two from the second list, such as a Florida driver's license and two recent utility bills.
