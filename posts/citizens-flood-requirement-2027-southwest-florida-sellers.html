Will you lose your Citizens policy on January 1, 2027? Southwest Florida 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 22, 2026·8 min read

Do Southwest Florida homeowners need flood insurance to keep a Citizens policy in 2027?

Most do. Citizens Property Insurance has been phasing in a flood insurance requirement since 2023, and the last step lands on January 1, 2027, when it applies to all policies regardless of dwelling value. Condominium unit-owner policies, tenant content policies, and policies that exclude windstorm or hail coverage are exempt. Everyone else has to produce proof of flood coverage to renew — and the date that controls you is your renewal date, not January 1.

If you are thinking about selling in Bonita Springs, Estero, Naples, Fort Myers, or Cape Coral in the next year, that renewal date probably sits somewhere inside your listing window. This is the part almost nobody is planning for.

The market you're selling into

Southwest Florida is still a patient market, and patience is what makes an insurance problem expensive.

  • Lee County: a median 59 days on market, with sellers netting about 93.2% of the original asking price (Momentum, October 20, 2026).
  • Estero, 33928: median sale price $503,750 over the trailing twelve months, down 2.6% year over year, 66 median days on market, 92.9% of list, with 412 active listings against 130 pending.
  • Bonita Springs: a median 75 days on market and about 91.0% of ask (Momentum, October 12, 2026).
  • Collier County excluding Marco Island, August 2026: median closed price of $575,000 across all property types, down 1.7%, with 5.4 months of supply and an average 99 days on market.
  • Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed mortgage at 7.40% in its October 15, 2026 survey.

Do the arithmetic on a two-to-three month marketing period plus a 30-to-45 day closing and you are looking at four or five months between the day you list and the day you hand over the keys. A policy renewal inside that window is not a footnote. It is a live risk to the transaction.

The insurance picture itself has improved, modestly and unevenly. The Office of Insurance Regulation has approved reductions covering more than 62,000 policies, 48 companies have filed for decreases since January 2024, and more than 20 new insurers have entered the state. Citizens cut 2026 homeowners multiperil rates by an average of 8.8% statewide and wind-only rates by 5.5%. But on figures the Insurance Information Institute attributes to state data, the average annual homeowners premium still runs about $3,576 in Lee County, down 1.5%, and about $5,344 in Collier County, up 0.2%. We wrote more about what that does and doesn't do for sellers in our look at whether falling insurance is finally helping Southwest Florida sellers.

What the January 1 deadline actually asks of you

The requirement comes from legislation the Florida Legislature passed in December 2022, and Citizens publishes the schedule on its own flood page. Homes inside a special flood hazard area have had to carry flood coverage since 2023. For everyone outside the flood zone, the thresholds stepped down by dwelling value — Coverage A on your declarations page:

  • Effective 01.01.24 — policies with a dwelling value of $600,000 or more
  • Effective 01.01.25 — $500,000 or more
  • Effective 01.01.26 — $400,000 or more
  • Effective 01.01.27 — all policies, regardless of value

Three things follow from that, and they are the three things we end up explaining on the phone.

It is aimed at homes that have never needed flood insurance. Roughly 91% of sampled Estero properties sit in Zone X. Being out of the flood zone has been the reason not to buy a flood policy for years. From January 1, 2027, if you are a Citizens dwelling policyholder with wind coverage, it stops being a reason.

How much coverage is not your choice. Citizens asks for flood coverage equal to or greater than your Citizens dwelling value. Where the NFIP cannot write that much, Citizens says it will accept the maximum you are eligible for — $250,000 of Coverage A under the regular program, $35,000 under the emergency program. Citizens does not sell flood insurance itself, so this is a second policy with a second carrier.

Whether it applies to you turns on your policy form, not your building. This is where Southwest Florida gets genuinely confusing. Citizens' bulletins name the exempt forms specifically — the unit-owners forms CIT HO-6, HW-6, CIT DP-1C, and CIT DP-3C. Plenty of coach homes, carriage homes, and attached villas here look like condominiums and are insured on a dwelling form, and plenty of free-standing homes are legally condominium units. The exemption follows the paperwork. Pull your declarations page and read the form number before you assume anything.

Proof, per Citizens, means a copy of the flood policy declarations, or a submitted application plus proof of payment while the policy is pending, together with a completed Policyholder Affirmation Regarding Flood Insurance, form CIT FW01.

Two dates decide whether this becomes a problem

Work backward from your renewal, because two outside clocks are running and neither one cares about your closing date.

The 30-day wait. A new NFIP policy generally does not take effect for 30 days. There is a well-known exception that makes a flood policy effective at closing when it is purchased in connection with making, increasing, extending, or renewing a loan — which is why buyers can bind flood at the closing table. A current owner buying flood coverage to satisfy an insurance requirement is not in a loan transaction. The ordinary 30-day wait applies to you. Buying the policy the week before renewal does not get you a policy in force at renewal.

Citizens' nonrenewal calendar. Citizens' agent alert describes courtesy letters going out about 145 days before a policy's expiration date, nonrenewal notices mailed 130 days before expiration, and reminder letters at 60 days. Submit the flood information before the nonrenewal generates and the policy can simply be amended. Let it generate and you are filing a reinstatement request instead. For a policy renewing in, say, March 2027, the nonrenewal notice is a late-October 2026 event. That is now.

There is a third clock worth knowing about, because it is the one nobody controls. The NFIP's authorization has been running on short-term extensions — 36 of them since the end of fiscal 2017 — and the current one runs to midnight on December 12, 2026. During the lapse that accompanied the 2025 shutdown, no new federal flood policies were sold or renewed while the authority was down. We are not predicting a lapse, and private flood carriers are a real option. We are saying that treating a federal program's calendar as something you can lean on in the final fortnight of December is a bad plan when your own insurance renewal depends on it.

Four mistakes we see sellers make here

  1. Assuming "I'm not in a flood zone" settles it. It settles the lender question. It does not settle the Citizens question, and after January 1, 2027 it settles nothing at all.
  2. Reading the mail as junk. A courtesy letter 145 days out looks like marketing. It is the only warning that arrives before the nonrenewal notice.
  3. Waiting until the home is under contract. A buyer's lender will want evidence of insurability, and a wind policy in nonrenewal status mid-contract is the kind of problem that re-opens price. Addendum H lets a buyer walk over insurance costs, and a seller who cannot demonstrate a clean policy is negotiating from behind.
  4. Cancelling the flood policy on the way out the door. An NFIP policy can be assigned to the buyer at closing, and the discount and glide-path rate travel with the property. We covered the mechanics in our guide to transferring flood insurance to your buyer. The coverage you were required to buy is an asset at the closing table — if nobody cancels it first.

How we market a listing around this

Our approach is to treat insurance as part of the pricing file rather than a disclosure we get to at the end. Before a home goes live we want the declarations pages, the roof documentation, the wind mitigation and four-point reports, and any flood policy and elevation certificate assembled in one place. A buyer's agent who can hand a lender a complete insurance picture on day one removes a reason to discount. In this market, where homes are closing at 91% to 93% of ask across Lee and Collier, that is worth more than another price reduction.

Frequently asked questions

How much flood coverage does Citizens require?
Flood coverage equal to or greater than the dwelling value, Coverage A, on your Citizens policy. Where the NFIP cannot write that much, Citizens says it will accept the maximum you are eligible for — $250,000 of Coverage A under the regular program, $35,000 under the emergency program.

My home in Estero is not in a flood zone. Does this still apply?
Yes, if you are a Citizens dwelling policyholder with wind coverage. The phase-in was written for homes outside a special flood hazard area, and roughly 91% of sampled Estero properties are in Zone X. Being out of the flood zone is the situation the January 1, 2027 date addresses.

What happens if I do not provide proof?
Citizens nonrenews the policy, with the notice mailed about 130 days before expiration. Get the flood information in before the nonrenewal generates and the policy is amended instead; after it generates, you are filing a reinstatement request.

Can I buy the flood policy the week before renewal?
Usually not. A new NFIP policy generally carries a 30-day waiting period. The exception that makes a policy effective at closing applies to loan transactions, and a current owner buying coverage to satisfy an insurance requirement is not in one.

What will the flood policy cost me?
It depends on the specific property — elevation, construction, and the coverage amount Citizens requires. We do not publish an average, because the averages floating around for Southwest Florida come from sources that disagree with each other. A quote on your address is the only number worth acting on, and how flood insurance costs shape pricing explains why that number matters to your list price.

Does any of this change my closing costs?
Not directly. Doc stamps at $0.70 per $100 of sale price, title, and prorations work the same way regardless; our breakdown of seller closing costs in Lee and Collier counties walks through the line items. What changes is whether the deal closes on the terms you agreed to.

Where to start

Find your Citizens declarations page and look at two things: the form number and the expiration date. If the form is a dwelling form and the expiration date falls in 2027, you have a deadline — and if you are planning to sell, you have two overlapping calendars to reconcile rather than one.

That reconciliation is a 20-minute conversation, and it is much cheaper to have it now than in week six of a listing.
