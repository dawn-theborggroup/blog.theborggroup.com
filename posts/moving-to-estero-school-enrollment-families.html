Moving to Estero, FL: the 2026 complete school enrollment guide for families

By Mark & Dawn Borg·October 24, 2026·10 min read

Does buying a house in Estero, FL guarantee your child a seat at the nearest school?

No. In Estero and the rest of Lee County, your home address does not assign your child to the closest public school. Lee County uses proximity zones and a Choice Application with a lottery, so you rank a short list of schools your address makes you eligible for and seats are awarded by lottery and capacity. Twenty minutes south in Collier County, assignment works the opposite way — by geographic attendance boundary tied to your address. If you are relocating to Southwest Florida with school-age children, that single difference between two neighboring counties should shape which house you buy and when you close.

Most families relocating to Estero from out of state arrive with one assumption baked in: find the neighborhood, buy the house, and your children attend the school down the road. That is how it works in most of the country. It is not how it works in Lee County.

We want to be clear about what this guide does and does not do. We do not rate, rank, or compare schools — that is not a Realtor's job, and under Fair Housing guidelines it is not a conversation we will have. What we can do, and what nobody seems to explain to relocating buyers until after they have closed, is tell you exactly how the enrollment system works, where your address matters, where it does not, and which deadlines will cost you if you miss them.

Why Southwest Florida, and why Estero

Estero is a planned village in southern Lee County, sitting between Naples about 20 minutes south and Fort Myers about 15 minutes north, with Southwest Florida International Airport less than five miles away. Florida Gulf Coast University sits on its eastern edge.

The draw is familiar: no state income tax, a year-round outdoor climate, and a cost of ownership that still compares favorably to the Northeast and Midwest even after Florida's insurance increases. For families specifically, Estero adds something the beach towns do not — space, newer construction, and a village government actively building recreation. Estero's adopted fiscal 2026-27 budget runs $90.8 million and carries $75.9 million in capital projects, including $43.6 million for parks and recreation. That figure covers $26.9 million for Phase 2 of Estero Sports Park plus improvements at Estero RiverPark and the Happehatchee Center, with $69 million in bonds planned for parks work.

There is also the Bonita Estero Rail Trail. Estero committed $19 million toward acquiring an 11.4-mile inactive rail corridor alongside Bonita Springs and Collier County, against a $60 million total purchase price, with Bonita Springs voters approving a $35 million general-obligation bond. Closing was targeted for this fall. Be realistic about the timeline: after closing, Seminole Gulf Railway has up to two years to remove the tracks, and construction is expected to take another 12 to 24 months. The finished trail will run from northern Collier County through downtown Bonita Springs, past the Estero entertainment complex and Estero RiverPark, ending at Estero Parkway. It is a real amenity. It is not a 2027 amenity.

The market is cooperating with buyers right now. Over the twelve months to October 20, 2026, the Estero 33928 market recorded a median sale price of $503,750, down 2.6% year over year, with a median 66 days on market and homes selling at 92.9% of asking price. There were 412 active listings against 130 pending. Across Lee County, the median was 59 days on market at 93.2% of original asking price. With Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed at 7.40% in its October 15, 2026 survey, financed buyers are negotiating harder than they could two years ago.

How Lee County actually assigns students

This is the part worth reading twice.

Lee County ran a countywide School Choice system for roughly seventeen years, a structure that traced back to the 1964 Rosalind Blalock desegregation case. The district reached unitary status in 1999, with a supervisory period running through the 2005-06 school year. Under that system, families ranked an average of twelve schools and the district guaranteed busing regardless of distance.

In February 2023, with enrollment having grown from about 65,000 students in 2005-06 to more than 100,000, the School Board unanimously approved the Proximity Plan. District officials said close to 3,000 students were arriving late to school each day. The plan cut the number of elementary schools a family can choose from to four, drawn from the proximity options tied to their address, and activated district transportation only within those proximity zones.

Here is what that means in practice for you:

  • Incoming kindergarteners and students new to the district go into the lottery automatically. You rank the schools your address makes you eligible for, and assignment comes out of a lottery.
  • A rising 1st–5th grader can stay at a current school outside the new zone, but the family waives district transportation unless that school falls inside the new boundary.
  • Sibling and Proximity are named preference categories. The district's own guidance is that a school where you hold a preference should be ranked number one on the application. For 2026-27, siblings must be linked in the Focus parent portal by matching parent names and addresses.
  • Middle and high school work on a different geography. The district is divided into three Residential/Choice Zones — West, East, and South — each split into three sub-zones (W1–W3, E1–E3, S1–S3). Families select from schools in their own sub-zone and contiguous sub-zones within the same zone.

The busing rule at the high school level is the one that catches relocating parents hardest. Transportation to a given high school is provided only for specified sub-zones. In the district's own words, “Students living outside of the listed Subzones will not have busing provided.” Your child can still attend — you just drive. Lee County also demonstrated this year that bus service can be withdrawn: Island Coast High School was removed as an option for zone E1 for 2026-27 because of a busing problem tied to low student count, while remaining available to W3.

Two homes a few streets apart, at the same price, in the same village, can produce different school option sets and different transportation outcomes. That is not a detail to discover after closing.

The dates that decide the outcome

  • Choice Applications appear at the start of Student Open Enrollment, at the end of January. This is the window that matters most. Close in November and you are applying on schedule. Close in June and you are working the waitlist.
  • If you do not get your number-one ranked school, you are automatically waitlisted for it. No second application is required.
  • Waitlist Phase 1 runs immediately through August 28, with daily placements. Phase 2 places students once at the end of Quarter 1, for Quarter 2. Phase 3 places once at the end of Quarter 2, for Quarter 3 and second semester.
  • Career waivers for incoming 9th and 10th graders stay open until May 1. Hardship and Cross Zone waivers run through the Focus parent portal.
  • For 2026-27 the district also eliminated second-semester transfer requests and dropped the requirement for advanced and magnet program exit waivers.

Verify your specific address with Student Enrollment at (239) 337-8247 before you write an offer. The district main line is (239) 334-1102. Zone maps and rules change year to year, and the only answer that counts is the one tied to your parcel in the current plan.

The capacity question in east Estero

If you are looking at the Corkscrew Road corridor — and most families shopping new construction in Estero are — there is a supply issue you should price in.

The east Corkscrew area passed 10,000 homes by the end of June 2026. In late April 2026, Collier County approved Corkscrew Grove, roughly 9,000 more homes near SR-82. According to Engage Estero's October 2026 infrastructure update, nearby elementary, middle, and high schools are at or near capacity. The Kingston developer identified a site for a K-8 school, but the district's ten-year plan does not include one there. True North Classical Academy, a K-12 charter planned for 3,100 students, is projected to open in August 2028, starting with grades K-2.

In a choice-and-lottery system, capacity is the binding constraint. More homes plus flat seat counts means a tighter lottery. That is the honest read.

Roads are catching up unevenly too. Phase II of the Corkscrew Road widening, from Ben Hill Griffin Parkway to Alico Road, is projected to finish in December 2026, and the multi-use path, street lights, and landscaping were expected to complete in fall 2026. Beyond Alico Road, the draft MPO 2050 plan carries no funding for widening over the next ten years.

None of this makes east Estero a bad buy. New construction in Estero is where the newest product and the best builder incentives are. It means the school and commute questions deserve the same scrutiny you would give a roof.

Neighborhoods and communities

Estero's gated communities span a wide range, and the right one for a family usually comes down to price, CDD exposure, and where the house sits relative to Corkscrew Road traffic.

Verdana Village is known for its indoor sports complex and pickleball courts. The Place at Corkscrew carries a resort pool and the Barefoot Bar & Grill. Corkscrew Shores is built around a 240-acre lake with kayaking and a lakefront pavilion, and carries no CDD. Bella Terra spans roughly 1,000 acres and is resale-only with entry pricing well below the newer communities. Kingston and Stoneybrook round out the eastern corridor.

What it actually costs

Three separate taxing authorities appear on an Estero property tax bill, and for fiscal 2026-27 they are:

  • Lee County School District — 5.271 mills. Adopted September 8, 2026, alongside a $3,055,366,436 budget.
  • Lee County countywide — 3.7623 mills. Adopted at 0.89% over the rollback rate.
  • Village of Estero — 0.73 mills. Adopted below the 0.7336 rolled-back rate.

Note the order. The school district is by far the largest single line on your bill — larger than the county and the village combined. It is also the one thing your address does not buy you a seat at. That is not a complaint about the district; it is the structural fact that makes the enrollment process worth your attention rather than your assumption.

Then add the costs that are specific to your parcel:

  • CDD assessments, where they apply, land on the tax bill as a separate line. FY2027 figures on file for Estero districts: V-Dana (Verdana Village) $1,414.90–$3,337.07; Corkscrew Farms (The Place) $1,185.41–$1,717.32; Habitat (Bella Terra) $1,040.06–$1,279.22; Stoneybrook $808.28–$1,514.11; WildBlue $2,851.13–$5,411.60; Cypress Shadows, serving Preserve at Corkscrew, $2,633.94–$3,447.33 tentative. Corkscrew Shores and Bella Terra carry no CDD debt assessment. Our CDD fees guide explains the mechanism.
  • HOA dues, which in Estero range from a few hundred dollars a month in lower-amenity communities to well over a thousand in full-resort communities.
  • Insurance. The average annual homeowners premium in Lee County ran about $3,576 as of March 2026, down 1.5% year over year, per Florida OIR data compiled by the Insurance Information Institute. Collier County averaged about $5,344 — a real argument for the Lee County side of the line. Citizens is down to 336,000 policies from a 1.41 million peak in October 2023, and its 2026 homeowners multiperil rates came in 8.8% lower statewide. Most of Estero sits in FEMA Zone X, and Lee County's CRS Class 6 rating earns a 20% NFIP premium discount where flood coverage applies. NFIP is authorized through midnight on December 12, 2026.

What to expect as an out-of-state buyer

Florida's process will feel unfamiliar in a few specific ways.

A title company closes your transaction rather than an attorney. The seller pays doc stamps at $0.70 per $100 of the sale price. You will receive a Seller's Property Disclosure, and in a gated community you will also face HOA approval and an HOA estoppel letter before closing. Many communities and most insurers want current four-point and wind mitigation inspections.

The item that costs families real money is the homestead exemption timeline. Per the Lee County Property Appraiser, you must have legal title or a beneficial interest in the property as of January 1, and the property must be your permanent residence as of January 1. Applications are due by March 1 of the year you qualify. The exemption reduces the first $25,000 of assessed value, with an additional exemption of up to $26,411, adjusted annually for inflation.

Read that additional exemption carefully: it applies only to non-school taxes. School millage is your biggest line item and your additional homestead exemption does not touch it. Our guides to the Florida homestead exemption and establishing Florida residency and domicile go deeper.

Closing on December 20 versus January 5 can move your first full tax year. For a relocating family, the enrollment calendar and the homestead calendar both argue for the same thing: close before year end if you reasonably can.

Mistakes families make relocating to Estero

Assuming the house comes with the school. It does not, in Lee County. Buyers coming from Collier County listings make this mistake in reverse and are surprised that Estero does not work like Naples.

Closing in June and expecting a seat in August. Open enrollment opens at the end of January. A summer closing usually means the waitlist, and Phase 1 placements run only through August 28.

Reading a competitor's blog post from 2023 as current. Zone maps, bus sub-zones, and waiver rules changed for 2026-27. Several of the pages that rank for this question describe the 2023-24 plan.

Buying in east Estero without asking about capacity. Ten thousand homes went in, and nearby schools are at or near capacity with no new K-8 in the district's ten-year plan.

Treating the school millage as a fixed cost and the CDD as a detail. It is the reverse. The millage is the same across Estero; the CDD assessment varies by thousands of dollars between communities a mile apart.

Frequently asked questions

Does my Estero address guarantee my child a seat at Pinewoods Elementary, Three Oaks Middle, or Estero High?

No. The Village of Estero lists Pinewoods Elementary (K-5), Three Oaks Middle School (6-8), and Estero High School (9-12) as the public schools serving the area, but Lee County assigns students through proximity zones and a Choice Application with a lottery rather than by address-based attendance boundary. Confirm what your specific parcel is eligible for with Lee County Student Enrollment at (239) 337-8247.

When does Lee County school choice open for the next school year?

Choice Applications become available at the start of Student Open Enrollment, at the end of January. If you do not receive your number-one ranked school you are automatically waitlisted, with Phase 1 placements running daily through August 28, Phase 2 at the end of Quarter 1, and Phase 3 at the end of Quarter 2.

Is school assignment different in Naples than in Estero?

Yes, and significantly. Collier County Public Schools assigns students by geographic attendance zone tied to the home address, with an interactive zoning tool for address lookups, and students new to the district attend their zoned school unless they obtain out-of-zone or school choice approval. Estero is in Lee County, which uses proximity zones, a Choice Application, and a lottery instead.

Will my child get a bus in Estero?

It depends on the address and the school. Elementary transportation is activated only within your proximity zone, and at the high school level busing is provided only to students living in the sub-zones a given school serves — the district states that students outside those sub-zones will not have busing provided, though they may still attend. Lee County also removed Island Coast High School as an option for zone E1 for 2026-27 over a busing issue, which shows these assignments do change.

Should we close before the end of the year?

If your timeline allows it, there are two good reasons to. You must hold title and occupy the home as your permanent residence as of January 1 to qualify for that year's homestead exemption, with applications due by March 1. And closing before the end-of-January open enrollment window puts you in the regular Choice Application cycle rather than on the waitlist.
