Bonita National, Bonita Springs FL: golf-deeded or social membership in 2026?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 23, 2026·9 min read

Bonita National Golf & Country Club in Bonita Springs bundles golf membership directly into the deed on roughly 866 of its 1,469 homes, while about 593 homes are social-deeded and can never be upgraded to golf. Golf-deeded owners pay about $9,789 a year in combined HOA and golf assessments plus lawn care, versus roughly $3,948 less per year for social-deeded owners — and that decision is locked in at the deed, not something you can change after closing. If you golf even occasionally, the math almost always favors golf-deeded. If you don't, buying social-deeded saves real money every single year.

Bonita National is one of the more misunderstood communities in Bonita Springs, mostly because its fee structure doesn't work like the mandatory-club model buyers expect from older country clubs. There's no six-figure initiation fee here. Instead, the golf membership is baked into specific homes at the deed level, and Lennar — the builder — has been fully sold out for several years, so every purchase here today is a resale. Here's what that actually means for your offer.

Community overview

Bonita National sits on Bonita Beach Road, about 5.5 miles east of I-75 in Bonita Springs, zip code 34135. Lennar acquired the 657.59-acre site in September 2013 for $45 million, broke ground in January 2014, opened the golf course in January 2015, and recorded the final plat in October 2019. The community is built out: 1,459 of the 1,469 planned homes are complete, and Lennar's allocation has fully sold. Every transaction here now is a resale — there's no builder sales center or model row to visit anymore.

The land breaks down as 98.36 acres of golf course, 122.1 acres of lakes and flowways, 162.12 acres of preserve buffering the CREW Bird Rookery Swamp, and the remainder split between single-family and multi-family residential. The community is gated with 24/7 guard attendance and has no age restriction, though its mix of condos, coach homes, and estate-size single-family homes draws both full-time residents and seasonal buyers.

Homes and pricing

Six distinct home collections span a wide price range: Terrace condos (270 units, roughly $320K–$600K, 1,194–1,408 sq ft), Veranda condos (252 units, roughly $450K–$750K, 1,400–1,900 sq ft), Coach Homes (280 attached-condo units, roughly $600K–$950K, 1,741–2,110 sq ft), Executive single-family homes (383 units, roughly $650K–$1.1M, 1,649–2,246 sq ft), Manor single-family homes (157 units, roughly $1M–$1.5M, 2,245–3,828 sq ft), and Estate single-family homes (127 units, roughly $1.3M–$1.7M+, 2,394–3,800 sq ft).

Over the trailing 12 months, 98 homes closed at a median price of $750,000, ranging from $320,000 to $1.66 million. The median time to contract ran 62 days, and homes are closing at 96.9% of list price on average — a market that rewards a well-priced offer but isn't desperate.

The single biggest factor in what you'll pay isn't the floor plan — it's the deed type. A golf-deeded Executive home and a social-deeded Executive home with the same square footage can carry meaningfully different resale values, because the buyer pool for golf-deeded inventory is narrower and more committed.

Amenities and lifestyle

The golf course is the centerpiece: an 18-hole, par-72 Gordon Lewis championship design stretching 6,988 yards from the Black tees, with a 73.6 rating and 133 slope. It runs along the CREW Bird Rookery Swamp preserve, and 17 of its 18 greens are protected by water — a layout that rewards accuracy over distance. Troon Privé manages day-to-day club operations.

Beyond golf, the amenity campus includes a 25,000-square-foot clubhouse with three dining venues, an 8,500-square-foot sports facility with a fitness center and personal training, a resort-style main pool plus satellite pools, the U-Topia Spa, 8 Har-Tru tennis courts, 4 pickleball courts, 3 bocce courts, and a member travel program with a full calendar of social events.

Who this fits best: buyers who want real, playable championship golf without a legacy country club's six-figure buy-in, and who are comfortable buying resale rather than new construction. If you golf regularly, the golf-deeded homes deliver serious value per dollar of annual dues. If golf isn't part of your life, the social-deeded inventory gives you the same gated, amenity-rich lifestyle without paying for a course you won't use.

The buying process

Because Bonita National is a fully built-out, resale-only community, the buying process looks different than it would at a new-construction site still selling from a builder. There's no sales center, no builder incentives to time, and no design-center upgrade budget to plan around. What replaces those considerations is deed-type due diligence — and it matters more here than in almost any other Bonita Springs community.

Confirm the deed type before you tour, not after you fall in love with the house. Golf and social membership are appurtenant to the deed and transfer automatically at closing — you can't add golf later if you buy social, and you can't opt out of the golf assessment if you buy golf-deeded. Ask your agent to verify deed type directly against the HOA and Golf Club records, not just the MLS listing sheet.

Budget the capital contributions due at closing, separate from your down payment: roughly $4,000 in HOA capital contribution for every buyer, plus $5,000 in golf capital contribution if the home is golf-deeded, plus a $150 resale application fee — up to roughly $9,150 in one-time closing costs beyond standard closing costs.

Order HOA estoppel letters early, and expect more than one. Bonita National's governance includes a Master HOA, a separate Golf Club entity, 11 condo sub-associations, and a CDD — meaning a condo buyer may need estoppel letters from two or three separate entities. Starting this the day you go under contract avoids a scramble near closing.

If you're buying a condo, check where the building sits on the milestone-inspection timeline. Bonita National's condo buildings were constructed between 2015 and 2018, roughly two decades out from Florida's 30-year structural milestone inspection trigger — still verify the association's reserve study and SIRS compliance as part of your due diligence.

Plan your first visit around the open house schedule — Sundays from 1 to 4 p.m. only. And use your own buyer's agent from the start, even on a resale, since a community with this many overlapping governing documents benefits from someone who can read all of them before you sign. How it compares

Bonita National vs. Bonita Bay: Bonita Bay is the legacy country club model — a separate club membership with initiation fees running roughly $70,000 to $90,000 plus about $12,930 a year in dues, on top of your home purchase, in exchange for five golf courses, a private beach club, and a full-service marina. Bonita National flips that structure: golf is bundled into the deed for a flat, all-in annual cost of about $9,789 plus lawn care, with no separate initiation fee at all. If a big upfront membership buy-in is the barrier keeping you out of golf communities, Bonita National removes it. If you want five courses and a marina instead of one, Bonita Bay is still the deeper amenity package — see our Bonita Bay buyer's guide.

Bonita National vs. Verdana Village: Verdana Village is still active new construction from Lennar and Pulte, with HOA fees of $342–$393 a month and CDD fees of $1,320–$2,218 a year, and an average sold price around $611,564. Golf there is optional and structured as a separate membership you can decline entirely — there's no fixed pool of homes locked into mandatory dues the way roughly 59% of Bonita National's inventory is. If you want the flexibility to opt out of golf costs completely, or new construction with a builder warranty, Verdana Village is the better fit. If you want an established, mature golf course and clubhouse today, Bonita National delivers that now. See our Verdana Village buyer's guide.
