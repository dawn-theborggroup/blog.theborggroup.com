When is the best time to sell a home in Southwest Florida? A 2026 seasonal guide

By Mark & Dawn Borg·August 6, 2026·11 min read

The best time to sell a home in Southwest Florida is between January and April, when seasonal demand from snowbirds and affluent out-of-state buyers peaks. Homes listed in March average just 32 days on market, compared to 48 days for October listings, and early spring sellers can net up to 10% more than those who list in December. If peak season isn't an option, a pre-season listing in October-November or a well-priced summer listing can still produce strong results in today's inventory-constrained market.

The question comes up in almost every seller consultation we have: When should I list? It's the right question. In most markets, timing matters. In Southwest Florida, it matters more than almost anywhere else in the country because this market doesn't just slow down in summer — it transforms. The buyers, the competition, the urgency, and the dollars-per-square-foot calculation all shift dramatically depending on the month you put that sign in the ground.

The current state of the Southwest Florida market

Before we get to timing strategy, you need to understand the market you're entering. As of mid-2026, active listings across Southwest Florida have dropped to approximately 19,177, down from 24,688 just one year ago — a 22% reduction in available inventory. In Bonita Springs and Estero specifically, homes for sale fell 28% year-over-year, while closed sales jumped 16% and pending sales surged 37%.

The median sale price in Bonita Springs and Estero is tracking around $524,725, with an average of 65 days on market. In Collier County (Naples and the surrounding area), months of supply sits at 7.7, with homes averaging over 100 days on market. Lee County is slightly tighter at 6.8 months of supply.

What does this mean for you as a seller? The homes priced correctly are moving. The ones that aren't are sitting — and with nearly 2,000 price reductions logged in a single week across the region in late spring, there's no hiding an overpriced listing in today's market. Buyers are doing their homework. Cash buyers made up 64% of luxury transactions in the region during Q1 2026. Out-of-state buyers, primarily from New York, Illinois, and Ohio, continue to drive the top end of the market.

This market doesn't just slow down in summer. It transforms. The buyers, the competition, the urgency, and the dollars-per-square-foot can all shift dramatically depending on the month you put that sign in the ground.

A seller's season-by-season breakdown

Southwest Florida doesn't follow the national real estate calendar. Here's what each window actually looks like.

January through April: peak season

Motivated buyers who've spent winters in Bonita Springs, Naples, Estero, or Fort Myers walk into open houses, tour communities, and write offers. They're not browsing on Zillow from a couch in Cleveland — they're here, they're motivated, and roughly 40% of high-end Southwest Florida transactions involve buyers from New York, Illinois, or Ohio making decisions while they're actually in the market.

The numbers are clear. Homes listed in March average just 32 days on market, compared to 48 days for October listings — a 33% difference in sell time. Early spring sellers capture up to 10% more than comparable homes listed in December. If you're selling a home priced above $700K in a luxury community like Bonita Bay, WildBlue, or a waterfront property in Naples, this premium is magnified: cash buyers with no financing contingencies dominate your buyer pool, and they close fast.

One tactical note worth knowing: Thursday listings receive 18% more views in the first 48 hours than any other day of the week. If you're going live in peak season, coordinate your launch for a Thursday so you hit the weekend with maximum momentum.

May: the family relocation rush

May brings a different buyer, and a secondary price premium of roughly 13% above the annual average. This segment is families relocating for work, lifestyle, or retirement who need to close before August and the new school year. These buyers are on a real deadline, which means they're motivated and pre-approved. A home priced fairly and in good condition can generate fast, clean offers from buyers who've been searching for months and can't afford to wait.

June through September: the summer strategy

Here's what most sellers don't fully account for: hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, and it creates a psychological drag on showings, even in years without significant storms. Out-of-state buyers who represent a large portion of Southwest Florida's buyer pool are simply less likely to schedule a trip to tour homes when NOAA is tracking active systems. August and September are the slowest months. Foot traffic thins. Days on market stretch.

That said, the buyers who show up in summer are almost never casual — they're relocating on a specific timeline, making a second-home decision they've already committed to, or buying investment property on a schedule. A correctly priced home in a desirable community can absolutely sell in July or August. The risk is overpricing. A home that enters the market too high in August doesn't just sit — it accumulates market time that shadows you into fall. If you must sell in summer, price it right from day one and budget for longer exposure. Summer is not the season to "test the market."

October through December: pre-season positioning

This window is consistently underestimated by sellers, and consistently rewarded by the market. As October arrives, early seasonal residents start returning, and competing inventory hasn't yet reached its January peak.

Three mistakes worth avoiding

Skipping Florida-specific pre-listing preparation. Southwest Florida buyers, especially experienced out-of-state buyers with agents who know the market, will request a four-point inspection and wind mitigation report immediately. Having those documents ready, along with a current CDD fee statement, a pre-listing inspection, and a completed Seller's Property Disclosure (including the FD-2 flood disclosure now required in Florida), prevents the delays that kill deals. Florida updated the standard wind mitigation form in 2026; make sure yours reflects the current requirements.

Listing in summer with peak-season price expectations. The math doesn't work. Fewer buyers, longer days on market, and the hurricane-season psychological drag all suppress demand from June through September. Sellers who insist on February pricing in August end up carrying their listing into October, where it now competes with fresh pre-season inventory at a price the market already rejected.

Listing a vacant home without staging. Vacant homes in luxury communities take significantly longer to sell. Staged homes sell 22% faster than vacant properties in the same price range. If you've already relocated, virtual staging is better than nothing. Real, properly curated furniture and design is better still, and the cost is almost always recouped in price or speed.

Timing your sale correctly is one of the highest-leverage decisions you'll make as a Southwest Florida seller. The difference between a March listing and an August listing, in pricing power, days on market, and buyer quality, is significant. And the difference between a well-prepared October pre-season launch and a rushed January scramble can translate directly to tens of thousands of dollars.
