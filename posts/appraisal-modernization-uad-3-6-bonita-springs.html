What is appraisal modernization (UAD 3.6) and how does it affect your Bonita Springs home sale?

By Mark & Dawn Borg·September 2, 2026·6 min read

Appraisal Modernization is the mortgage industry's move to a single, data-driven appraisal report format called UAD 3.6, replacing the separate paper-style forms appraisers have used for over 20 years. Every appraisal sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac must use the new format starting November 2, 2026, which means more detailed data fields for property condition, renovations, and features like energy-efficient upgrades. For Bonita Springs sellers and buyers, this generally means a more thorough — and potentially more accurate — valuation, along with a short adjustment period as lenders and appraisers get used to the new system this fall.

If you list your home in Bonita Springs this fall, there's a good chance the appraiser who walks through it will be using paperwork that didn't exist a year ago.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have spent the better part of a decade rebuilding how appraisals get reported, and 2026 is the year it finally goes live everywhere. The industry calls it Appraisal Modernization. The technical name is UAD 3.6 — the Uniform Appraisal Dataset, version 3.6 — and every appraisal sold to the two mortgage giants has to use it starting November 2, 2026.

This isn't a rumor moving through real estate Facebook groups. It's confirmed guidance from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, and it's the single biggest change to how homes get valued in this country in over 20 years — one that touches nearly every transaction closing across Southwest Florida between now and year-end.

What's actually changing

For two decades, appraisers have filled out one of a handful of static forms depending on property type — a 1004 for a single-family home, a 1073 for a condo, and so on. UAD 3.6 replaces all of it with a single, flexible, data-driven report that adjusts automatically to whatever you're appraising, whether that's a high-rise condo on Bonita Beach or a golf-course estate in Estero.

A few things are different under the hood: Expanded data fields — appraisers now capture far more detail on condition, quality, renovations, accessory dwelling units, and energy-efficient improvements than the old checkbox-style forms allowed. Standardized definitions — "good condition" and "updated kitchen" mean the same thing nationwide, instead of varying by appraiser or region. Fully digital delivery — appraisal files now arrive as structured data packages, photos, sketches, and supporting documentation bundled together, instead of a scanned PDF.

The rollout has moved in phases: a limited testing period, then broad production starting in January 2026 (when lenders could choose either format), and now the run-up to the mandatory switch on November 2, 2026, when the legacy forms retire for good.

This is exactly the kind of shift we walk our sellers through before we even put a sign in the yard — making sure the story your home tells on paper matches the one it tells in person.

What this means for your Bonita Springs sale

Here's the part that actually affects you: the more documentation you provide, the better this new system tends to work for you — especially in a market like Southwest Florida where the transition won't be seamless. Appraisers, lenders, and title companies are all adjusting to a new system at the same time, and the transition window — broadly, this fall through the November 2 deadline — is where the learning curve shows up.

Florida's seasonal market adds another layer: appraiser demand always climbs as snowbirds and seasonal buyers return each fall, and this year that seasonal crunch lands right on top of the UAD 3.6 rollout. Practically, that can mean appraisals taking a little longer to schedule or complete, more back-and-forth if documentation is incomplete, and some lenders further along on UAD 3.6 than others. If you're under contract or planning to list before year-end, it's worth building a few extra days of cushion into your appraisal contingency and confirming with your lender where they stand on the rollout.

How to prepare if you're buying or selling this fall

  1. Ask your lender directly whether they're using UAD 3.6 yet, or still on the legacy format.
  2. Gather your documentation now — permits, upgrade receipts, and warranty paperwork — rather than scrambling once the appraisal is scheduled.
  3. Build in extra time. A few additional days in your appraisal contingency can absorb any transition-period delays.
  4. Work with an agent who's already tracking this. The way your listing gets marketed and documented should already reflect the data fields appraisers will be filling in.
