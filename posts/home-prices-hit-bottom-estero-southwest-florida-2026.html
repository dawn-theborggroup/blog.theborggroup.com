Have home prices hit bottom in Estero? Southwest Florida 2026

By Mark & Dawn Borg·November 12, 2026·7 min read

Have home prices hit bottom in Estero and Southwest Florida?

For single-family homes, the numbers say prices have stopped falling — for condos, they haven't yet. Lee County's median single-family sale price has held at or above the prior year for six straight months, from April through September 2026, while the condo median fell 8.9% year over year in September. If you're selling in Estero, which answer applies to you depends on what you own, which community it's in, and how you price it.

"Has the market bottomed?" is the question a lot of Estero homeowners are asking this fall. It's a fair question. After two years of headlines about falling prices, rising inventory, and homes sitting, you want to know whether waiting will help you or cost you.

Here's the honest answer, built from the latest closed-sale data rather than from headlines.

What the fall 2026 numbers actually show

Florida Realtors publishes monthly closed-sale data for every metro area in the state. Lee County is the Cape Coral-Fort Myers metro, and Estero sits inside it. Here's how Lee County's single-family median sale price compared with the same month a year earlier:

  • April 2026: $390,000 (0.0%)
  • May 2026: $380,000 (+1.3%)
  • June 2026: $385,000 (+1.3%)
  • July 2026: $385,000 (+2.7%)
  • August 2026: $380,000 (+2.7%)
  • September 2026: $375,500 (+0.1%)

That's six months in a row with no year-over-year decline. Year to date through September, the single-family median is $385,000 — exactly flat with 2025 — and closed sales are up 8.7%. More buyers are closing, and they're paying about what buyers paid a year ago.

Now look at townhouses and condos in the same county and the same months:

  • September 2026: median $255,000, down 8.9% from a year earlier, on 343 closed sales (+5.9%)
  • Year to date: median $280,000, down 3.4%, on 4,421 closed sales (+25.3%)

Condo buyers are back in force — sales volume is up by a quarter — but they're buying at lower prices. That's what a market still finding its floor looks like.

One more number matters. According to Florida Gulf Coast University's Regional Economic Research Institute, Lee County had 9,635 active listings in October 2026, down 12.7% from October 2025 but up 5.0% from September. Fewer homes are competing with yours than a year ago, but inventory is already building as season approaches. That figure combines single-family homes, condos, and townhomes.

For context, the statewide single-family median in September was $415,000, up 1.2%, and Collier County's was $785,000, up 1.6%. Southwest Florida isn't an outlier anymore on single-family pricing. It's moving with the rest of the state.

What does this look like in Estero specifically?

Florida Realtors doesn't publish Estero on its own, so we look at MLS-based figures published by local brokerages, and they need careful reading. As of November 9, 2026, Momentum Realty's Estero (33928) page showed a 12-month median sale price of $503,750, down 2.4% from the prior 12 months, on 1,479 sales, with a median of 66 days on market and a 93.6% sale-to-ask ratio.

The same publisher's community-level view tells a sharper story: across Estero's communities, the median one-year price change was −9.6% for the 12 months ending November 1. In other words, the citywide median dipped modestly, but the typical community saw a bigger drop. Some Estero neighborhoods are holding firm, and others are still adjusting.

Two cautions on those Estero figures. First, they're trailing 12-month numbers, so they still include last winter's softer sales and lag what's happening right now. Second, a sale-to-ask ratio around 94% means buyers are still negotiating. "Prices stopped falling" is not the same as "sellers have the upper hand."

What this means for you as an Estero seller

If you own a single-family home, the data supports a simple conclusion: waiting for prices to fall further isn't the risk it was a year ago, and waiting for a rebound isn't a plan either. Prices are flat. Your result will come from how you price against current competition, not from where the market goes next.

If you own a condo or coach home, the picture is different. Prices are still sliding, and buyers are weighing insurance, reserves, and assessments alongside the price. If you're carrying a condo you don't need, every month of waiting has a cost — and the trend hasn't turned in your favor yet. Our guide to selling a condo with a special assessment covers the questions buyers are asking right now.

For both, timing still matters. Season runs January through April, and that's when the most buyers are physically here touring. It's also when the most listings compete for them. Homes that are priced and photographed in November and December get in front of early-arriving buyers before the spring crowd of new listings shows up.

Estero also has a few local factors worth knowing:

  • New-construction competition. If your community is near Corkscrew Road, builders are still adding supply and offering incentives. We covered what that means in should you sell on Corkscrew Road before more homes arrive.
  • Flood insurance rules. Starting January 1, 2027, Citizens Property Insurance will require flood coverage on all of its homeowners policies, not only homes in high-risk zones. Buyers who'd insure through Citizens will price that in. See what the Citizens flood requirement means for sellers.
  • A new route north and south. The Three Oaks Parkway extension opened November 2, 2026, linking Radio Road in Collier to Daniels Parkway in Lee, with final completion expected in early 2027, according to WGCU. It's a useful talking point if your home sits near the corridor.

Common mistakes Estero sellers are making right now

  • Reading single-family headlines as condo news. "Prices have stabilized" is true for detached homes in Lee County. It isn't true for condos yet. Price a condo from condo sales.
  • Pricing from last winter or from 2022. Your comps should be the last 90 to 120 days of closed sales in your community, plus what's active and pending against you today.
  • Assuming your community matches the citywide number. The gap between Estero's citywide median (−2.4%) and the typical community (−9.6%) is exactly why a city average can mislead you.
  • Listing without your insurance and flood documents ready. A current wind mitigation report, four-point inspection for older homes, and your flood zone and elevation certificate (if you have one) answer the buyer's first questions before they become objections.
  • Waiting for spring to "test the market." Overpriced listings lose their best weeks of attention. Here's what overpricing really costs Southwest Florida sellers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Have Southwest Florida home prices stopped falling?

For single-family homes in Lee County, yes for now. Florida Realtors data shows the median single-family sale price at or above the prior year every month from April through September 2026, and flat year to date at $385,000. Condo and townhouse prices are still declining, down 8.9% year over year in September.

Are condo prices still dropping in Lee County?

Yes. The Lee County townhouse and condo median was $255,000 in September 2026, down 8.9% from a year earlier, and $280,000 year to date, down 3.4%. Sales volume is up about 25% this year, so buyers are active, but they're buying at lower prices.

Should I wait until spring to sell my Estero home?

Not if you're waiting for prices to rise. Single-family prices are flat, condo prices are still falling, and inventory typically builds into season. Listing in November or December puts your home in front of early-arriving seasonal buyers before the spring wave of new listings.

How many homes are for sale in Lee County right now?

Florida Gulf Coast University's Regional Economic Research Institute counted 9,635 active listings in Lee County in October 2026, including single-family homes, condos, and townhomes. That's down 12.7% from a year earlier but up 5.0% from September.

What's the median home price in Estero, FL?

MLS-based figures published by Momentum Realty showed a 12-month median sale price of $503,750 for Estero (33928) as of November 9, 2026, down 2.4% from the prior 12 months. Prices vary widely by community, so your home's value depends on its own neighborhood's recent closed sales.
