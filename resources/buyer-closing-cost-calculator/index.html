Quick Answer

When you finance a home in Lee or Collier County, expect closing costs of about 1% to 3% of the price, plus the first year of homeowners insurance, prepaid interest and escrow reserves. The Florida-specific items are documentary stamp tax on the mortgage ($0.35 per $100 of the loan) and intangible tax (0.2% of the loan). The owner's title policy is customarily paid by the seller in Lee County and by the buyer in Collier County. If you buy before the seller pays this year's property taxes, the seller credits you for the days they owned the home.

Who pays for title insurance in Lee vs. Collier County?

It comes down to local custom. In Lee County, the seller usually pays for the owner's title policy and picks the title company. In Collier County, the buyer usually pays and picks. Florida sets title insurance premiums by rule, so the policy costs the same everywhere: $5.75 per $1,000 on the first $100,000 and $5.00 per $1,000 up to $1 million. On a $550,000 home, that's $2,825, which a Collier buyer usually pays and a Lee buyer usually doesn't. Either way, if you have a mortgage, you'll pay for the lender's policy, which is far less when it's issued along with the owner's policy. Custom isn't law, so who pays can be negotiated in the contract.

What are doc stamps and intangible tax on a mortgage?

They're two Florida taxes that apply only when you borrow. Documentary stamp tax on the mortgage note is $0.35 for every $100 of the loan, and intangible tax is 0.2% of the loan. On a $440,000 loan, that's $1,540 plus $880. Cash buyers don't pay either one. (The seller customarily pays a separate doc stamp tax on the deed.)

What are prepaids and reserves?

They aren't fees. They're money paid ahead for costs you'd owe anyway. Your first year of homeowners (and flood) insurance is usually paid at or before closing. If your lender escrows taxes and insurance, it collects a few months up front to start the account. And you'll prepay interest from your closing date through the end of that month, which is why closing near the end of a month lowers your cash to close.

Why does the seller give me a tax credit?

Florida property taxes are paid in arrears. This year's bill arrives in November and is due by March. If you close before the seller pays it, the seller credits you for each day they owned the home this year, and you pay the full bill when it comes. That credit is based on the seller's bill, which may be lower than your future taxes, especially after a sale resets the assessed value. Which costs surprise buyers in Southwest Florida?

Association fees. Many gated and condo communities here charge a one-time capital contribution when a home sells, sometimes thousands of dollars, along with application fees and dues collected in advance. Insurance can also surprise buyers, especially on older homes or in flood zones. These are the costs we look for before you write an offer, so your budget matches the home.
